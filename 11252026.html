Executive summary

1

A named US bank breach leads, and the Korean wave adds a fifth savings bank. Herring Bank (Amarillo, Texas) disclosed a breach affecting 422,332 people, including SSNs and financial account and card numbers — the scale only became public with the Texas Attorney General filing on 24 November. Rakuten Securities’ second report the same day lifts its leak to up to about 16.39 million accounts, now including per-product asset balances for about 1.68 million accounts. In Korea, Hana Savings Bank confirmed an intrusion into its customer re-verification system, leaking data on 552 customers — the fifth savings bank hit since late September.

2

Three new KEV additions, seven new watchlist rows, and a second critical vCenter flaw. CISA added Optimizely CVE-2026-132224, Idunno.AntiCsrf CVE-2026-131914 and Drupal Core CVE-2026-132165 on 24 November (all due 27 November). Broadcom’s VMSA-2026-0011, also 24 November, discloses a second critical vCenter authentication bypass, CVE-2026-123903 (9.8), distinct from the exploited CVE-2026-123890, plus ESX hypervisor RCE flaws CVE-2026-123910 / CVE-2026-123911 (9.8 each) — no exploitation reported for any of the 23 flaws in that advisory. The vCenter zero-day now has a full public exploit chain: Rapid7 reproduced it end to end and its Metasploit module now contains real code. Three WHMCS billing-platform flaws (up to 9.0) are new rows.

3

The AI evidence points the same way from two directions. GreyNoise’s GT13 campaign against Check Point management servers is assessed — not confirmed — to have used LLM or agentic tooling, and the UK NCSC says it has seen a marked rise in AI-assisted attacks since September, with the agentic framework ARTEX most associated. Both agencies stress the same point: the weaknesses being exploited are not new — leaked credentials, default credentials, misconfiguration — only the speed, cost and persistence have changed. No new AI-scaled phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a named bank appeared in-window.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

No new MAS, FCA/BoE/PRA, EU or US AI-in-finance governance development appeared in-window. The control problem this window sharpens is concrete and comes from the GT13 and NCSC findings together: agentic activity is detectable by behaviour — self-labelling user agents and resource names, automated multi-host scripting, unforced operational errors — rather than IP lists (all 99 GT13 IPs were ExpressVPN exits or compromised devices). Banks running Check Point Security Management should treat the management server as crown-jewel infrastructure: patch CVE-2026-16232, restrict Trusted Clients, audit for application-token logins. The other control lessons carry: third-party AI inference supply chains can see, retain and leak prompts and credentials despite Zero Data Retention flags (OpenRouter, carried); AI provider keys are being harvested and validated at scale (Wiz — Anthropic keys from 50 hosts; ArgonHunt, carried); exposed AI gateways concentrate provider API keys (LiteLLM, carried); agent frameworks can be left publicly accessible without authentication (Cairn lesson, carried); and “agentic” labels from vendors are not evidence — ask what the agent did, with what tools, and what it could reach (Bell Canada, carried — the “agentic” claim rests on one word from Bell with no published evidence). The NCSC’s warning that future frameworks may prioritise stealth is a forecast, not a current observation — detection that keys on today’s noise will need to be re-tested as the tooling changes.
