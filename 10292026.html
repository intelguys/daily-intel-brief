APAC

Japan's non-payment test, and Korea's shift to a state-level response

Both stories advanced materially in the window, and both moved on the response side rather than the intrusion side.

Japan — GMO: the leak site was suspended, and the bounty is working

HighIn window

By roughly 04:00 JST on 28 October the US-based registrar behind the extortion site suspended the domain and a mirror, taking the publication channel offline. GMO had refused the demand outright and stated it would never comply. The attackers had threatened to publish another customer's data each day at noon; the first release, on 27 October, appeared to be one customer's photograph, name, address and financial/online activity details.

The bounty is the operationally interesting part. GMO opened its “GMO Cybercrime Bounty” at 12:00 JST on 28 October, capped at USD 1 million (~¥150 M), seeking the perpetrators' identities, infrastructure (servers, IPs, domains), crypto fund flows and command chain. Japanese reporting counts 113 tips in the first six hours. Compensation is tiered: ¥5,000 goodwill plus ¥10,000 (basic data), ¥15,000 (financial/authentication data) or ¥25,000 (highly private data) per category, capped at ¥3 M per disclosure.

Why this matters beyond Japan. Scope remains “more than 26,000 servers/customers affected” and unconfirmed by GMO. A registrar takedown plus a paid-tips programme is a template that other financial-adjacent victims will be asked about — and it worked faster than the criminal process usually does. It also carries a cost nobody has quantified: the data was already published once.

Sources: GMO — 12th report, 27 Oct · GMO — response policy notice, 28 Oct · Japan Times — site shut down, 28 Oct · Asahi — extortion site knocked offline, 28 Oct · Yomiuri — 113 tips in six hours

Korea — police probe and a presidential emergency meeting

HighIn window

The National Office of Investigation opened a preliminary (pre-indictment) inquiry on the morning of 28 October, handled by the same team already working the earlier financial-sector cases; police are reported as not ruling out a common actor across the September and October waves. The same day, the presidential National Security Office (deputy chief Song Ki-ho) convened an emergency meeting with the FSC, FSS, the science ministry, the Personal Information Protection Commission, the Financial Security Institute and KISA.

On 29 October the FSS governor told the board chairs of eight bank holding companies that AI-enabled simultaneous attacks may become routine, and criticised firms treating security compliance as a formality. Reporting on the same day counts 20 publicly identified financial firms affected and notes the wave has spread from banks to securities, asset management, insurance and crypto. A stalled “Digital Finance Safety Act” is back under scrutiny, and the FSC has said bank-level security standards will be extended to the virtual-asset sector.

The entry-point pattern is the transferable finding: account-opening servers, web systems and internal groupware — external-facing but non-core. No reporting describes access to transaction networks.

EMEA

ECB — AI-related cyber action plans due 31 October

HighTwo days

Significant institutions must submit concrete AI-related cyber action plans to their Joint Supervisory Teams by 31 October 2026. The requirement was reiterated publicly on 20 October. For a board, this is now a documentation-completeness question rather than a policy question: what is submitted has to be evidenced, and the Korean wave gives a concrete scenario to test it against.

Sources: BIS — Elderson speech, 27 Oct

AMER

One insurer claim that survives verification, and a kernel bug on a two-day clock

Two of the three “financial” leak-feed rows this window were false positives on the sector field. The one that held is an insurer.

Akira claims CHAT Insurance — the only financial claim that verified

LowUnverified claim

A leak-site entry posted 28 October 12:58 UTC under the generic name “Insurance Services” resolves to CHAT Insurance, an Ontario commercial, home, auto and travel insurer founded in 2015 with about ten offices and 20,000+ policyholders. The claim describes ~112 GB including employee identity documents (passports, licences, birth certificates), client PII for roughly 250 individuals, contracts and NDAs. No ransom figure or sample was disclosed at the time of writing, and no company statement was found.

Handling: a claim is not an incident. But an insurer with 20,000 policyholders in a claim of this shape is worth a vendor-risk question if you place business with it or rely on it as a broker counterparty.

Sources: ransomware.live — Akira victim record · ThreatCluster listing · Yazoul — unverified-claim caveat, 28 Oct

Linux kernel CVE-2026-46300 — KEV with a 31 October deadline

HighDue 31 Oct

Added to KEV on 28 October with forensicTriage: Yes and a deadline of 31 October. The flaw is a missing initialisation in socket-buffer coalescing: skb_try_coalesce() can fail to preserve the shared-fragment marker, with the result that ESP input processing decrypts shared fragments in place, allowing unintended modification of page-cache-backed data.

Read the vector, not the score. NVD lists CVSS 7.8 with AV:L/PR:L — local, low privileges. A KEV listing on a local vector means it is being used inside real intrusion chains, most plausibly as a post-access step against hypervisors, container hosts and IPsec endpoints. For a financial estate, the population that matters is virtualisation hosts and site-to-site VPN gateways, not desktops.

Sources: CISA KEV alert, 28 Oct · NVD — CVE-2026-46300

VMware ESX CVE-2026-41709 — a KEV entry that is really about hiding

HighCVSS 2.7

Also added on 28 October, due 11 November: insufficient logging in VMware ESX, exploitable by an actor who already holds administrator access, allowing operations to be performed without being logged. NVD scores it 2.7; the KEV listing is the more informative signal. This is an anti-forensics primitive — its value to an attacker is that it degrades exactly the logs a financial institution would rely on during an incident, which makes it relevant to the Korean and Japanese cases even though it is not their entry point.

Sources: CISA KEV alert, 28 Oct · NVD — CVE-2026-41709

Cross-regional

What outlasts the individual incidents

1 · Non-core systems are carrying the losses

Account-opening servers, groupware, web front ends, a VPS platform for FX traders. In every case this week the compromised component sat outside the transaction network — which is where the detection investment usually sits. The control that would have helped is treating external-facing internal systems as tier-1: log depth, segmentation, and the same monitoring as core.

2 · Non-payment now has a worked example — and a cost

GMO refused, lost the data once, then saw the publication channel suspended within a day and drew 113 bounty tips in six hours. That is a better outcome than paying, and it is not a clean one: the data is already out. Boards should rehearse this path with the cost stated honestly rather than presented as a win.

3 · The sector-tag on leak feeds is unreliable, and this window proves it

Of three “financial/insurance/bank” rows, one was a genuine insurer (CHAT), one was a UK fashion retailer tagged “Insurance”, and one was a Pennsylvania architecture firm whose blurb contains the word “bank”. Verify the victim's business before it enters an incident count — this is the third window in a row where the tag misled.

4 · Two KEV entries with infrastructure relevance, both with short deadlines

A local-vector kernel flaw due in two days and an anti-forensics flaw in ESX due in thirteen. Neither is a banking-core vulnerability; both sit in the layer that everything else runs on.

Recommendations

What we would do, and by when

Immediate · 0–7 days

  1. Patch or mitigate CVE-2026-46300 on hypervisors, container hosts and IPsec endpoints by 31 October. Treat the local vector as a chain step, not as low risk; confirm kernel versions on the virtualisation layer, where patching is slowest.
  2. Submit the ECB AI-related cyber action plan by 31 October where in scope, and use the Korean entry-point pattern (account-opening servers, groupware, web front ends) as the test scenario.
  3. Inventory VMware ESX logging configuration and confirm that administrative actions are captured out-of-band. CVE-2026-41709 is an anti-forensics primitive; the mitigation is a log path an ESX admin cannot silently disable.
  4. Check any exposure to CHAT Insurance if it appears in your broker or vendor chain — a claim, not an incident, but the counterparty question is cheap to ask.

Short term · 1–4 weeks

  1. Re-tier external-facing non-core systems — account opening, groupware, marketing and web front ends — to tier-1 monitoring and segmentation.
  2. Rehearse the non-payment playbook (registrar takedown, bounty, compensation tiers, communications) using GMO as the worked example, with legal and comms in the room.
  3. Add a victim-verification step to leak-feed intake so sector tags are confirmed against the entity's actual business before counting.

Strategic · 1–6 months

  1. Treat third-party platforms that hold trading sessions, policyholder data or customer PII as in-scope for your own resilience testing, not just for contractual assurances.

Confidence

Why these judgements are not higher than they are

The Korean wave still may not be one campaign

Four disclosures, four dates, four systems — and now a police hypothesis that they may share an actor. A hypothesis with official attention is still a hypothesis. We keep the incidents separate and say so.

The GMO outcome is a snapshot, not a resolution

A registrar suspension is reversible by a determined attacker with a new domain, and GMO itself warns replacement sites may appear. “Site down” and “attack stopped” are different claims.

CHAT Insurance is a claim

No company statement, no regulator filing, no sample. One tracker lists it as published, others explicitly hedge. It stays at Low.

EMEA and AMER silence is a sourcing artefact

FS-ISAC member bulletins were not available, and no European or US regulator published an incident notice in the window. The correct phrasing is “not found in the sources consulted”.
