1.1 Plaid KARTE cascade — supplier breach keeps growing

Status: confirmed.

Plaid's KARTE customer-experience platform was attacked on 13 November: a vulnerability behind a public data-retrieval route was exploited, then stolen credentials were used to reach the KARTE admin console. Plaid's investigation was completed on 19 November and published on 20 November; it found data from about 2,200 companies leaked or potentially leaked, including integration credentials (secret keys and access tokens) for 754 client companies. Company disclosures stacked up all week: JINS disclosed about 18.29 million member accounts on 17 November; MUFG Bank about 6.5 million records on 18 November; SMBC up to about 14 million on 20 November — the largest single-bank figure; ZOZO about 29.18 million and Marui Group about 17.33 million (including about 8.5 million EPOS card holders) on 20 November, pushing published counts past 140 million records — a tally of company disclosures with overlaps, not 140 million people. KARTE stays offline with a staged restart targeted for mid-January 2027, per-company data handovers begin 24 November, and Plaid forecasts a ¥2 billion loss. No attribution has been published.

Sources: Reuters Japan — SMBC / MUFG KARTE disclosures (20 Nov, translated from Japanese) · Impress Watch — Plaid investigation complete, restart targeted mid-January 2027 (20 Nov, translated from Japanese)

1.2 n0n publishes Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard customer data

Status: confirmed (publication and company confirmations); the Qubole link is reported, not confirmed.

After its 48-hour deadline set on 16 November expired, the n0n group published customer marketing data attributed to all three firms on 19 November. JPMorgan confirmed third-party service providers were targeted and marketing data that may include customer names and email addresses was accessed; Visa confirmed an incident at an Asia-Pacific third-party provider with its own systems unaffected; Mastercard confirmed a limited exposure from one benefit programme. The published sets hold about 16,000 JPMorgan Reserve customer email addresses (plus 124 employee emails), over 6,000 Visa premium customers' details in Asia-Pacific, and about 2,000 alleged Mastercard entries that researchers say look like test data rather than genuine customer records. No card numbers, passwords or balances are in the published sets. BleepingComputer reported on 20 November that n0n claims it attacked data-platform provider Qubole on 11 November and used compromised Qubole credentials to reach customers' AWS environments; Qubole has not responded and none of the three firms has named a vendor, so that link stays reported.

Sources: Reuters — JPMorgan says third-party vendors targeted in cyberattack (19 Nov) · Cybernews — Visa confirms cyberattack as hackers claim JPMorgan was hit too (19–20 Nov) · BleepingComputer — n0n claims Qubole attack behind Chase, Visa and Mastercard leaks (20 Nov)

1.3 VMware vCenter zero-day CVE-2026-123890 — exploited since September, now in KEV

Status: confirmed.

Google Threat Intelligence Group disclosed on 19 November that suspected PRC-nexus actor ARMORY CASTLE exploited CVE-2026-123890, an unauthenticated authentication bypass in vCenter Server's Directory Service (9.8), as a zero-day in early September, using a custom Go tool (BONESAW) to create a vSphere single-sign-on administrator account, exfiltrate the directory database and steal the STS SAML signing key. Broadcom patched it the same day in VMSA-2026-0009 (fixed in vCenter 9.1.1.0200, 9.0.2.0200 and 8.0 U3m); there is no workaround. CISA added the flaw to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 20 November, with US federal remediation due 23 November. By 23 November, aggregators listed six public proof-of-concept repositories, none verified and at least one reported fake, and Rapid7's Metasploit pull request #22173 still contained no code as of 09:00 SGT on 23 November — do not run unverified exploit code.

Sources: Google Threat Intelligence Group — ARMORY CASTLE exploits VMware vCenter zero-day (19 Nov) · CISA KEV feed (catalogue v2026.11.20, 1,790 entries)

1.4 NGINX2Root — five critical flaws, fixes shipped, PoC public

Status: confirmed (flaws and fixes); no in-the-wild exploitation reported.

F5 shipped fixes on 18 November for five unauthenticated remote-code-execution flaws in NGINX, led by CVE-2026-123007 (9.2 CVSS 4.0), an integer underflow in HTTP/2 flow-control accounting present since NGINX 1.9.5 (2015). The other four are CVE-2026-123633, CVE-2026-123634, CVE-2026-121810 and CVE-2026-113425, covering HTTP/2, HTTP/3, resolver and mail-proxy paths. Rapid7 published a working proof of concept for the lead flaw on disclosure day; fake nginx2root exploits are also circulating on GitHub and should not be run. Fixed in NGINX Open Source 1.30.7 (stable) / 1.31.9 (mainline) and NGINX Plus 37.0.8.1 / 37.3.0.1 (for CVE-2026-123007; CVE-2026-123633 is fixed in Plus 37.0.7.1 / 37.2.0.1); disabling HTTP/2 is the workaround where patching must wait. The flaws were not in CISA KEV as of 23 November. NGINX sits in front of banking web, API and payment-gateway services, so this is a patch-now item despite the absence of reported exploitation.

Sources: F5 advisory K000163926 — CVE-2026-123007 · Wiz — NGINX HTTP/2 RCE vulnerabilities (“NGINX2Root”, 18 Nov) · Rapid7 — CVE-2026-123007 critical NGINX HTTP/2 integer underflow (18 Nov)

1.5 Cleverbridge breach — card numbers and CVVs stolen from a payment processor

Status: confirmed.

Payment processor Cleverbridge disclosed on 22 November that hackers breached its systems and copied payment card data: card number, expiry date and card security code (CVV/CVC) used for a purchase through Cleverbridge. It detected unauthorised activity on 4 November, took systems offline the same day and restored them the same day; its investigation later determined a third party had copied the data. The company has not said how many customers were affected, how attackers got in, how long they were inside, or which merchants' buyers were hit — its listed clients include Red Hat, SUSE, Tenable, Veeam, Jamf, Sony and Parallels. It confirmed the incident was not ransomware and nothing was encrypted, and says it has notified data protection authorities, law enforcement, payment card networks and payment partners, and is working with TransUnion's Cyberscout on notifications and complimentary credit monitoring. Malwarebytes is the only merchant so far to confirm impact, saying only “a small subset” of its customers was affected. Issuing banks should watch for unauthorised transactions and consider replacing affected cards. No standalone Cleverbridge incident-page URL was found; the company's notification is carried via BleepingComputer.

Source: BleepingComputer — Cleverbridge warns hackers stole credit card numbers, CVVs in data breach (22 Nov; carries the company's breach notification)

1.6 Samsung Securities breach — complaints-system data of 8,241 customers leaked

Status: confirmed.

Samsung Securities disclosed on 23 November (about 09:29 SGT, after that morning's briefing window, so this item comes from the wire, not a daily briefing) that an unauthorised outsider accessed its website's Voice of Customer complaints system and exfiltrated attachments customers had uploaded with complaints. The firm found traces of the intrusion on the afternoon of 21 November. Attachments belonging to 8,241 customers leaked, across 13 fields varying by person, including name, account number, address, contact details and driver's licence number. Only customers who had filed through the complaints system are affected. The firm blocked the IP address used, suspended the service, reported to the Financial Supervisory Service and the Korea Internet & Security Agency, and will notify affected customers individually; its chief executive, Park Jong-moon, posted a public apology, and the firm says it will compensate customers if actual loss is confirmed. Trading and payment systems are not reported breached, so the immediate risk is identity fraud and targeted phishing. It is another Korean securities firm in the current breach wave. Attacker, entry route, any misuse, and any link to the wider wave are still unknown.

Source: YTN — Samsung Securities disclosure (23 Nov, translated from Korean)

1.7 Paydon breach — 251,172 records, services halted, staged restart under way

Status: confirmed.

Infcurion, the contractor running the Wallet Station payment platform behind Japan's Paydon app, confirmed unauthorised access on 19 November. A total of 251,172 records leaked, including member names, dates of birth, phone numbers, sex, email addresses and passwords, plus bank account information, point balances and regional promotion voucher point balances, transaction history and merchant information. Four Kagoshima financial institutions — Kagoshima Bank, Minami Nippon Bank, Kagoshima Mutual Credit Cooperative and Kagoshima Shinkin Bank — halted all Paydon services at 06:20 JST on 20 November. Infcurion's third report (noon JST, 21 November) confirms no new unauthorised access since the system was cut off on 19 November; from 20 November it paused Wallet Station for providers that were not the target of the leak so emergency checks could be carried out, and providers whose checks found no intrusion and no leak began restarting in stages from 21 November. The provider whose data leaked remains suspended with no restart date, and no misuse of the leaked information had been confirmed. The item was missed by the 20, 21 and 22 November briefs and caught as a wire catch-up on 22 November.

Source: Paydon / Infcurion official notice (20 Nov, translated from Japanese)

1.8 KEPCO breach — about 1.52 million people's data, including bank and card numbers

Status: confirmed.

State-run Korea Electric Power Corp. confirmed on 22 November that personal data of about 1.52 million people leaked from two systems: 650,487 users of its KEPCO ON customer-service and bill-payment platform and 869,761 users of its electronic procurement system (SRM), for a combined 1,520,248 records. The KEPCO ON leak exposed 12 categories including names, resident registration numbers, phone numbers, addresses, bank names, account numbers, account-holder names, card issuers, card numbers and cardholder names, plus billing records; the SRM leak exposed corporate representatives' and employees' names, phone numbers and emails. KEPCO says an external attacker accessed both systems; it discovered the breach on 20 November, blocked external IP access and suspended some services, filed with the Personal Information Protection Commission on 22 November, and is working with financial companies to prevent secondary damage. Its president issued a public apology. This is a second breach in as many months, after an October leak of employee records. KEPCO is an energy utility, not a bank, but the exposed data is financial data, and Korean banks should expect phishing and voice-phishing using it. Per-system counts differ slightly between outlets (650,487 vs 654,487 KEPCO ON users).

Sources: Yonhap News Agency — KEPCO confirms about 1.52 million people's data leaked (22 Nov) · The Korea Times — KEPCO reports another massive data breach (22 Nov)

1.9 Rakuten Securities leak — up to about 15.7 million accounts, FSA action pending

Status: confirmed.

Rakuten Securities disclosed on 17 November that customer data held in a test-environment system may have leaked; the leak was confirmed on 18 November. Up to about 11.25 million accounts' login IDs were exposed, and up to about 4.47 million accounts had names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, employers, withdrawal bank accounts, declared income and financial-asset data exposed — the two groups do not overlap, so the maximum is about 15.72 million accounts. The company detected possible unauthorised access on 16 November and found the test-environment data may have leaked on 11 November; the cause is under investigation. No passwords or PINs are confirmed leaked and no unauthorised transactions attributable to the incident have been confirmed. Japan's Financial Services Agency issued a reporting order on 19 November; Financial Services Minister Satsuki Katayama addressed the leak on 20 November and the FSA will consider a business improvement order if information-management flaws are found. A lawyer-led class-action recruitment site passed 20,000 registrants by 14:00 JST on 20 November. A fake “compensation policy” screenshot is circulating; Rakuten has announced no compensation, and contacts asking for lawsuit fees are fake.

Sources: Reuters Japan — Rakuten Securities announcement (17 Nov, translated from Japanese) · NHK WORLD-JAPAN — Rakuten Securities possible leak (17 Nov)

1.10 BankWeaver — bank digital-banking middleware turned into a backdoor

Status: confirmed (campaign and one victim, per vendor research); the six further banks' compromise is unconfirmed.

Forescout Research (Vedere Labs) disclosed “BankWeaver” on 19 November: attackers abused a Temenos Quantum Fabric authentication service configured for anonymous access and reachable from the internet, exchanged an anonymous token for an administrator token without credentials, and deployed two malicious Java components — FabricShell for command execution and database queries, and FabricShadow for host reconnaissance and data exfiltration. One unnamed financial services institution was confirmed compromised in a breach running from early September 2026. Six further banks appear to share the same exposure, but compromise has not been confirmed for them, and Temenos confirmed the cause is a misconfiguration. No MITRE IDs were published. Banks should check whether their Quantum Fabric authentication service accepts anonymous access and is internet-reachable, and hunt for FabricShell and FabricShadow components. (This item rests on the 20 Nov briefing record; the Forescout primary was not re-fetched for this pack.)

Source: Forescout Research — BankWeaver (19 Nov)

1.11 Java4Shell patching progress — fixes now broadly available, still no confirmed exploitation

Status: confirmed (flaw and patch status); exploitation unconfirmed.

CISA issued an urgent alert on 17 November for CVE-2026-114938 (“Java4Shell”), a memory-corruption flaw in the HotSpot JVM's AVX-512 Base64 decoder that can allow unauthenticated remote code execution on x86-64 systems with AVX-512 VBMI (OpenJDK 17.0.6 and later, also 21, 25 and 27); Oracle released fixed builds on 17 November as part of its November CPU. Patching progressed through the week: Eclipse Temurin published fixed binaries for all four lines on x64 Linux on 18 November, with Windows and macOS builds landing by 20 November; Microsoft shipped fixed builds on 18 November; Red Hat's Build of OpenJDK fixed builds shipped on 18 November and all six RHEL errata followed on 19 November; Keycloak 26.8 container images now ship the fixed runtime; and Okta published fixed versions on 19 November. Cisco's advisory (rev 1.3, 20 Nov) lists the affected products with fixes still to be determined; Catalyst Center was corrected to not affected in rev 1.3. Elastic's fix is slated for bundled JDK 27.0.1.1+4, and Siemens Industrial Edge Management Pro (V1, V2 and Virtual) is affected in all versions with no fix — block direct internet access. GreyNoise reports 261 Java4Shell-shaped sessions from 7 IPs as of 21 November, with zero successful intrusions, and the flaw was not in CISA KEV as of 23 November. Two proof-of-concept repositories were verified by reading code and are linked on the Java4Shell tracker — arkrwn's crash/leak reproducer and ikow/java4shell-poc (heap corruption confirmed on three products, not RCE). Aggregator counts reached 55 on 19 November, 70 by 20 November and 78 by 23 November (unverified); none of the additional repositories was verified or linked.

Sources: CISA — Critical Java Vulnerability Requires Immediate Patching (17 Nov) · Wiz — CVE-2026-114938 RCE Java4Shell OpenJDK (17 Nov)

1.12 ruby2shell — critical pre-auth RCE in Ruby, reachable in default Rails apps

Status: confirmed.

Ruby's advisory was posted 18 November and published 19 November 00:00 UTC: CVE-2026-132825 (“ruby2shell”), a one-byte heap overflow in string case conversion, present since Ruby 2.4.0, which Ruby rates Critical (NVD scores it 7.4 on a local vector with high complexity — the scoring disagreement is noted, not re-rated). It is reachable without authentication in default Ruby on Rails and classic-style Sinatra applications, because Rack's MethodOverride middleware upper-cases the _method field of a crafted form submission before any application code runs. Fixed in Ruby 3.3.14, 3.4.13 and 4.0.10, released 18 November; Ruby 3.2 and older are end of life and receive no fix. There is no workaround — upgrade the interpreter and restart app servers; upgrading gems does nothing. Cloudflare WAF rules block known patterns as a compensating control only. Self-hosted software bundling affected Ruby includes GitLab, GitHub Enterprise Server, Mastodon, OpenProject, Discourse and Chatwoot. Ruby is aware of proof-of-concept code showing remote code execution in some scenarios; Veria Labs, which published the research on 19 November, plans to publish its proof of concept about 25 November. No in-the-wild exploitation has been reported. The flaw was missed by the 20 and 21 November editions and caught on 22 November.

Sources: Ruby — Multiple vulnerabilities in Regexp and String (advisory posted 18 Nov, published 19 Nov 00:00 UTC) · Cloudflare — How Cloudflare WAF protects against ruby2shell (CVE-2026-132825) (20 Nov) · Censys — Advisory: Ruby heap buffer overflow in string case conversion, CVE-2026-132825 (19 Nov)

1.13 Binance aftermath — laundering accelerates while full reopening stays unconfirmed

Status: confirmed (Elliptic blockchain analysis; holdings estimates unreconciled).

Elliptic's 20 November update says at least 2,310 BTC (about $149 million) of the stolen Binance funds has gone into coinjoin mixers, about 90% of it since 17 November — 480 BTC on 17 November alone, more than the previous two weeks combined — almost all coordinated by Kruw, a Wasabi Wallet coordinator. Five of the nine largest ETH wallets have been emptied since 17 November, moving about 488,000 ETH (about $860 million). About $2.35 billion remains in addresses linked to the theft, mostly ETH and BTC, and about $2.85 billion is frozen; very little has been cashed out, and the 18,270 BTC stolen directly from Binance has still not moved. The laundering spans more than 27,000 wallets on at least 16 blockchains, about 55 services and about 90,000 transactions. Binance obtained a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts on 18 November (per the 22 Nov briefing record, not the Elliptic article). Full withdrawal reopening was aimed at about 22 November, but no confirmation of completion was found by 23 November; withdrawals had reopened on 87 networks and a new zero-knowledge proof of reserves covering 198 tokens (snapshot 12 Nov 00:00 UTC, block height 975,493, BTC at 100.05%) was announced on 14 November. Attribution to North Korea's TraderTraitor is per the FBI (31 October). The $2.35 billion remaining-holdings figure is Elliptic's; Lookonchain counts 602,402 ETH at about $1.09 billion — the estimates are unreconciled, so they are not added together.

Source: Elliptic — How North Korea is laundering the $6.4 billion stolen from Binance (updated 20 Nov)

Geopolitical cascades

The week's pattern is not seven separate incidents. It is the same three cascades repeating: a supplier or shared platform fails and the damage spreads to firms that never dealt with the attacker; AI tooling lowers the cost of entry (voice, agents, loan-fraud automation); and regulators, once they act, convert one incident into sector-wide deadlines.

3.1 Korea — the FSI audit becomes a sector-wide inspection, then another securities firm is breached

What happened

The Financial Security Institute (FSI) breach set the sequence in motion before this window: a webshell attack at 13:54 on 9 November failed, and an SMS-authentication bypass was then used to create a fake FSS-level account. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) gave that timeline to the National Assembly on 13 November, and its special audit of the FSI began on 16 November. The FSI's evaluation system was still shut down at the end of the window, with no restoration date, and 2,912 firms form the assessment population whose appeals are blocked. Reporting put the wider Korean wave at 37 firms and about 473,000 records in one month. On 17 November the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) called in CISOs and security officers from 227 electronic-finance and 47 online investment-linked firms — 274 firms in total — for an intensive external-attack-surface inspection, with first-round self-assessments due 30 November. The same day it disclosed new incidents at Spoon (Giftiell, about 18,000 people's data) and Zoomfund (about KRW 300 million in customer deposits), and began surprise on-site inspections of high-risk branches at four banks, the first in about ten years. The FSC will require large firms to run vulnerability assessments twice a year instead of once, and to identify and classify all IT assets.

The wave kept producing victims through the week: Coinone's breach via its customer-support contractor MetaM (disclosed 15 November; passwords, resident registration numbers, bank account numbers and wallet addresses may have leaked; victim count unconfirmed; disclosure took about 70 hours 40 minutes), KOMSCO's chak gift-certificate platform breach (3,083 customers confirmed), and Mirae Asset Life Insurance (10,418 victims as of 5 November, up from about 6,800 — crossing the 10,000-person 72-hour reporting duty). The KB Kookmin count was revised from 119 to 2,210 after FSS inspection. Then, on 23 November, Samsung Securities disclosed the Voice of Customer breach (8,241 customers; see §1.6).

Sources: NoCutNews (17 Nov, translated from Korean) · NoCutNews (18 Nov, translated from Korean)

Cascade mechanism

One shared assessment body (the FSI) fails, so the regulator cannot trust the sector's own evaluation system — and responds by inspecting the sector itself, on a deadline. Each new disclosure (a contractor, a gift-certificate platform, a complaints portal) then widens the lesson: the entry point is rarely the core banking system. It is a support contractor, a staff-facing mobile or loan system, or a customer-facing web form.

Who is exposed next

The 274 summoned firms, above all small and mid-sized operators — the FSS says intrusion attempts via external contact points continue there. Firms whose 30 November self-assessments surface unpatched external exposure, and the 8,241 Samsung Securities complainants, who now hold a verified customer list for impersonation calls and phishing citing the incident. Korean banks should also expect phishing and voice-phishing using data from the separate KEPCO breach (about 1.52 million people, disclosed 22 November), which is a utility breach whose exposed data is financial data.

Still unknown

Who attacked Samsung Securities and how they entered the Voice of Customer system; whether any of the leaked data has been misused or any customer has suffered a loss; whether the Samsung incident is linked to the wider wave or stands alone — no link has been established; and the FSI audit's findings and the evaluation system's restoration date.

3.2 EU Commission / ARTEX — a confirmed AI campaign, an unconfirmed tool attribution

What happened

On 19 November the European Commission confirmed it had responded to an AI-generated cyber campaign detected on 22 October and confirmed after initial investigation on 26 October. At the midday Brussels briefing, spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed the campaign was a misuse of a publicly available AI tool. Several applications were taken down temporarily as a precaution; most have been restored. The Commission named no attribution, no ransom demand, and no tool, entry point or affected application, and said there is no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff “as a whole”. What the Commission did not say is as important as what it did. Exposure-census context

The disclosure landed amid large, fast-moving counts of exposed offensive-AI consoles: Spectrum's 18 November census counted 2,335 exposed consoles (436 ARTEX hosts in one hosting network, AS54600); ExtraHop counted 3,447 ARTEX hosts; SOCRadar counted 916 confirmed live ARTEX-family consoles in the US. A high-profile public institution confirms an AI-tool campaign while declining to name the tool, and press reporting names it anyway. Other EU institutions and agencies running similar publicly available AI tooling, organisations whose exposed consoles appear in the censuses, and financial firms whose staff or vendors use the same class of agentic tooling.

Still unknown

Whether ARTEX was used at all in the Commission incident; how it was used; whether data was stolen; the duration and the systems affected; and attribution.

3.3 Ukraine / FBI — AI-voice vishing confirmed as a ransomware entry point

What happened

Ukrainian police, working with the FBI, arrested four suspected key members of a ransomware group on 18–19 November. The group won initial access with AI-generated voice calls impersonating IT support: it built its own platform with AI-generated voice messages to automate fraudulent calls, tricking employees into disclosing credentials or installing remote-access software (TeamViewer / AnyDesk). In one incident an attacker kept an employee on the phone while another accessed the company's systems. At least 28 companies paid more than $40.5 million in ransoms. Sectors spanned oil and gas, pharmaceutical, manufacturing, logistics, financial and critical infrastructure. No MITRE mapping was published for the group, and no group name was published in the briefings.

Sources: BleepingComputer (19 Nov) · Help Net Security (19 Nov)

Cascade mechanism

AI voice moves vishing from a fraud problem to a ransomware initial-access problem. The control that fails is not email filtering but help-desk verification: a convincing voice, a plausible IT-support pretext, and a live human kept on the line while a second operator moves inside. That is the same human-verification weakness behind the separate Unit 42 “Dialing for Data” vishing waves reported the same week, which target finance and defeat MFA through adversary-in-the-middle techniques. The arrests validate the threat model; they do not remove the tooling, which is built on publicly available voice synthesis.

Who is exposed next

Any firm whose IT support resets credentials or installs remote-access software on the strength of a phone call — especially financial firms, where a single help-desk interaction can hand over VPN or remote-access credentials. Firms relying on SMS-based MFA are doubly exposed, since the same social-engineering path defeats it.

Still unknown

The group's name; whether the arrests disrupt its operations or only remove four members; the full victim list; and whether the voice platform was shared with or sold to other groups.

NBFC loan-diversion fraud — two figures, unreconciled. Cascade mechanism

The Court is turning a fraud pattern into duties for three parties at once: platforms must show how they will stop the scams, banks face compensation liability where due diligence fails, and the RBI must produce new KYC rules on a fixed timetable. That is the same regulatory-amplifier pattern as Korea's 30 November self-assessment deadline — the same date, a separate regime, and a coincidence worth noting rather than a connection. NBFCs and fintech lenders with approved-but-undisbursed loan pipelines, and any firm operating a payout API without tight controls on who can redirect a disbursement. The true NBFC victim count and loss (₹65 crore / 12 NBFCs against about ₹105.67 crore / 14 NBFCs); how far AI tooling went beyond “traces of AI”; the PayU access method; and the outcome of the 23 November Supreme Court hearing.

Company disclosures then stacked up through the week (see §1.1 for figures). Published counts across company disclosures top 140 million records. (Separately, the SMBC Consumer Finance incident, up to 320,118 people, is a different incident and is not part of the KARTE total.) Cascade mechanism

A marketing analytics platform held integration keys into 754 companies' systems, so one compromise becomes a credential incident for hundreds of firms at once — and the 754-company key exposure, not the record counts, is the consequential detail. Who is exposed next

The 754 companies whose integration credentials leaked — each must check whether its keys were misused at linked services (none disclosed as of 20 November) and rotate them; former KARTE clients, who must check whether their data was retained after contract end; and customers of all disclosing firms, who face phishing using the leaked fields. Japan's FSA has already issued a reporting order to Rakuten Securities over its separate leak, and its minister has spoken of a possible business improvement order — a sign that regulator attention is widening from the incident to the sector's vendor management.

Whether the leaked keys were misused at any linked service; the true number of affected people; and each company's full scope, which will only emerge from the per-company data handovers starting 24 November.

3.6 United States — CISA KEV: four due dates land on one day

What happened

CISA added four exploited flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalogue on 20 November — Apache Roller (CVE-2026-129448), VMware vCenter (CVE-2026-123890), SAP NetWeaver (CVE-2025-42884) and Cisco Expressway (CVE-2026-76466) — taking the catalogue to version 2026.11.20 with 1,790 entries. All four fell due on Monday 23 November. No new additions were made on 21, 22 or 23 November. The 18 November additions (Synology, LiteLLM, Apache Axis, REDCap) fell due on 21 November, and the 13 November additions fell due on 16 November.

Sources: CISA alert (20 Nov) · CISA KEV catalogue (live feed)

What a due date does and does not mean

A KEV due date is the remediation deadline for US federal civilian agencies under CISA's process. It does not mean the vulnerability is fixed everywhere, that exploitation has stopped, or that the deadline binds private-sector firms — it is not an expiry date for the risk. Its power is as a forcing function: vendors, auditors and customers treat the date as a patching deadline even where it does not legally apply, and four flaws sharing one due date concentrates that pressure into a single change window.

Cascade mechanism

The four flaws differ in ways that matter for the cascade. vCenter (CVE-2026-123890, 9.8) was exploited as a zero-day since September, attributed by Google Threat Intelligence to PRC-nexus group ARMORY CASTLE; proof-of-concept code is circulating in unverified repositories, at least one reported fake, and Rapid7's Metasploit pull request still had no code as of 23 November 09:00. Expressway (CVE-2026-76466) saw its first in-the-wild exploitation documented by F6 on 20 November (SGT), days after the public proof of concept — two internet-facing boxes breached, then a pivot to internal cluster nodes and root access. Roller was already actively exploited when added; NetWeaver is the oldest flaw of the four (a 2025 CVE). One due date therefore covers four different exploitation timelines, and the weakest-patched of the four sets the sector's exposure.

Who is exposed next

Organisations running any of the four products unpatched, above all internet-facing vCenter and Expressway deployments, and firms whose vendors or MSPs run them. The due date passing does not reduce that exposure.

Still unknown

Patch uptake across the four products; whether the due date passing changes attacker behaviour; and which circulating vCenter proof-of-concept repositories, if any, are genuine.

Binance's 198-token zero-knowledge proof of reserves went live, formally announced on 14 November (snapshot 12 November 00:00 UTC, block height 975,493; BTC at 100.05%). Withdrawals had reopened on 87 networks, with full reopening aimed at about 22 November — but no completion confirmation had appeared by the 23 November briefing, and no final root-cause report had appeared in the window's briefings. Meanwhile the stolen funds kept moving. Elliptic's 20 November update says at least 2,310 BTC (about $149 million) of the stolen funds has gone into coinjoin mixers, about 90% of it since 17 November — almost all coordinated by Kruw, a Wasabi Wallet coordinator — and five of the nine largest ETH wallets were emptied since 17 November (about 488,000 ETH, about $860 million). About $2.35 billion remains in attacker-linked wallets and about $2.85 billion is frozen; very little has been cashed out. The 18,270 BTC stolen directly from Binance has still not moved. The laundering spans more than 27,000 wallets on at least 16 blockchains, about 55 services and about 90,000 transactions. Binance obtained a $6.5 billion worldwide freezing order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts on 18 November (per the 22 Nov briefing record). Attribution to North Korea's TraderTraitor is per the FBI (31 October).

Two earlier movements set the pace: on 17 November, 74,881 ETH (about $137 million) was dispersed within about an hour, and 34,279.66 ETH (about $63 million) moved from a wallet dormant since 30 October to three new addresses.

Source: Elliptic (updated 20 Nov)

Cascade mechanism

Proof of reserves and withdrawal reopening address customer confidence; they do not touch the laundering. The freezing order, the mixers and the dispersed wallets form a second, slower cascade through the crypto ecosystem: over-the-counter brokers, mixers and receiving exchanges must now decide what to do with funds that are publicly traced but still moving, and the acceleration after 17 November — 90% of the coinjoin volume in a few days — suggests the operators moved faster once the proof and reopening timetable were public.

Who is exposed next

OTC brokers, mixers and exchanges receiving the flows; Binance customers still waiting for full withdrawal reopening; and regulators watching whether a freezing order of this size can actually be enforced across 16 blockchains and 55 services.

Still unknown

Whether full withdrawal reopening was completed, and when; the final root cause; how much of the frozen $2.85 billion is recoverable and returns to customers; and the remaining-holdings total — Elliptic puts about $2.35 billion in attacker-linked wallets, while Lookonchain counts 602,402 ETH at about $1.09 billion. Those estimates are unreconciled and count different things; they must not be added together.

3.8 Cross-cutting read

Three patterns run through all seven topics:

1. Supplier concentration is the main cascade. The FSI, MetaM, KARTE's 754 keys, Wellington IT's credit-union platform and the suspected shared vendor behind the n0n Visa / JPMorgan / Mastercard publications (Qubole — reported, not confirmed; Qubole has not responded) all show the same shape: one provider's failure becomes dozens of firms' incidents. The question to ask of every vendor is now the one the n0n case forces: what customer data does it hold, which of your cloud roles can it assume, and are those roles still needed?

2. AI is the entry point, not only the payload. ARTEX (reported, unconfirmed at the Commission), AI-voice vishing (confirmed, Ukraine / FBI), CyberStrikeAI in the NBFC fraud (reported in one case), and agentic tooling elsewhere in the week all lower the cost of the first step — a call, a scan, a diverted loan — rather than the last one.

3. Regulators are the amplifier. The FSS's 274-firm call-in and 30 November deadline, Japan's FSA reporting order to Rakuten, India's Supreme Court directions and the RBI's 30 November KYC timetable, and the UK Bank of England / FCA / PRA cyber-resilience consultation (closing 26 February 2027) all convert single incidents into sector-wide work. Singapore's MAS warning against giving AI agents bank login credentials belongs in the same pattern. The 30 November convergence — Korean self-assessments and RBI KYC notification due the same day — is a coincidence of two separate regimes, not a coordinated deadline, but firms operating in both will feel it as one.
