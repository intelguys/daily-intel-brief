2 BLUF — Key judgments

Five global judgments in estimative language, with confidence.

Key judgment 1

It is likely that supplier failure, not direct attack on banks, is now banks' main incident route worldwide.

High confidence

One compromise became dozens of firms' incidents across Asia and Europe: KARTE's 754 leaked integration credentials, BankWeaver, Wellington IT, Paydon, and a reported Qubole link.

Why it matters: the perimeter is now the supplier's. A marketing platform, a middleware vendor, a payments contractor and a credit-union provider each turned one breach into sector-wide exposure.

Executive action: rotate vendor keys and inventory vendor-run tools and shared payment platforms before the next disclosure wave (see Section 10).

Key judgment 2

It is likely that AI has moved from research into confirmed operations against financial targets.

High confidence

Cairn agents mapped a MONAI Label flaw to arbitrary file write in about 11 minutes and reached root; AI-voice calls impersonating IT support opened the way into at least 28 companies that paid over $40.5 million.

Why it matters: voice cloning defeats the help-desk channel, and autonomous agents compress the patch window to minutes.

Executive action: mandate callback verification and phishing-resistant MFA for help-desk resets, and treat any internet-reachable agent or automation framework as a critical exposure.

Key judgment 3

It is almost certain that exploited — not new — flaws are the immediate patching priority.

High confidence

13 of the 20 tracked flaws were already exploited, and several KEV additions date from 2025 or earlier; exploitation, not age, set the priority. A vCenter zero-day (CVE-2026-123890) was exploited since early September by suspected state-linked actor ARMORY CASTLE; Broadcom patched 19 November, CISA listed it with a 23 November due date.

Why it matters: virtualisation underpins core banking systems worldwide, so a compromised vCenter can expose every workload it hosts.

Executive action: close the four KEV items due 23 November and hunt, not just patch, vCenter (see Section 10, row 1).

Key judgment 4

It is likely that ransomware's leverage has shifted from encryption to publication, and that deadlines no longer predict publication.

High confidence

n0n published Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard customer data on 19 November, after its deadline expired; the retail batch, Vortex/Carrington and Pixelleaks/Ekwiik deadlines all passed quietly.

Why it matters: firms cannot timetable their response around an actor's deadline; published customer names and emails become targeted-phishing feedstock.

Executive action: treat any shared data-platform vendor with cross-account cloud access as a concentration risk and pre-stage customer-notification and phishing-watch plans.

Key judgment 5

It is likely that regulators will convert this week's incidents into standing duties within weeks.

High confidence

Regulators on three continents moved in the same week. Korea's FSS called in 274 firms for inspection, self-assessments due 30 November. India's Supreme Court is driving RBI KYC Amendment Directions to be notified by 30 November and operationalised by 31 December. The UK designated five more critical third parties and opened a joint cyber-resilience consultation closing 26 February 2027.

Why it matters: incident response now carries a regulatory timetable, not just a technical one.

Executive action: assign ownership of the 30 November Korean and Indian deadlines now, even without direct exposure; diary the UK consultation response (see Section 9).

The story of the week — so what

The week began with a breach that did not look like a banking incident. Plaid's KARTE marketing platform was attacked on 13 November; the investigation published on 20 November showed data from about 2,200 companies leaked or may have leaked, and integration credentials — secret keys and access tokens — exposed for 754 client companies. No misuse had been disclosed by 20 November, and per-company handovers were due from 24 November, so it was still unfolding as the window closed.

In Korea, a breach at the sector's own assessment body brought regulation within days. After the Financial Security Institute breach, the Financial Supervisory Service called in officers from 227 electronic-finance and 47 investment-linked firms on 17 November — 274 in total — for intensive inspection of external attack surfaces, with self-assessments due 30 November. New victims kept appearing through peripheral systems, not core banking: Samsung Securities disclosed access to its complaints system on 23 November.

The same pattern repeated wherever a trusted intermediary sat between a bank and its customers. Attackers abused a Temenos Quantum Fabric authentication service configured for anonymous access and reachable from the internet, exchanging an anonymous token for an administrator token at one unnamed institution — no vulnerability was needed. In Ireland, Wellington IT's CU Online platform, serving more than a third of the country's credit unions, was attacked with valid credentials, affecting about 40 of them, and the saved payee records taken are prime material for impersonation fraud.

The week's most urgent vulnerability was months old. A vCenter zero-day had been exploited since early September by the suspected state-linked actor ARMORY CASTLE; Broadcom patched it on 19 November, and CISA listed it with a due date of 23 November. AI, meanwhile, crossed from tooling into fraud: police in Ukraine and the FBI arrested four suspected members of a group that used AI-generated voice calls impersonating IT support to get into companies; at least 28 paid more than $40.5 million in ransoms.

Read together, these were one pattern, not separate incidents. The path in ran through trusted intermediaries — a marketing platform, a core banking software deployment, a managed IT provider — and the prize was the means of entry and impersonation — integration keys, an administrator token, valid logins, payee records. Defending the bank's own perimeter is no longer enough; the work is knowing who holds the keys, and rotating them before they are used.

4 Macro landscape and geopolitical drivers

Korea — APAC driver

Korea: breach wave became a sector-wide inspection.

The FSI breach — a webshell on 9 November, then an SMS-auth bypass creating a fake FSS-level account — led to an FSC special audit from 16 November. The FSI evaluation system was still shut down at window end, appeals from 2,912 firms blocked, and the FSS called in CISOs and security officers from 227 e-finance and 47 online investment-linked firms (274 total) for an intensive external-attack-surface inspection, self-assessments due 30 November. Surprise on-site inspections hit high-risk branches at four banks — the first in about 10 years — under a wider programme covering about 580 financial companies. The wider Korean wave: 37 firms, about 473,000 records in one month.

Implication: one regional driver among global ones — Korean counterparties and subsidiaries face a hard 30 November checkpoint; the wave hits peripheral systems (complaints portals, contractor access), not core banking. Check those first.

KARTE cascade

One marketing platform's breach became a systemic credential incident with global reach.

Attack 13 November; investigation completed 19 November, published 20 November. Data from about 2,200 companies was exposed in four overlapping groups, with published counts passing 140 million records as a tally of company disclosures (not a headcount). Plaid forecasts a ¥2 billion loss; staged restart targeted for mid-January 2027.

Implication: a non-financial supplier became a systemic credential incident for banks — SMBC up to about 14 million records maximum, MUFG Bank about 6.5 million, the wider MUFG group about 8 million across five businesses. Concentration risk now sits in marketing and data platforms, not only core banking software.

AI fraud, confirmed live

AI-voice fraud and AI agents confirmed live worldwide in the same week.

Ukrainian police and the FBI arrested four suspected members of a group behind at least 28 companies paying over $40.5 million, using AI-generated voice calls impersonating IT support. Separately, Cairn agents autonomously found and exploited a zero-day in about 11 minutes. Singapore's MAS warned on 19 November against giving personal AI agents financial-account logins and will facilitate a "know your agent" protocol under SAFR, with an Agentic Money Sandbox (no delivery or start dates given). Singapore's CIDeX exercise, 17–20 November, simulated an AI-agent swarm attacking critical infrastructure including Banking & Finance, with a fully agentic AI Blue Team trialled (results not published).

Implication: voice and agent channels are now live attack paths, not emerging risks; banks need terms, channels and limits for agent-directed transactions until a "know your agent" channel exists.

European Commission

EU confirmed its first AI-tool campaign against itself; tool unattributed.

On 19 November the European Commission confirmed it had responded to an AI-generated cyber campaign detected 22 October and confirmed 26 October; several applications were taken down temporarily, most restored. No attribution, ransom demand, tool, entry point or affected application was named, and no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff "as a whole". Press reporting (POLITICO via The Record) attributes ARTEX use via CERT-EU; the Commission has not confirmed this.

Implication: defend against AI-generated campaigns as a class, wherever they originate — the confirmed fact is the campaign, not the tool named in press reporting.

5 Priority threat themes

Grouped by intent and capability, not by malware family, across all regions in scope.

Theme A — Supplier-mediated credential exposure

Steal reusable access at scale by abusing trusted vendor access.

KARTE

754 client companies' integration credentials exposed; no key misuse disclosed as of 20 November.

Secret keys and access tokens for 754 client companies were exposed, with 570 companies' linked-in data and 487 companies' end-user behaviour data; up to 409 companies' cached data is treated as potentially leaked — an estimate, as the cache was overwritten quickly and Plaid cannot tell what was read. Handovers begin 24 November.

Qubole / n0n

Qubole link: reported, not confirmed.

Wiz documented an attacker using IAM credentials from Qubole's AWS account (14–26 October) to assume customer integration roles and steal secrets and S3 data. n0n claims it attacked Qubole on 11 November — reported, not confirmed; Qubole has not responded and none of the three firms has named a vendor. n0n's claimed volumes (53.9 GB / 30,743 files; "nearly a petabyte") are unverified and not used.

BankWeaver

One misconfigured Temenos service gave anonymous administrator access.

A misconfigured Temenos Quantum Fabric authentication service — anonymous access, internet-reachable — let an anonymous token be exchanged for an administrator token without credentials; FabricShell and FabricShadow were deployed. One unnamed financial services institution is confirmed compromised (breach from early September 2026); six further banks share the exposure, compromise not confirmed. No MITRE IDs published.

Barracuda / Bitrise

Entry was an ordinary credential failure in every case.

Barracuda: an unretired cloud key issued to an outside AI vendor for a finished project, sitting in a config file in the vendor's GitHub repo; used 1–2 November (about 44 hours, about 10 cloud accounts). Bitrise: a dormant internal test bot account, unused since 2022, no 2FA, signed in 13 November at 11:46:48 UTC with a valid username and password; within under 11 minutes agents were using cloud keys. 15 customer workspaces accessed; 919 cloud service-account keys revoked.

Business-model shift (v3-supported): access comes through the supplier's credential, not by breaking the bank — valid accounts, unretired keys, dormant accounts. The week's dominant initial-access pattern: Credential Access is the largest published tactic, 11 of 53 IDs.

Theme B — Exploitation of edge and infrastructure flaws

Gain unauthenticated access via public-facing RCE; old flaws becoming urgent.

vCenter zero-day

9.8 vCenter zero-day exploited since early September.

Scored 9.8 by Broadcom, exploited since early September 2026 by ARMORY CASTLE (suspected PRC-nexus, formerly UNC5221) via a BeyondTrust Remote Support zero-day, CVE-2026-102764. The actor created a vSphere SSO admin account, exfiltrated the directory database and stole the STS SAML signing key ("Golden SAML"). Patched 19 November, listed in KEV 20 November, federal due date 23 November. No verified public proof of concept as of 23 November 09:00 SGT; at least one reported fake.

NGINX2Root

Five unauthenticated NGINX RCE flaws, public PoC, no reported exploitation.

Lead CVE-2026-123007, each 9.2 (CVSS 4.0) / 8.1 (CVSS 3.1) per F5, with a working public proof of concept. No in-the-wild exploitation reported; not in KEV as of 23 November.

Apache Axis

End-of-life Axis 1.x exploited; no fixed release, fix is per product.

Unsafe reflection in end-of-life Axis 1.x (CVE-2026-90436), scored 9.8 by CISA and 9.3 by Atlassian; exploited 13 November against Confluence Data Center and N-central, Metasploit module on 17 November. There is no fixed Axis 1.x release; the fix is per product.

Supporting count: 20 flaws tracked: 13 exploited, 1 reportedly exploited, 3 with public proof of concept and no confirmed exploitation, 3 with no exploitation reported or probing only. Nine entered KEV (1 on 17 November, 4 on 18 November, 4 on 20 November); catalogue v2026.11.20, 1,790 entries.

Theme C — AI-accelerated fraud and social engineering

Bypass human and MFA controls: voice cloning, adversary-in-the-middle, autonomous agents.

AI-voice vishing arrests

AI-voice calls impersonating IT support: 28 companies, over $40.5 million paid.

Employees were tricked into disclosing credentials or installing TeamViewer/AnyDesk; at least 28 companies paid over $40.5 million. The wider network is 27+ members, 60+ people across eight jurisdictions; four arrested 18–19 November. Named victims in court records: Warren Oil ($5m paid after a $10m demand), Newpark Resources ($1.5m), Quantum Health ($5m after a $15m demand). No group name published; no MITRE mapping published.

Com-affiliated vishing

Vishing waves accelerated from August 2026; passwords and MFA approvals captured live.

Com-affiliated actors run vishing waves in which the caller, posing as internal IT, steers the employee to an adversary-in-the-middle proxy capturing password and MFA approval in real time, then enrols their own MFA device (Vortex: within minutes). Exfiltration covers mailbox, OneDrive and SharePoint; targets are data-rich industries including financial services (investment portfolios, deal data), primarily US, UK and Australia. No victim names or counts published.

India fraud at scale

Loan-diversion and payout-API fraud at crore scale; NBFC figures do not reconcile.

PayU payout-API manipulation: ₹25.67 crore (about US$2.9m) via 265 unauthorised transactions to 156 accounts; two arrested 15 November; access method still under audit. NBFC loan-diversion fraud: Delhi Police report 12 NBFCs in Delhi-NCR lost over ₹65 crore in three months, diverting approved-but-undisbursed loans; one lender lost ₹41.27 crore in about 90 minutes on 16 November; "traces of AI" reported; CyberStrikeAI used in at least one October case. A wider figure of about ₹105.67 crore across 14 NBFCs is unreconciled — neither figure is used as the loss total. Separately, Delhi Police arrested 22 people in a fake-banking-app crackdown: 22,139 victims, proceeds of more than ₹20 crore.

Infostealer feedstock

Compromised machines roughly tripled; session-cookie theft outlasts password resets.

Hudson Rock weekly report, 9–16 November: 37,241 compromised machines (prior week 12,382 — roughly tripled), 8,382 compromised employees, 6,715 compromised users, 22,144 Android devices, 406,864 domains; top families Generic Stealer 29,138 and Lumma 7,232. A new log shop sells AI-written profiles of infected owners (job, employer, software, most valuable accounts); machines listed at $9–$10, operator claims tens of millions of logs — unverified.

Business-model shift (v3-supported): Visa's Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report (17 November, covering January–June 2026): AI is not creating new fraud categories — it makes existing attacks faster, larger and more precise, increasingly targeting third-party providers. Incidents involving third-party agents rose 62% versus the prior six months; third parties' share of compromised payment credentials is about 56%; recovered payment credentials from breaches rose 1,200%. Agentic tooling also produced named victims via ARTEX/BaaRai and Cairn.

Theme D — Data-extortion ransomware

Extort by publication via credential-only access plus a leak site. No encryption needed.

n0n publications

n0n published three brands' customer data on 19 November, after its deadline.

JPMorgan Chase: about 16,000 invitation-only Reserve programme customer email addresses plus 124 employee emails (confirmed; third-party providers targeted; no card numbers, passwords or balances in published sets). Visa: over 6,000 premium Asia-Pacific customers' details (confirmed; Asia-Pacific third-party provider; no payment data). Mastercard: about 2,000 entries in a 280 KB file (publication confirmed; the set looks like test data, not genuine customer records). Also: Samsung, 220 MB including about 150,000 smart-TV viewing events (publication confirmed, scope unconfirmed), and Alo Yoga, 209,051 customer emails (published 14 November). n0n first seen 18 September 2026; initial access credential-only (infostealer credentials, FortiGate SSL-VPN stuffing, weak RDP/WinRM, exposed AWS keys). No vulnerability exploitation documented; no geographic attribution. Bitdefender caveat: 100+ listed versus just over 20 real targets.

Business-model shift (v3-supported): the move in ransomware-as-a-service toward pure data extortion is evidenced by publication without encryption. The weekly report's TTP section states Impact has no published MITRE technique this week despite n0n's publications and the ransomware arrests: "Impact here was publication and extortion, not encryption". Ransom payments stand at zero transactions across all 55 collected ransom wallets as of 16 October 2026 — no in-window update, so not presented as current to 23 November.

Reading the landscape

Insight 1 — Agentic AI is now operating, not demonstrating: The evidence is no longer about what autonomous agents might do. ARTEX produced named victims: at Barracuda, an unretired cloud key issued to an outside AI vendor for a finished project let an intruder move through about ten cloud accounts in about 44 hours, and at Bitrise, a dormant test bot account, unused since 2022 and without two-factor authentication, gave agents cloud keys in under eleven minutes. Cairn agents, given only the instruction to obtain shell access, mapped a MONAI Label flaw to arbitrary file write in about eleven minutes and then reached root. In India, CyberStrikeAI was used in at least one October loan-diversion case. The change is speed and scale at the credential stage, not a new exploit class: the agents chained secrets that were already valid, already issued, or already left behind.

Insight 2 — The failure was exposed credentials, not novel malware: Across the week's largest incidents, the break was usually a credential or key held by a supplier. Plaid KARTE exposed integration credentials for 754 client companies; BankWeaver needed no vulnerability at all, exchanging an anonymous token for an administrator token on a misconfigured, internet-reachable Temenos service; Wellington IT was reached with valid credentials that made about five million requests for payee data; and the Paydon contractor breach leaked 251,172 records including passwords and bank account information, halting services at four banks. The link between n0n's Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard publications and Qubole remains reported, not confirmed. The pattern is concentration: one trusted credential opens many firms at once, so revoking and rotating supplier keys matters more than hunting for a new malware family.

Insight 3 — Regulators are moving from guidance to inspection and deadlines: Korea's FSS summoned 274 firms — 227 electronic-finance and 47 online investment-linked firms — for an intensive external-attack-surface inspection, with self-assessments due 30 November and a warning of strict accountability after a poorly done inspection. The UK designated five more critical third parties, taking the total to nine, and the Bank of England, PRA and FCA opened a joint cyber-resilience consultation closing 26 February 2027. In India, the Supreme Court is driving RBI KYC Amendment Directions to be notified by 30 November and operationalised by 31 December, with banks to compensate victims in full where due diligence fails — as summarised in secondary reporting, with no primary order text found. Supervision is now testing whether firms can prove the controls, not merely describe them.

8 Third-party and supply chain

Five findings, with concentration risk spanning Asia and Europe.

KARTE

KARTE: the systemic concentration story.

One marketing platform: attack on 13 November via a vulnerability behind a public data-retrieval route, then stolen credentials to the admin console. Data from about 2,200 companies leaked or may have leaked — 754 integration credentials, 570 companies' linked-in data, 487 companies' behaviour data, up to 409 companies' cached data (estimate); published counts passed 140 million records (a tally, not a headcount). Former clients hit too: Stripe International data retained after ending KARTE in May 2026; Overflow data retained about 3 years after the contract ended in November 2023. Plaid forecasts a ¥2 billion loss; platform offline until a staged restart targeted for mid-January 2027.

One vendor, many banks

One vendor, many banks: a repeated pattern.

BankWeaver: one misconfigured Temenos Quantum Fabric auth service — one confirmed financial institution compromised, six further banks sharing the exposure. Wellington IT: CU Online runs mobile banking for more than a third of Ireland's credit unions; about 40 credit unions affected (press: 43); attack 2–4 October, about 5m requests with valid credentials, disclosed six weeks late. Trusted scripts

Trusted scripts and payment processors: the cascade path.

Assessed, not confirmed; the Qubole link is reported, not confirmed. Wiz documented the underlying Qubole cross-account campaign of 14–26 October.

11 Outlook and intelligence gaps

What to expect

From 24 November, per-company handovers begin for the Plaid KARTE breach, in which integration credentials were exposed for 754 client companies and no key misuse had been disclosed as of 20 November; further disclosures are likely as handovers proceed. In Korea, self-assessments from 274 firms fall due on 30 November, and intrusion attempts through external contact points continued, above all at smaller operators, so further peripheral-system breaches by then remain likely.

For vCenter, the picture has already moved: a first public proof of concept with real code was reported on 23 November, after this report's 23 November 09:00 snapshot — the dinosn repository, whose contents were checked but not run.
