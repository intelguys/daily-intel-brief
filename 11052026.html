Executive summary

1

Two named banks, one lesson: the route in was a supplier or provider system, not a customer's password alone. Bank SinoPac confirmed on 4 November that external hackers exploited security settings at the foreign supplier of its new online banking transaction system, making 427 unauthorised transfers totalling about NT$41.76 million (about US$1.27 million) on 3 November; customers were reimbursed on 3 November and the receiving account at Taipei Fubon Bank was frozen. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, in its evening statement on 4 November, gave a provisional estimate of about €100 million, said attackers reached the bank directly through the platform of one of its service providers — not through client accounts and, in its Q&A, not through Fortuneo — used the bank's own account to transfer funds to other banks, and converted funds into cryptocurrency. In both cases the exact flaw, the provider's name (Arkéa) or supplier's name (SinoPac), and the attackers' identity are undisclosed, so do not fill those gaps from press speculation.

2

The deadline cluster is now today and tomorrow, and the new KEV entry is an old Apple flaw with a live campaign behind it. CISA added CVE-2024-27834 (Apple WebKit pointer-authentication bypass, NVD 5.5) on 4 November, taking the catalogue to 1,768 entries, due 18 November. The timing matches Sekoia's Poisoned Source report the same day: APT29 (moderate-to-high confidence) used n-day chains — no zero-days — against phones that had missed updates, stealing cookies and, in the deepest chain, messages, credentials, location and photos. WSO2 CVE-2025-11969 falls due today, 5 November; WordPress CVE-2026-119977 falls due tomorrow, 6 November.

3

An edge zero-day has been reclassified, which changes who should still patch. Zyxel's GS1900 flaw CVE-2026-107441 — not the June flaw CVE-2026-7273 it was first blamed on — was the vulnerability actually exploited to compromise 996 switches in 48 countries from mid-August, stealing configurations and hashed root passwords. Firmware patched for the June flaw was still vulnerable, so a June patch is not evidence of safety; the October 22 firmware is the fix. Separately, SonicWall warned on 4 November that two flaws in end-of-life Global Management System software will not be patched at all — the only fix is migration to Network Security Manager.

Supply chain & third-party risk

SinoPac supplier & Arkéa provider — NEW

Both named-bank cases turn on a third party's system: SinoPac's new online banking transaction system from a foreign supplier, and a service provider's platform that reached Arkéa directly. Neither bank has named the third party or disclosed the flaw. The control lesson is the same in both: inventory which supplier or provider platforms can initiate or authorise transfers on the bank's own account or a customer's account, and test their verification and account-enumeration controls — not just the bank's customer-facing login.

Amadeus / Singapore Airlines — NEW, 4 Nov

Singapore Airlines said about 46,000 booking records may have been affected through Amadeus' travel booking display environment: names, booking references, itineraries and booking classes, plus email, phone, travel-document and KrisFlyer details for about 620 records. SIA says its own systems were not breached, no bookings were changed, and Amadeus confirmed no bank account or credit-card information was exposed. Other carriers (Cathay Pacific about 53,000 bookings, Japan Airlines about 92,000 customers in secondary reporting) have reported the same provider incident. Do not add carrier figures together — they count different record types. BFSI relevance is indirect: a provider display flaw that exposes identity details is phishing fuel, and the same pattern — a trusted vendor's system account or display environment — appears in the Cathay account of this incident.

NIKKE launcher supply chain — NEW, 4 Nov

Level Infinite confirmed that PC launcher script files for Goddess of Victory: NIKKE may have been tampered with by a third party; a client fix was issued 4 Nov at 02:30 UTC+9. Independent researcher analysis cited by BleepingComputer says four JavaScript files on the official CDN were modified on 27 Sep to load code from attacker domains, installing a remote-access agent and a DLL that can steal cryptocurrency wallet data from browser extensions (MetaMask, Phantom, Coinbase Wallet and others). Scope of impact is still under investigation by the publisher. No bank victim — relevance is wallet-credential theft via a trusted game launcher update channel.

DreamHost & mSecure — credential exposure, 4 Nov

DreamHost confirmed a breach of its web File Manager from August to 23 Oct, exposing FTP/SFTP usernames and passwords of users who logged in during that period and possibly webmail session information for a limited number of users; account counts are unanswered. mSeven warned mSecure customers to change passwords, reset 2FA and remove passkeys after suspicious activity; customer-shared support replies cited by BleepingComputer say an encrypted customer database was stolen from mSecure Cloud, which mSeven has not confirmed in sources read. Neither names a BFSI victim; both are credential-reuse risks for staff and customers who reuse passwords.

Emerging fraud & attack vectors

SinoPac unauthorised transfers — the fraud pattern itself

Transfers were made without customers logging in, in many cases for a fixed NT$100,000, to one destination account. Customers reported face-scan confirmations they did not make, per customer posts cited in Chinese-language reporting (translated from Chinese) — that detail is customer-reported, not bank-confirmed, and the bank's explanation is supplier security settings exploited by trial-and-error, not a customer device compromise. The distinction matters for customer advice: do not blame customers for clicking a link when the bank says the route was a supplier system.

Festive-season fake offers targeting an unnamed Indian bank — reported, 4 Nov

Brand-protection firm mFilterIt reported nearly 200,000 fake festive offers, cloned sites and phishing links across platforms, including nearly 240,000 fraudulent cases targeting a single leading Indian bank across 10 platforms (fake promotional sites, phishing pages, brand impersonation, coupon abuse). The bank is unnamed in sources read, so this is a vendor-reported, sector-relevant pattern, not a confirmed incident at a named bank. The firm says AI lowers the cost, skill and language barriers to producing fake creatives at scale — a vendor assessment, not a named AI campaign.

Exchange restoration & laundering phishing — ongoing

Arkéa, Binance and Mercado Bitcoin stories are all live phishing and laundering bait: Arkéa funds were converted via specialist crypto platforms, with Kraken reportedly alerting after unusually large transfers; Binance and Mercado Bitcoin restoration messages are being mimicked. Verify via official status pages only, never via links in messages claiming to restore withdrawals or refunds.

Abrigo / Sageworks — status unchanged

No new Abrigo statement was found in-window. Borrower-facing Online Portal Now remained unavailable in 3 Nov reporting, and the breach-or-not attestation (scope, data types) is still outstanding. Carried as an open third-party item, not a new incident.

Do not present the absence of a new report as evidence that key theft has stopped.

AI as a weapon / accelerator — scale, not a named campaign

No new named AI-scaled phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a BFSI victim was found in this window. The closest in-window item is mFilterIt's festive-season finding (see Fraud): AI is said to lower the cost, skill and language barriers to producing fake offers and cloned sites at scale, including cases targeting an unnamed Indian bank. AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI — agent detection announced

CrowdStrike announced at Fal.Con Europe on 4 Nov that Agent Insider Threat Detection will be added to Falcon Next-Gen SIEM in Q4, learning normal behaviour for each AI agent and its owner and flagging deviations even without a policy violation, alongside generally available coordinated multi-agent investigations and the Falcon Guardian AI Kill Switch. Treat the roadmap honestly: the insider-threat detection is forthcoming, not generally available today, and a later Policy-Drift Agent Guardrail is scheduled for Q1 fiscal 2028. The standing controls still apply regardless of vendor: key inventory and rotation, endpoint exposure (no unauthenticated Ollama/LiteLLM on the internet), spend and invocation monitoring with revoke-and-rotate, and agent-tool detection (rapid, tool-driven sequences from a single session).

No new AI-scaled phishing, vishing or deepfake item in this window

No newly reported AI-generated phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a BFSI victim was found for 4–5 November. That is absence of reporting, not evidence it has stopped. The SinoPac, Arkéa and festive-fraud items in this edition are conventional or scale-based impersonation and are not re-labelled as AI campaigns without evidence.

Indicators & defensive actions

Notable IOCs / behavioural indicators

  • SinoPac: non-designated transfers of about NT$100,000, mostly, to a single Taipei Fubon Bank account, without a customer login; accounts sharing an ID-number prefix; a sudden cluster of transfers to one new destination is the detection signal, not a single transfer.
  • Arkéa (investigator analytics, not bank-confirmed): USDC/USDT withdrawals from Kraken, Crypto.com and OKX accounts over 1–2 Nov (Bitquery: $108.8 million); conversion of $62.98 million in stablecoins to 27,553 ETH in about four hours via UniswapX (Bitquery).
  • APT29 Poisoned Source: iOS 16.7 and 18.5, Chrome 139–145 except 143; Plerdy tracker script tampered 27 Aug–3 Sep (a.plerdy.com/public/js/click/main.js); payloads stop on reboot but stolen cookies/sessions persist until revoked.
  • Zyxel: GS1900 switches on pre-22-Oct firmware (including June-patched builds); factory default credentials (564 of 996 compromised switches); configuration and hashed root password exfiltration.
  • Metabase: Metabase 58+ on versions below 58.24 / 59.21 / 60.17 / 61.11 / 62.9 / 63.5, especially internet-reachable; unauthenticated SQL injection to admin.
  • WordPress (carried): requests for staged installer paths under wp-content/upgrade/ around a core upgrade window.
  • Binance / Mercado Bitcoin (carried): restoration and refund messages with links, WhatsApp or phone calls are phishing — verify via official status pages only.
