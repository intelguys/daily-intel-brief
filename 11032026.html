Executive summary

1

Mercado Bitcoin and Binance are the most serious items. They are two separate incidents. Mercado Bitcoin, Brazil's largest exchange, was drained across 17 blockchains in the early hours of 2 Nov. About $10m was frozen. No attacker was named, and how the attacker got in is unknown. Critique: the ~$49m total is a researcher's estimate. The exchange's own figure is about $38m. They measure different things and must not be added together. The exchange's promise of no customer loss is its own claim, not independent proof. Separately, Binance lost about $6.3bn. The FBI blames North Korea's TraderTraitor/Lazarus cluster. North Korea denied this on 3 Nov. Net outflows were $3.64bn. Fake “$150 compensation” scams are using the incident to target customers.

2

Ivanti is the top patch-and-hunt priority. The federal deadline for CVE-2026-111357 passed on 2 Nov. The second deadline, for CVE-2026-118527, is 13 Nov. Siemens CVE-2026-110936 is due today, 3 Nov. Critique: a passed deadline does not mean every system is safe. Patching alone does not answer whether a system was already used. Activity held only in memory may not be seen by Ivanti's Integrity Checker. A clean check is therefore not proof that a system was never touched. The risk is direct: these flaws can give an attacker a foothold, then access to identity and credential stores.

3

Vendor risk is the second theme. Abrigo restored Sageworks on 2 Nov. The platform had been shut down after “irregular activity”. More than 2,400 US banks and credit unions were affected. Borrower-facing features were still down. Critique: “irregular activity” does not answer the key question — was data taken, or not? The claim against AU Small Finance Bank, via a Salesforce-hosted platform, is also unverified. The bank says core systems are unaffected. CTOS restored credit-reporting services. In each case, the problem sits with a vendor, but the bank must still show what was, and was not, exposed.

No new FIN7, LockBit, RansomHub, Cl0p or bank-focused Initial Access Broker campaign was found in this window. That is a limit of what was published and searched. It is not evidence that those groups were inactive. “Confirmed exploitation” below means a CISA KEV listing, a vendor confirmation, or a named incident-response account. It does not mean a leak-site claim or an aggregator's assertion. A leak-site listing date is not a compromise date.

TraderTraitor / Lazarus Group (North Korea)

Crypto exchange · Global — Binance (Binance.com; US platform said unaffected)

TTPs
Access may have started with a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop. It then reached a service container, and unauthorised withdrawals followed. This is an analyst mapping (T1078 Valid Accounts / T1566 Phishing). Binance has not finally confirmed how access began. It later said “additional points of breach” were found. Funds were converted mostly to ETH, then moved through DeFi and instant-swap services (T1020 / T1496-adjacent, per FBI via Help Net Security). The FBI listed 628 addresses to block: 608 ETH, 19 BTC, 1 SOL.
Impact
About $6.3bn was stolen on 29 Oct. Reporting calls it the largest crypto theft on record. About $3bn was frozen on the first day (Tether/Circle et al.). Binance says about 95% of stolen assets were replaced from its treasury, futures insurance and emergency reserves. Net outflow was $3.64bn in 9.5 hours on 1 Nov ($4.649bn out / $1.01bn in; 2.1% of $171bn AUM, per Binance). On 3 Nov, North Korea denied involvement via Kim Yo-jong (“absurd rhetoric” / “trite provocation”). Critique: a denial does not disprove the FBI attribution. The attribution does not make it certain either. The full evidence is not public. The outflow and replacement figures also come from Binance itself. They are useful, but they are not audited.

UTA0576 — Ivanti Connect Secure chain

Edge/VPN users, incl. financial firms on Ivanti Connect Secure · Global

TTPs
Three flaws are chained to reach unauthenticated root: CVE-2026-104935 (auth bypass / RCE), CVE-2026-118527 (predictable upload) and CVE-2026-111357 (privilege escalation). Ivanti says the impact is “limited to a small number of customers” (via HOL). Critique: that phrase is the vendor's. It does not name the customers or the data taken. CISA lists ransomware use as unknown. No public exploit code is cited in the advisories. The risk is a foothold plus theft from identity stores. There is no confirmed BFSI victim in this window.

Supply chain & third-party risk

The problem is often at the vendor, not the bank. A service coming back is not the same as an answer on cause or exposure.

Abrigo / Sageworks — restored, unconfirmed

Sageworks was taken offline on 30 Oct after “irregular activity”. It was partly restored on 2 Nov (reported 2 Nov 14:00 ET by BleepingComputer). More than 2,400 US banks and credit unions were affected. Borrower-facing features were still down. Critique: Abrigo called this “irregular activity”, not a breach. That wording leaves the main question open: was data taken, or not? There is no breach confirmation for the outage. No link has been shown to the earlier ShinyHunters claim of 1.7m Salesforce records taken from Abrigo. Researchers advise rotating Sageworks API keys as a precaution.

AU Small Finance Bank — claim still unverified

The bank said on 2 Nov that it is investigating a possible leak on a Salesforce-hosted SaaS platform. It says core systems are unaffected and services are normal. Salesforce said it found no flaw at its end. Critique: “core systems are unaffected” does not rule out a leak from the hosted platform. The ScarlettGroup claim — KYC, PAN/Aadhaar, bank statements, CIBIL reports, loan agreements — remains unconfirmed. So do the customer count and whether the Salesforce platform is the source.

CTOS — services restored

CTOS restored credit-reporting services after a cybersecurity breach. This was reported on 2 Nov (BursaKL). Critique: the report gives no detail on what was exposed. Restoration alone does not answer that question.

Spandana Sphoorty — status update only

Business Standard (2 Nov 13:31 IST) adds detail. The ~₹7.8 crore in unauthorised transfers on 1 Nov went to 82 beneficiary accounts. Services were stopped. A complaint was lodged on 1 Nov. A cyber-fraud insurance claim is being filed. Critique: a claim being filed is not a claim accepted or paid. It should not be read as “no loss”. No recovery, reversal, or freeze was confirmed in this window. This item was in the Monday briefing. It is not new.

Emerging fraud & attack vectors

Fake Binance “$150 compensation” scams

Fake “$150 compensation” screenshots are circulating. So are “check your reward hub” and claim links. They use the Binance incident as a lure. Binance states no cash-voucher campaign is running. Proposed community rewards were still waiting for regulatory approval at 1 Nov. Critique: the scam's claim is false on Binance's own statement. Warn customers through official channels only. Do not repeat the claim links.

Mule-account laundering — Assam, India

Six arrests were made on 28–30 Oct and reported on 2 Nov. The network operated across states for about 2 years. It included an SBI account. Money was credited, then withdrawn quickly, over 47 days (22 Jun – 5 Aug 2026). “Several crores” were allegedly siphoned. Critique: these are police claims at arrest stage. The suspected Pakistan-intelligence link is under probe only.

DDoS — multi-day attacks are the planning case

Qrator reporting (2 Nov) describes a Cyprus bank hit by a 200-hour DDoS attack in Q3 2026. Fintech accounted for 31.3% of Q3 DDoS. Critique: these are one vendor's figures for one quarter. They are a reason to plan for multi-day attacks, not a measure of any one bank's risk. The practical point stands: test multi-day DDoS plans and keep DORA-aligned resilience evidence. Do not plan for a single day only.

CyberDakait mobile trojans — context, not new

A 3 Nov Netcrook republication is recirculating an older Group-IB report, dated 28 Oct. It lists 7 families (GhostBat/GhostGrab, cyb3rx, appkjl, godz, d-passive, d-shine, tenxen), 17,960 devices, 7,611,443 SMS intercepted, 11 Indian banks and 2 government programmes impersonated, APKs via phishing and GitHub, and Firebase/Telegram C2. Critique: republication is not new activity. This is not a new 24-hour item. It is included only because it is recirculating.

Indicators & defensive actions

Only indicators the sources actually published are listed. Where no technical indicators were published, that gap is stated. It is not filled in.

Notable IOCs & behavioural indicators

  • Binance / TraderTraitor (FBI, 31 Oct, republished 2 Nov): 628 wallet addresses to block — 608 Ethereum, 19 Bitcoin, 1 Solana. Stolen funds were converted mostly to ETH. Onward laundering via DeFi and instant-swap services is expected. Screen deposits, withdrawals and any counterparty that touches these addresses. Note: a freeze is not a return of funds.
  • Mercado Bitcoin: no wallet addresses, hashes, or domains were published in the Chainalysis blog excerpt found. The only indicator is behaviour: a multi-chain drain “in waves”, largest in BTC/ETH, then Base/Tron/Polygon. Critique: without addresses, there is nothing to block from this source. The useful window is the first hours, when stablecoin issuers can still freeze funds. Prioritise issuer and law-enforcement escalation over address lists that were not published.
  • Ivanti chain: hunt for wu.cgi uploads in /home/runtime/tmp/ under MD5-named files, Perl execution racing deletion, dsinvoked config-trust abuse, AD queries for LAPS passwords and BitLocker recovery keys, and any Connect Secure appliance still on ≤25.1.1.2 / ≤22.8R2.5 (or 25.1.2.2 per VECTR-CAST). A clean Integrity Checker result does not clear an appliance.
  • Akira / Pear / Settra: no hashes, IPs, or domains were in the leak-tracker sources found. The only indicators are the leak claims themselves (130 GB pending for Princeton; wire amounts and trial-balance totals for Kingdom Title). Use them to scope exposure — were those records in the claimed set? Do not treat them as technical indicators.
  • Fraud: fake Binance “$150 compensation” screenshots and “check your reward hub” / claim links. Assam mule pattern: money is credited, then withdrawn quickly, in the same account, over weeks.
  • Sageworks / Salesforce third-party: no indicators were published. The only signal is behaviour: a vendor “irregular activity” shutdown (30 Oct), a partial restoration (2 Nov), and no breach confirmation. Use vendor status pages and API-key rotation as the control. Do not wait for a vendor indicator feed that does not exist here.

Prioritised actions — SOC

  1. Block or screen the FBI's 628 TraderTraitor addresses. Alert on any ETH-conversion or DeFi-swap pattern that touches Binance- or Mercado-related flows. Warn customers about the fake-$150 scam through official channels only. Do not repeat the claim links.
  2. Hunt the Ivanti and Siemens estates today. Confirm every Connect Secure node is on 25.1.1.3 / 22.8R2.6. Also complete forensic triage for CVE-2026-111357. The deadline has passed, and patching alone does not close the question. Confirm Siemens Software Center ≥ V4.0.9.8 (due today). Check ClickFix and DLL-sideloading detections.
  3. Treat Abrigo/Sageworks and Salesforce-hosted loan platforms as exposed until the vendor proves otherwise. Rotate Sageworks API keys. Review vendor-access logs and the data shared with the platform. Ask Abrigo and Salesforce for written scope: which fields, which customers, and whether data was taken — yes or no. Do not close the item on “services restored”.
  4. Raise helpdesk and identity controls. This is the Scattered-Spider-style pattern. No new incident is needed to justify it. Check self-service password resets for executives, Azure Global Admin grants, Veeam service-account resets, and lookalike domains that imitate the bank.
  5. Test DDoS readiness for more than one day. The Cyprus bank faced a 200-hour attack in Q3. Fintech took 31.3% of Q3 DDoS (Qrator). Keep tested, multi-day plans and DORA-aligned resilience evidence.

Prioritised actions — Vulnerability Management

  1. Today: Ivanti 25.1.1.3 / 22.8R2.6, plus forensic triage. Siemens ≥ V4.0.9.8. GitLab to 19.2.8 / 19.3.5 / 19.4.2. Also check custom container images. They can drop required patches, as in the Alpine Linux case.
  2. This week: Cisco ASA/FTD for CVE-2026-76418 (DoS only, per reporting — do not treat it as RCE). Apache Fineract to 1.16.1 (both SQLi CVEs) and Syncope upgrades. Android fleet to the 2026-11-05 patch level (or the latest OEM build, plus Play system updates for Media and Codecs).
  3. Verify before emergency change: VMware CVE-2026-41709. Check CISA KEV and Broadcom VMSA-2026-0006.1 first. Do not cite the single-source claim as fact.
  4. Watch. Do not yet close: Binance's full ZK proof-of-reserves (due 3 Nov UTC). Re-check after the UTC day ends. Check that it covers balances after the 1 Nov withdrawals, not only the 31 Oct snapshot. Also watch: Abrigo's root-cause statement, the AU SFB investigation scope, and Mercado Bitcoin's reopening and final loss figure (Chainalysis ~$49m vs the exchange's ~$38m — still unreconciled).
