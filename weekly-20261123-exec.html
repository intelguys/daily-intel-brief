Bottom line

Supplier failure defined the week: one compromise at a marketing platform, a middleware vendor, or a credit-union provider became dozens of firms' incidents. AI moved from research into confirmed operations: agents exploited a zero-day alone, cloned voices opened a ransomware gang's way in, and agentic tooling produced named victims. The most dangerous vulnerability news was old: a vCenter zero-day exploited since September, and 13 of the 20 tracked flaws were already exploited. Regulators turned incidents into duties, so for BFSI the perimeter is now the supplier's: rotate vendor keys, verify help-desk calls, and patch exploited flaws first.

5 key developments

  1. Supplier failure was the week's spine. One compromise became dozens of firms' incidents: KARTE's 754 leaked keys, BankWeaver, Wellington IT, Paydon, and a reported Qubole link. Confidence: High
  2. AI agents found and exploited a zero-day on their own. Cairn agents mapped a MONAI Label flaw to arbitrary file write in about 11 minutes, then reached root. Confidence: High
  3. AI-voice vishing is now a confirmed ransomware entry point. Police and the FBI arrested four suspected members of a group behind at least 28 companies paying over $40.5 million. Confidence: High
  4. A vCenter zero-day was exploited since early September. A suspected state-linked actor, ARMORY CASTLE, used it; Broadcom patched on 19 November and CISA listed it with a 23 November due date. Confidence: High
  5. Ransomware moved from claims to publications. n0n published Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard customer data on 19 November after its deadline expired. Deadlines stopped predicting publication: the retail batch, Vortex/Carrington and Pixelleaks/Ekwiik deadlines all passed quietly. Confidence: High

For the board

  1. Which of our suppliers could turn one compromise into our incident — and have we rotated every KARTE credential and checked for misuse?
  2. Are exploited flaws patched first — are the four KEV items due 23 November closed, and was vCenter hunted as well as patched?
  3. Could a cloned voice pass our help-desk or finance checks — what stops AI-voice vishing, now a confirmed ransomware entry point, from opening our way in?
