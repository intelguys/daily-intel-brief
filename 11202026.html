n0n — Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard customer data published after deadline; all three confirm third-party vendor incidents

Global card networks and the largest US bank; premium-cardmember marketing data held by third-party vendors

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published for the n0n listings. The Qubole campaign initial access, as published by Wiz (9–11 Nov), used IAM credentials from Qubole’s own AWS account to assume customer integration roles and steal secrets and S3 data — MITRE T1078.004 (Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts) is already recorded as confirmed on the n0n profile page; the link between that campaign and n0n is unverified.
Impact
Update — new in-window, the lead item. After the 48-hour deadline set on 16 November expired, n0n published datasets it attributes to all three firms on 19 November. Cybernews researchers examined the files: the JPMorgan set is about 502 KB covering roughly 16,000 customer email addresses tied to the invitation-only Reserve card programme, with phone numbers in some cases, JPMorgan account IDs and 124 employee email addresses; the Visa set covers over 6,000 premium customers in Asia-Pacific, with names, personal email addresses, mobile numbers and premium travel and dining bookings, likely from a marketing platform tracking Visa premium dining programmes; the alleged Mastercard set is about 280 KB with roughly 2,000 entries, poorly formatted, with records for multiple individuals mixed into single entries — Cybernews says it is likely test data rather than genuine customer information. No card numbers, passwords or balances are reported in any of the files, and Reuters could not independently verify the authenticity of the data. JPMorgan confirmed on 19 November, via a spokesperson to Reuters, that third parties providing services to the firm were targeted and attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include some customer names and email addresses; the bank said its services were not disrupted, no sensitive financial or payment data was affected, it will contact affected customers directly and offer complimentary credit monitoring, and the incident is unrelated to the service disruption on 16 November, which it attributes to a software bug. Visa confirmed to Cybernews that the incident originated with a third-party service provider in Asia-Pacific, that Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted, and that there is no indication payment data such as card numbers was affected; Visa has not named the provider. Mastercard confirmed on 19 November (statement added to the Cybernews report at 16:16 UTC, 00:16 SGT 20 November) that it was recently informed of an incident at a third-party provider that resulted in the exposure of a limited amount of information from a subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit programme, that there is no impact to the Mastercard payment network or to people’s ability to use their cards, and that the exposure has been contained. No vendor has been named by any of the three firms, and whether the three datasets came from one shared marketing platform is not established — Cybernews notes the leaked records from the group’s several ransom campaigns appear to originate from a single third-party source, but that is not independently established. Fraud teams should warn premium clients about impersonation using the leaked names, emails, phone numbers and booking details, and watch for staff-targeted phishing using the exposed employee addresses.

Unknown actor — “BankWeaver”: bank digital-banking middleware turned into a backdoor; six more banks share the exposure

A financial institution running Temenos Quantum Fabric; six further banks in Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the Americas share the configuration

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published. Forescout Research–Vedere Labs reports attackers abused a misconfigured Temenos Quantum Fabric (formerly Kony Fabric) deployment: an authentication service issuing privileged tokens was set to accept anonymous access and was internet-reachable; attackers captured the anonymous token and exchanged it for an admin token without valid credentials, then uploaded malicious Java components — FabricShell, a web shell running operating-system commands and database queries, and FabricShadow, a reconnaissance hook. The same reporting links the actor to Solana-blockchain-memo command-and-control, the technique Cisco Talos described for UAT-12643 on 18 November — that link is the secondary report’s, not Forescout’s published wording as read here.
Impact
Reported — Forescout disclosure, 19 November, as carried by secondary reporting; the Forescout primary advisory URL was not retrieved verbatim in this run, so treat the detail as reported rather than primary-confirmed. The investigated breach ran from early September. Six additional banks across Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the Americas share the same configuration and exposure — Forescout stresses their compromise is not confirmed. Temenos attributed the behaviour to the anonymous-access configuration, released updates and guidance, and advised restricting internet access to admin and authentication services. Any bank running Quantum Fabric should check whether its authentication service accepts anonymous access and whether it is internet-reachable, and treat a positive answer as an active exposure, not a theoretical one.

Unknown actor — PayU Payments cloud servers breached; ₹25.67 crore siphoned via manipulated payout API, two arrested

PayU Payments Private Limited (India); payout API and cloud servers

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published. Attackers gained access to three cloud servers, manipulated the company’s payout API and carried out 265 unauthorised transactions involving 156 bank accounts. How access was obtained is still under audit and has not been established.
Impact
Confirmed (MediaNama, 19 Nov; PTI / Hindustan Times). Gurugram Cyber Police arrested two men in Ludhiana on 15 November over the alleged attack, following a complaint filed by PayU on 9 November. PayU’s security team detected unusual API activity around 23:30 on 5 November, after the transfers had taken place; the company’s cloud service provider had also flagged suspicious access. Police have frozen around ₹1.5 crore of the stolen funds. The two arrested men allegedly supplied bank accounts used to receive the stolen money. The incident follows a CERT-In and CSIRT-Fin advisory of 9 October warning financial institutions about attacks targeting payment and disbursement APIs, though investigators have not established whether the attack involved the specific vulnerabilities in that warning. The pattern is the same one as the NBFC loan-diversion frauds: a disbursement API, once reached, moves money without needing to break customer authentication.

Gurugram Cyber Police arrested two men in Ludhiana on 15 November over an alleged attack on PayU Payments’ cloud servers, following a complaint filed by PayU on 9 November. The operational point is the same as the NBFC loan-diversion pattern: a disbursement API is a standing instruction to move money, and an attacker who reaches it does not need to break customer authentication.

AI in the current threat landscape

AI as a target, AI as a weapon or accelerator, and AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI.

AI as a target

Microsoft agent-framework PyPI hijack — Wiz ties it to a compromised release-pipeline credential (update, 19 Nov). Wiz assesses that a client certificate and private key for Microsoft’s Engineering System Release Pipeline were compromised to publish the malicious agent-framework versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2. The stealer harvested environment variables and credential files, including LLM API keys and Hugging Face tokens, from AI-agent development environments, and requested fresh identity tokens from GitHub Actions, Azure DevOps and Azure. The tooling identified itself as “Cairn”, an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. Only three victim exfiltrations were observed in the sample Wiz could read — not a verified victim total. Microsoft has still published no security advisory.

LiteLLM KEV attribution is contested (in-window coverage, 19 Nov). Google Threat Intelligence Group attributes an LLMjacking intrusion — attackers who bought ordinary LiteLLM virtual keys escalating them to proxy-admin credentials — to CVE-2026-40217 in its 2 November report and 16 November AI Threat Tracker; Corgea disputes the attribution, saying the key-escalation technique is a separate bug fixed in 1.84.0. The attribution is contested; do not present it as settled. The flaw itself (CVSS 4.0 5.9) was added to CISA KEV on 18 November and is covered in the watchlist.

No new exposed-LLM-endpoint or LLM/API-key theft incident was found in this window.

AI as a weapon or accelerator

European Commission confirms an AI-generated cyber campaign (confirmed, 19 Nov). The Commission confirmed it responded to a detected AI-generated campaign — misuse of a publicly available AI tool — detected on 22 October and confirmed after initial investigation on 26 October. Several applications were taken down temporarily as a precaution; most have been restored. No attribution, no ransom demand, and no tool, entry point or affected application was named. A report that CERT-EU identified ARTEX use is unconfirmed by the Commission. AP called it the first time the EU has publicly reported a cyberattack using an AI tool. This is an observed AI-tool attack disclosure, not a forecast.

No new AI-scaled phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a named bank was found in this window. The Coinbase deepfake-Elon-Musk livestream item (AI defences stopped about $4.7 million in transfers) was covered in the 19 November edition; no new development appeared in-window. A 19 November analysis piece calls autonomous offensive frameworks “the third wave of AI-driven attacks” and recaps ARTEX, PentAGI and Cairn intrusions — that is analysis and opinion, not a new incident, and its forecast of stealthier self-hosted operations within three to six months is a forecast, not observed activity.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

Singapore MAS warns against giving AI agents bank login credentials (confirmed, 19 Nov). MAS cautioned against giving AI agents login credentials to personal bank accounts and said Singapore is developing a common protocol so banks can verify AI agents before allowing them to transact on customers’ behalf, defining what agents are authorised to do and their limits. Context: the CSA / GovTech / IMDA joint advisory AD-2026-015 (6 November) on personal AI agents and one-time passwords remains the standing guidance.

UK regulators consult on cyber resilience, with AI and quantum effective practices (confirmed, 19 Nov). The Bank of England, the Financial Conduct Authority and the Prudential Regulation Authority published a joint consultation on cyber risk and resilience management for financial firms, open until 26 February 2027, aligned with the NCSC’s Cyber Assessment Framework, plus separate effective practices on AI and quantum risks. Context: October 2026 saw the second-highest monthly number of cyber incidents reported to the FCA in five years, 52% above the previous 18-month peak in May 2025, and the Bank’s Systemic Risk Survey cites cyber as a top systemic risk.

Korea’s FSS asks firms for AI-industry exposure (reported, 19 Nov). South Korea’s Financial Supervisory Service asked major banks, securities firms and insurers to disclose direct and indirect AI-industry exposure (investments and loans); described as preventive, with no aggregate figure or deadline published. It is a regulatory signal, not an incident.

Singapore CIDeX exercise features AI-agent attack simulation (confirmed, 19 Nov). The Critical Infrastructure Defence Exercise (CIDeX), 17–20 November, features a coordinated “swarm” of AI agents simulating large-scale AI-enabled attacks against IT/OT testbeds, with more than 250 participants from 44 organisations, covering all 11 critical information infrastructure sectors including Banking & Finance. It is an exercise, not an incident — but it shows regulators now test defences against AI-agent attack swarms as a matter of routine.

Visa: AI is accelerating traditional fraud, not creating new categories (report released 17 Nov; covered in-window 19 Nov). Visa’s Fall 2026 Biannual Threats Report finds incidents involving third-party agents rose 62% against the prior six months, with third parties linked to about 56% of compromised payment credentials identified. The control problem is the same one this edition keeps returning to: the agent, the supplier and the platform are now part of the attack surface, and the fraud they enable looks like ordinary fraud, only faster.
