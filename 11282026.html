Heap buffer overflow in the kernel NFS server (nfsd), present since Linux 3.16 (2014): a specially crafted NFS request makes the server write past an allocated reply buffer, letting an unauthenticated remote actor who can reach TCP port 2049 read kernel memory, defeat KASLR and place a callback that executes a command with root privileges when the connection closes. No user interaction; low complexity; one TCP connection. Found independently twice: ANSSI’s Jérémy Jean (fix submitted August, merged into mainline 14 September) and OpenSec’s AI-powered testing harness (full remote-root exploit and patch shared with the NFSD maintainers in late September; OpenSec has kept its exploit private — as of 26 November there is no public exploit code). Detection (OpenSec): kernel warnings from nfsd4_check_resp_size, shells spawned by kernel workers on a file server, bursts of short NFSv4.1 sessions from one source, kernel oopses with nfsd4_*, svc_* or call_usermodehelper* frames. Moderate BFSI relevance — NAS appliances, build farms and Kubernetes storage nodes in bank estates; patch the kernel and reboot, restrict TCP 2049 to trusted clients, or disable nfsd where NFS sharing is not required. Kernel hardening does not remove the overflow.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

  • Dragos names MiniMax as the AI provider in its 19 Nov investigation (NEW in-window, 26 Nov). Dragos named MiniMax as the provider in its 19 November investigation (HD Moore on X; Reuters, 26 Nov): confidential links and test credentials sent to MiniMax via third-party providers were accessed even though Zero Data Retention had been requested. MiniMax said its security system stored the links and scanned them, then deleted them and stopped the activity. Control lesson: a third-party AI inference supply chain can see, retain and leak prompts and credentials despite a Zero Data Retention flag — the standing lesson from OpenRouter now has a second named case. Banks should treat anything sent to a third-party AI provider as potentially retained, and route confidential material only through providers with contractual retention controls and audit rights.
  • Google — Workspace AI-agent controls go GA; MCP/CLI mandatory from 1 February 2027 (carried, announced 24 Nov). From 1 February 2027, AI agents must connect to Workspace data through the MCP servers or CLI instead of direct REST API calls; agents that keep calling REST directly are throttled to Standard-tier quotas. Controls that matter for banks: dedicated agent OAuth client IDs (is_agentic flag); human-in-the-loop approval for sensitive actions; prompt-injection screening; a 30-day retention cap on derived data and a ban on using Workspace data for model training. The 1 February 2027 date is a genuine compliance clock for firms with agents on Workspace APIs.
  • No new MAS, FCA/BoE/PRA, EU or US AI-in-finance governance development appeared in-window. A Business Times opinion piece (Singapore banks not letting third-party AI agents in yet; agents could move deposits and amplify a bank run) is analysis, not a development — note only as context, not an item.

Third-party AI supply chains — OpenRouter and now MiniMax: confidential material sent to a third-party AI provider can be stored, scanned and accessed despite Zero Data Retention flags. Forecasts to mark as forecasts: Canada expects AI-enabled activity to become “more targeted, less visible and harder to detect”; Swiss NCSC expects more rapid, successful attacks; UK NCSC warns future frameworks may prioritise stealth over today’s noise.
