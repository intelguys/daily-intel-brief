Executive summary

1

Three unverified leak-site claims hit finance firms in a single day — and none is confirmed. A threat actor claims to have stolen 1.2 TB from India’s AU Small Finance Bank and lists it for sale at $8,500, directly contradicting the bank’s own account of a limited third-party leak. The MetaEncryptor ransomware group listed US insurer Pekin Insurance, and N0n published what it says are 217,000 documents from US insurance brokerage Higginbotham. Qilin claimed a finance firm and N0n listed two unnamed Canadian financial businesses the same day. South Korea’s Gopax exchange stayed frozen for a second day on an unverified hacking threat. Claims are multiplying faster than confirmations — treat every one as unproven until a victim or independent evidence confirms it.

2

Bank Rakyat’s defacement is now framed two ways, while Arkéa’s entry point is clarified and Mirae Asset’s fallout continues. Malaysia’s Communications Minister says the Bank Rakyat incident was a hack limited to defacement of a third-party-managed site with no data theft; the bank itself calls it a technical problem at a third-party provider. Neither addresses the actor’s claim of internal access. Arkéa was reached directly through a service provider’s platform — Fortuneo and human error are ruled out — and the bank says it has recovered part of the funds, without saying how much. In South Korea, the regulator has decided not to inspect two smaller firms (Zoomfund, Payhub) for now, will convene crypto-exchange CISOs on 19 November, and has extended its anti-phishing response period to the end of December.

3

Binance’s CFO steps down nine days after the $6.34 billion theft, as its proof of reserves is confirmed live — and the laundering picture is contested. Kaiser Ng leaves after more than five years, replaced by Justin Yek; Binance presents the handover as planned before the hack, not caused by it. Elliptic says attackers have converted about $175 million of stolen altcoins into about 2,300 BTC, though Arkham’s labelled wallets show far less — the two trackers measure different things and the gap is unresolved. The 44-token proof-of-reserves snapshot is now confirmed live, with every token at or above 100% backing. Chainalysis has brought Reactor back online alongside KYT and address screening, and Nubank’s crypto service is restored while Mercado Pago’s is not confirmed.

Supply chain & third-party risk

Chainalysis — Reactor back online, 7 Nov

Chainalysis’s incident-blog update on Saturday 7 November (13:50 EST / 18:50 UTC) says Reactor, KYT (user interface and API) and Address Screening are now back online, “restored with enhanced security measures”, and that customers are being emailed instructions to reconnect in a specific way. This is the first update to restore Reactor — the 6 November update had only begun phased KYT and address-screening restoration, with Reactor still offline. The 7 November update says nothing about Data Solutions, so its status remains unclear. All users must still reset passwords and rotate API keys (per the 6 November update), and restoration sits behind trusted-IP allowlisting. No new attribution, no new exposure figures, and no change to the company’s line that it has no evidence investigation data was accessed or attribution data manipulated. BFSI relevance is direct: KYT is the transaction-screening tool banks, insurers and crypto firms use for AML monitoring, and its outage was already disrupting downstream services (see Emerging fraud).

SMBC Consumer Finance partner fallout — Update

A new partner name in-window: Sanin Godo Bank disclosed on 7 November that 1,585 of its personal-loan customers were affected via the guarantee system — names, sex, address, phone, email, driver’s licence number and annual income. That is a subset of the up-to-320,118 figure, not an addition to it. The guarantee system remains suspended with resumption aimed at mid-November, and up to 188 partner institutions have stopped accepting new card-loan applications. The control question for any bank that outsources loan guarantees: which of your customers’ data sits in a guarantor’s system, and how would you know it had leaked?

Nubank restored, Mercado Pago still paused — Update

Nubank confirmed on 7 November that crypto deposits and withdrawals were restored the previous evening (Friday 6 November, 22:00 local); in-app buying and selling had continued throughout, and the bank said the fault was at an external data provider, not its own systems, with customer assets and data unaffected. Mercado Pago has not been confirmed restored as of the latest 7 November reports: it blocked some transfers to external wallets after an incident at a risk-monitoring / anti-money-laundering provider, and incoming deposits could be delayed but would be credited automatically. Neither company has named the provider, and there is no confirmation both incidents share a cause — do not attribute them to Paxos or to Chainalysis on the basis that both firms have worked with those providers historically. Treat as a downstream-outage report, not a confirmed Chainalysis consequence — but it is exactly the kind of disruption the Chainalysis outage was expected to cause, and other firms using the same screening provider should check their own crypto flows now rather than after a customer complaint.

Gopax freeze — second day, no resumption date

The Binance-backed South Korean exchange remains frozen on 7 November with no resumption date: standard withdrawals unavailable, crypto withdrawals only by emailed request to customer support and processed manually, assets being moved to cold wallets. The exchange says it has not verified the hacking threat in the email it received and has detected no intrusion or asset loss. The pattern to note is that a single unverified threat email can now halt a regulated exchange for days in the middle of a sector-wide breach wave — and that customers with funds on the exchange have no timeline.

Emerging fraud & attack vectors

US charges three over $129m AI-impersonation fraud — NEW (announced 5 Nov, reported in-window 7 Nov)

The US Department of Justice announced on 5 November that three foreign nationals — Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan and Guy Sheetrit — were arrested and charged over a scheme that used AI to clone the voices and images of CEOs and lawyers at major law firms, running from July 2024 to February 2026 and targeting thousands of companies. Named BFSI losses include a UK fintech ($11.58 million), a Rhode Island bank (about $2.68 million), a Massachusetts mutual fund ($4.64 million) and a New York investment company ($2.8 million); total alleged losses are at least $129 million. Funds were moved through accounts in the United States, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the UAE, Singapore, Austria, Slovakia and China, then converted to cryptocurrency. The seven counts include wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering. These are charges — allegations, not findings — and the defendants are presumed innocent. The DOJ also announced a Super Intelligence Task Force. This is a prosecution of past fraud, not a new campaign, but it is the clearest confirmed case yet of AI-cloned executive voices and images being used to move money out of financial firms — and it was not covered in earlier editions, so it is included here with its original announcement date stated.

Arkéa, Bank Rakyat and Mirae Asset phishing watch — ongoing, with new bait

Arkéa has warned customers of increased phishing risk after its attack, and its story remains live phishing and laundering bait. Bank Rakyat’s defaced pages and the minister’s statement will be cited in phishing within days, and Mirae Asset’s leak gives attackers 3,800 customer account numbers plus the names, email addresses and mobile numbers of 35,255 employees and contractors — enough to impersonate the brokerage to customers and staff internally. The AU Small Finance Bank sale claim, if it spreads, adds PAN and Aadhaar details to the impersonation toolkit for Indian customers. Customers should be told to use the official apps and known channels only: Mirae Asset’s temporary helpline ran on 7 November, and Bank Rakyat has directed customers to iRakyat, BRICK, QR2E and i-bizRAKYAT.

Korean secondary-damage watch — response period extended to end-December

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission has extended its secondary-damage special response period (voice phishing and smishing using leaked data) to the end of December, from 5 November. As of 6 November, no fund-loss cases from secondary phishing or smishing had been reported — but the extension itself signals the regulator expects the leaked data to be used. Banks with Korean customers should keep warning customers that no legitimate bank contact will ask for passwords, OTPs or transfers, and should watch for voice-phishing attempts citing the Mirae Asset, Zoomfund and Payhub incidents.

Restoration, refund and outage phishing — ongoing

Binance, Mercado Bitcoin (view-only reopening starts 9 November), Gopax (manual-withdrawal emails) and Nubank / Mercado Pago outage messages remain live phishing bait. Any message citing withdrawal restoration, a refund, a manual-withdrawal process or a crypto-service outage should be treated as suspect until verified through a known channel. Verify via official status pages only, never via links in messages claiming to restore withdrawals, refunds or loan access.

AI in the current threat landscape

AI as a target, AI as a weapon or accelerator, and AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI.

AI as a target — no new incident in this window

No new LLM or API-key theft, exposed LLM endpoint or LLMjacking incident was published in this window. The Sophos X-Ops research on malicious AI-agent “skills” was presented at BSides Sydney on 7 November (Younghoo Lee, “Decode facts, then reason”), but no post-talk write-up was found in-window; the research remains defensive work on a synthetic benchmark — a skill whose code does more than its description claims, for example a CSV-summariser that also reads credentials, contacts external hosts or runs shell commands, hidden by obfuscation — with no live incident, no in-the-wild malicious skill and no IOCs named. The Google / Mandiant LLMjacking research is dated 2–3 November and is carried as standing risk, not a new incident: stolen cloud credentials and LLM API keys, over $100,000 in fraudulent charges in a single day and up to $1 million cumulative at some victims, financial services among the victims, and exploitation of CVE-2026-40217 in LiteLLM. Standing controls still apply: key inventory and rotation, endpoint exposure (no unauthenticated Ollama / LiteLLM on the internet), spend and invocation monitoring with revoke-and-rotate.

AI as a weapon / accelerator — the DOJ $129m case is the confirmed example; the Mirae Asset characterisation is still only suspected

The one confirmed AI-scaled fraud item in this edition is the US Department of Justice case announced 5 November and reported in-window on 7 November: three people charged over AI-cloned voices and images of CEOs and lawyers used to defraud firms, with named BFSI losses including a UK fintech ($11.58 million), a Rhode Island bank (about $2.68 million), a Massachusetts mutual fund ($4.64 million) and a New York investment company ($2.8 million). The charges are allegations, not findings, and the scheme ran from July 2024 to February 2026 — it is a prosecution of past fraud, not a new campaign. Separately, the Mirae Asset Securities attack is still only suspected to involve AI agents: Korean reporting on 7 November still uses “believed / presumed / suspected” language with no forensic proof, named tool or IOCs, and the Financial Post reports that “financial authorities regard AI agents as having been mobilised and are continuing investigations” — reported, not confirmed. A Cheonji Ilbo article dated 8 November cites a Monitorlab AI Labs report on AI autonomous hacking tool ARTEX in the Korean financial-sector attacks; that is vendor analysis, not regulator confirmation of ARTEX use at Mirae Asset. CERT-In’s Crafty Falcon advisory and the Microsoft / Check Point ARTEX research are 4–5 November items with no new development in this window; they remain standing context, not new sightings.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI — Korea’s regulator moves on crypto CISOs; no new global guidance in-window

South Korea’s Financial Supervisory Service will convene the CISOs of all virtual-asset service providers on 19 November to review security-management practice and prevention measures, following the FSC’s 5 November announcement of a full review of external contact points and internal controls across all financial firms. The same reporting notes the FSS has only about 50 IT-security staff, all assigned to incident response, and is consulting the FSC on next year’s budget, staffing and a possible dedicated IT-security organisation — a capacity constraint that matters for how fast regulatory follow-up will arrive. No new OCC, MAS or CSA Singapore AI-in-finance guidance was issued in this window (the OCC examiner framework is 4 November; MAS’s final AI risk-management guidelines are 7 October). Standing context: the CSA / GovTech / IMDA personal-AI-agent advisory of 6 November — do not give agents access to banking, government or healthcare accounts — and Elastic’s finding that attackers execute commands through Claude Code and ChatGPT desktop in confirmed intrusions. Standing controls: agent-tool access, invocation monitoring, key inventory and rotation, endpoint exposure, and spend and invocation monitoring with revoke-and-rotate.

No new AI-scaled phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign in this window

No newly reported AI-generated phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a BFSI victim was found for 7–8 November. That is absence of reporting, not evidence it has stopped. The DOJ $129m case is a prosecution of past fraud, not a new campaign, and the Mirae Asset, Bank Rakyat, Arkéa and SMFG cases are not re-labelled as AI campaigns — none attributes them to AI tooling, and the single newspaper characterisation of Mirae Asset as a suspected AI-agent attack is unconfirmed.

Indicators & defensive actions

Notable IOCs / behavioural indicators

  • Binance laundering (Elliptic, 7 Nov): about $175 million of stolen altcoins converted to about 2,300 BTC in newly created BTC addresses; about 44 million XRP (about $51 million) swapped into BTC via THORChain overnight, leaving about 148 million XRP (about $173 million); about 53 BTC (about $4 million) entered Wasabi-style CoinJoin mixing in the period (about 88 BTC / $6.7 million cumulatively). Operation spans 20,000+ wallets, 16 blockchains, at least 36 services and about 65,000 transactions. Flag the gap: Arkham’s labelled wallets showed only about 18,385 BTC against 18,270.2 BTC drained (about +115 BTC) — far below Elliptic’s 2,300 BTC estimate; the two trackers measure different things and the gap is unresolved. Screen for THORChain swaps and CoinJoin mixing in post-incident transaction monitoring; do not rely on a single tracker’s figures.
  • OpenSSH sshole (Previdian, 6 Nov): source IP 89.116.89[.]18 (Japan-based) — eight exploitation attempts, all from this address; sshd log lines “Accepted password for” an account that has no password (alpine, root, key-only users), followed by non-interactive sessions running id, uname, hostname.
  • SonicWall CVE-2026-19868: no IOCs published. Detection is configuration-based: SSLVPN with SAML authentication enabled on SonicOS 7.3.3-7015 and older (Gen7) or 8.2.2-8015 and older (Gen8); review SSLVPN login events for alternate-method logins that bypassed SAML. No verified public PoC — one repo claim is unverified and is not linked.
  • NetScaler CVE-2026-88771: attacker-controlled text (for example a username containing a semicolon) POSTed to /nf/auth/doAuthentication.do, written to the appliance log and later executed by the maintenance script ns_monuploadd_err.pl — execution can lag the request by up to 24 hours, so check logs and persistence, not just the patch.
  • Keycloak CVE-2026-120984: no IOCs published. Detection is configuration-based: client-management access with authorisation services enabled; audit JavaScript policies attached to clients and review client creation / import events by privileged accounts.
  • Mirae Asset: no IOCs published. Leaked account numbers (about 3,800) and employee names, email addresses and mobile numbers are phishing and impersonation fuel — expect messages impersonating the brokerage and its staff.
  • Bank Rakyat: actor handle “Marlanwg”; defaced pages included personal-financing sections and the iRakyat login page; the defacement directed bank officials to the Session messaging app. The bank says the corporate site is restored.
  • AU Small Finance Bank / Pekin Insurance / Higginbotham: no IOCs published — all three are leak-site claims, and no data sample has been independently verified for AU Small Finance Bank or Pekin Insurance. Do not hunt for IOCs that do not exist; watch for victim confirmation or a data publication.
  • ARTEX (carried): service-principal sign-ins from IP 199.19.77.84 from 28 Oct across multiple application IDs; Docker image mianhuatang11/artex (about 9,000 downloads) — backdoored build served by 62 exposed consoles 2–4 Nov; 1,608 live ARTEX instances, 2,557 hosting; 10 misconfigured instances exposing full operator databases.
  • WordPress (carried): top attacking IPs per Wordfence — 103.59.160.171, 2001:df1:e8c0::106b, 51.89.103.78, 185.177.72.61, 195.3.220.7; requests for staged installer paths under wp-content/upgrade/ around a core upgrade window — including during the fix install itself.
  • Mirax RAT (carried): build tag “aquafina”; impersonates Google Play Services; dynamically generated package names; Accessibility-service abuse on Android.
  • Arara phishing (carried): about 7.13 million phishing emails sent impersonating a card company from news@arara.com, 28–30 Oct.
  • SMBC Consumer Finance (carried): affected customers are contacted individually by email or SMS — any such message with a link is suspect until verified via an official site; leaked licence numbers, income and guarantee amounts are directly usable for impersonation. Sanin Godo Bank’s 1,585 customers are a subset of the up-to-320,118 figure.
  • Arkéa (investigator analytics, not bank-confirmed): USDC/USDT withdrawals from Kraken, Crypto.com and OKX accounts over 1–2 Nov (Bitquery: $108.8 million); conversion of $62.98 million in stablecoins to 27,553 ETH in about four hours via UniswapX (Bitquery).
  • Binance / Mercado Bitcoin / Gopax (carried): restoration and refund messages with links, WhatsApp or phone calls are phishing — verify via official status pages only. Gopax manual-withdrawal emails are a new phishing pretext.
