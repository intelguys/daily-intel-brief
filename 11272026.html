AI as a weapon or accelerator

  • Microsoft Threat Intelligence — “What defenders need to know about ARTEX, the agentic AI framework threat actors abuse” (NEW, 25 Nov 18:09 UTC; reported 26 Nov; primary read in full). Microsoft’s first full defender guide on ARTEX. As of November 2026 it has observed six threat actors integrating ARTEX into their tooling: Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon — state-sponsored and financially motivated side by side. Four of them (Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon, Salt Typhoon) are newly named as ARTEX users in this report; Storm-0940 and Storm-3358 were already linked. Storm-3358 is the financially motivated actor Microsoft previously linked to targeting financial-sector organisations (6 Nov report, already covered). Scale and speed, all Microsoft’s own figures: one Storm-0940 password-spray campaign produced more than 151,000 failed sign-ins, peaking at 2,900 failures per minute; in multiple Storm-0940 intrusions privileged access came less than three hours after initial targeting; in one case an agent went from searching public GitHub repositories for application secrets to extracting data from Azure Key Vault in five minutes. During Storm-0940 activity, ARTEX agents added credentials to applications to maintain access, and one application assigned itself the Global Administrator role. The ConnectWise campaign (sign-in attempts against more than 2,250 customer tenants) is Microsoft’s October finding, restated by secondary reporting — the 2,250 figure is from that earlier report, not new in this one. Architecture and provenance: ARTEX (“AI autonomous penetration testing system”) is a Go backend coordinating multiple LLM agents — goals model, planner, worker agents with a real shell, reporter agent — over a knowledge graph, with an embedded Next.js web interface and PostgreSQL storage. Released July 2026 by GitHub user Autumn-27 with a Chinese-language interface; the original source was removed in October 2026 and forks continue in many languages. In November 2026 the China-based firm ThreatBook claimed ownership and began selling commercial access to “ARTEX Pro”, alongside CyberStrikeAI Enterprise Edition and other frameworks. Agents can be powered by virtually any LLM, including frontier, open-weight or guardrail-stripped models, and can switch models mid-campaign — Microsoft’s conclusion: ARTEX activity is “not necessarily constrained by any single provider’s safety controls or visible in any single provider’s telemetry.” Detection (primary): the durable signal is behavioural — bursts of varied requests at machine speed, retries at machine speed, web-server processes spawning shells, several short-lived web shells with incrementing names, rapid reads of /etc/shadow, .env and cloud credential files, two or three attack chains at once. Weak signals: HTTP user agents artex-enrich/* and norma/*, rotating webhook.site URLs to deliver scripts and return output, Defender Antivirus detection Behavior:Win64/ArtexAI. Microsoft also recommends honeypots, decoys, tarpits and fake wins, which in its testing pulled agents away from real assets. Microsoft publishes a 28-technique MITRE ATT&CK mapping in the blog — the first primary-source MITRE mapping in this week’s AI items (see TTP Tracker). BFSI relevance: Storm-3358’s financial-sector targeting is the direct link; the cloud-identity abuse pattern (stolen application credentials, added app credentials, self-assigned Global Admin, Key Vault reads in minutes) is the pattern banks running Azure/Microsoft 365 carry. No new named BFSI victim in this report.
  • Canada — Cyber Centre advisory “Cyber threat actors increasingly use automated and AI-enabled tools in cyber attacks” (CATCH-UP, 21 Nov; reported 22 and 26 Nov; primary read in full). The fifth national-agency warning in this sequence (CERT-In 20 Nov, UK NCSC 24 Nov, Italy CSIRT 23 Nov, Switzerland NCSC 24 Nov, Canada 21 Nov). The advisory is dated/last modified 21 November, so it is pre-window; it was not covered in earlier editions and surfaced in-window via 26 November reporting (Daily Star), so include as a catch-up dated 21 November, not as a 26 November publication. Numbers, all from the Cyber Centre’s own analysis: of globally observed AI-enabled offensive-security-tool infrastructure, ~46% ARTEX, 28% CyberStrikeAI, 14% PentAGI. ARTEX was made closed source on 8 October 2026 after being copied more than 12,000 times; “once a tool like this is released, organizations should expect it to stay in circulation.” The Centre is aware of activity targeting Canadian organisations from servers running ARTEX, ongoing for several months across multiple sectors — it has not attributed the activity to a specific actor, and stresses targeting “is not an indication of compromise”. In some activity described in its agentic-AI bulletin, less than three hours passed between selecting a target and gaining privileged access to cloud systems. Control advice specific to this advisory: monitor the cloud identity and application layer (alert on a new secret/certificate added to an application, newly granted permissions, consent to an unfamiliar application — these survive a password reset); suppress verbose error output in production (“detailed error messages and stack traces are what let an automated agent correct a failed payload and try again within seconds”); look for the shape of automated activity (high request volumes, long runs of failed requests followed by a success, rapid bursts of read-only validation-style API calls); verify remediation by re-scanning the external surface and hunting persistence that survives a password change (app credentials, altered recovery addresses, web shells, new service accounts). It also points to joint guidance on the careful adoption of agentic AI services for organisations running their own agents. Forecast, marked as such: the Centre assesses AI-enabled cyber activity “will likely become more targeted, less visible and harder to detect as the technology improves.” BFSI relevance: no Canadian bank or financial victim named; the cloud-identity tradecraft and the 46/28/14 infrastructure split are the usable figures. The advisory also notes the FBI’s 13 Nov ARTEX FLASH and Microsoft’s Storm-0940 ConnectWise reporting as context.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

  • Google — Workspace AI-agent controls go GA; MCP/CLI mandatory from 1 February 2027 (NEW, announced 24 Nov; reported 26 Nov).
