Executive summary

1

The RMM crisis deepened overnight: ScreenConnect is now a named worm, and N-able’s Hotfix 2 has already been bypassed. Huntress published the full SCKit picture on 12 Nov: 288 hosts across 48 organisations hit in about 90 minutes, spread through the RMM+ extension, which accepted a blank access key in versions 1.0.31/1.0.32 and bypasses ScreenConnect accounts, roles and MFA. The toolkit’s RelayTrap steals session cookies and is designed to trap any technician who connects — but Huntress is explicit that the technician-to-panel spread has not been observed to succeed, a correction to yesterday’s framing. Separately, researcher Sean Bales published two new N-central chains, 4EverAble2Shell and Agent2Shell, tested against Hotfix 2; Previdian and Huntback report probing within hours and 21 production servers mining XMRig, 19 of them on Hotfix 2 — while N-able says it has seen no compromise using the new flaws. The one solid discriminator: the server chain needs the Legacy API enabled, which is not the default. Disable it, and hunt for miners and for requests to /dms/services/ServerUI.

2

Brazil’s Caixa insurer disclosed a 7.6-million-customer breach, Korea’s and Taiwan’s regulators moved on last week’s breaches, and a GitHub credential-stealing campaign hit tens of thousands of repositories. Caixa Vida e Previdência confirmed attackers copied registration data for all 7.6 million active customers — names and CPFs throughout, plus addresses, phones, emails and birth dates in part of the records — detected 6 Nov and disclosed 12 Nov; the “no financial data” conclusion is explicitly preliminary, and some wire reporting says the data was published while the company says it has no evidence of public disclosure or misuse so far. In Korea, the Financial Services Commission will start a special audit of the breached Financial Security Institute from 16 November and the FSI president apologised; in Taiwan, the FSC gave SinoPac until the end of 12 Nov to file its full report, is examining vendor due diligence and has not ruled out a fine. In the US, Sawyer Savings Bank confirmed a breach of 16,069 people in the Storm-1175 campaign against small lenders, which Microsoft links to N-able exploitation via MSPs. And the wfx-loot campaign used stolen GitHub credentials to push a CI secret stealer into 61,764 repositories, exfiltrating credentials — including AI service keys — from at least 1,806 of them.

3

Citrix patched a 31-CVE batch, a public PoC now exists for the Apache mod_http2 RCE, and Check Point confirmed PHP2Shell exploitation on its own gateways — with a wiper. Citrix’s 12 Nov bulletins fix 31 CVEs (one outlet counts 12 headline flaws — the count is unresolved between sources), worst a 9.5 memory flaw and a 9.4 Console authentication bypass; no exploitation or PoC reported for the new flaws. A third-party research PoC for Apache CVE-2026-119618 was published on 12 Nov and verified as substantive research code; in-the-wild exploitation is still not confirmed. Check Point reports attackers chained PHP2Shell with a Gaia privilege-escalation flaw (CVE-2026-123039) to root on unpatched gateways, planted the TSGA backdoor, pivoted to a VMware ESXi host and ran a wiper — “a couple of dozen” systems in one region. Unit 42 separately published Cairn, a Chinese-speaking operator’s largely autonomous AI intrusions against at least 19 organisations, and Arctic Wolf tracked BlueNoroff’s AI-deepfake lures against 677 finance and crypto decision-makers. Europol’s Operation Endgame seized the Gootloader, CastleRAT and StealC infrastructure and secured 6.4 million victim datasets. No new CISA KEV additions were made on 12 November.

AI in the current threat landscape

AI as a target, AI as a weapon or accelerator, and AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI.

AI as a target

Notion revokes API keys after the Fireworks breach (follow-up, 12 Nov). Notion disabled exposed API keys on 11 Nov after finding them inside Notion AI chat transcripts exposed via the Fireworks AI breach. Fireworks attributes the October intrusions to a financially motivated LLMjacking crew reselling stolen model access; billing data, secrets, datasets and incomplete model weights were exposed for a limited set of accounts. wfx-loot (NEW, Aikido, 12 Nov): the GitHub CI secret stealer specifically harvests AI service keys (OpenAI, Anthropic, Cursor, Copilot) alongside cloud credentials and SSH keys — at least 1,806 repositories ran the payload with credentials exfiltrated. Recorded Future Insikt Group AI report (coverage 12 Nov): 103 AI-linked incidents, 31 Jul 2025–31 Jul 2026 — 32 involved AI in malware/offensive operations; 71 were attacks on AI infrastructure, AI-themed delivery, AI-assisted deception or abuse of AI services. Key cases: the LiteLLM PyPI compromise (Feb 2026, malicious v1.82.7/1.82.8), the Shai-Hulud worm expanding to Claude Code (May 2026), and a state-sponsored campaign against Taiwanese government targets using up to eight AI agents (Hermes/OpenClaw framework; 85 credentials cracked, 2,500+ personnel records exfiltrated, July 2026). Attackers mostly use AI to accelerate familiar crime (supply-chain compromise, phishing, extortion); fully autonomous operations remain rare. Timing caveat: the report appears to have gone live ~12 Nov 00:35 SGT, just before the 09:00 window — the in-window item is the 12 Nov coverage. Standing: Google/Mandiant LLMjacking research (2–3 Nov) — stolen cloud credentials and LLM API keys, fraudulent bills over $100,000 in a single day and up to ~$1m cumulative at some victims, financial services among victims; Railway’s final incident report (10 Nov) — ~$740,000 in unauthorised AI usage on stolen keys. Standing controls: key inventory and rotation, endpoint exposure, spend and invocation monitoring with revoke-and-rotate.

Cairn autonomous intrusions (NEW, Unit 42, 12 Nov). A Chinese-speaking operator ran 25 largely autonomous operations against at least 19 organisations in Aug–Sep 2026 using the open-source Cairn framework, supplying only a target domain and a one-line goal; the framework planned and executed itself, paired with Zhipu GLM 5.2 and DeepSeek models. BlueNoroff (NEW, Arctic Wolf, 12 Nov): AI deepfakes built with Google Generative AI, Grok Imagine, Wondershare AIGC and GPT-4o, no longer distinguishable by visual inspection, used to lure crypto and venture-capital decision-makers. FaceStealer (NEW, FBI, 12 Nov): North Korean actors are starting to use AI to create deepfake videos of well-known crypto/blockchain figures as part of a scheme that has stolen over $400m. Kozhikode deepfake case (NEW, police case reported 12 Nov): a fake video of India’s finance minister lured a victim into a trading scam, ₹49.2 lakh lost — the only in-window AI-scaled fraud case found. No new ARTEX, CyberStrikeAI, AntiFox, PentAGI or HexStrike AI reporting, tool update or observed use dated 12–13 Nov was found (the latest material is 5–11 Nov); Cairn is the new agentic item, and it is a different framework. AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

Post-training supply-chain attacks on coding agents (NEW, Microsoft, 12 Nov). Microsoft Security Blog (Defender Research + AI Red Team), “Trained to betray”: two studies show hidden backdoors in coding/terminal agents via the post-training supply chain. Dataset poisoning — 90 crafted examples hidden in ~9,000 fine-tuning conversations (~1%) taught a gpt-4.1-mini model to find any available command-execution tool and misuse it on routine, even paraphrased, requests (~78% attack success on in-distribution harnesses, transferring to an unseen harness). Model poisoning — an attacker controlling training before distributing an open-weight checkpoint (Qwen3.5-4B/9B, SFT + RL) reached 84.1–94.8% success on unseen repositories while preserving benign performance, with backdoors surviving later training. A dormant backdoor in an agent can invoke tools, execute commands, modify files or access data when triggered. Recommended controls: training-data scrutiny, checkpoint provenance, deployment-specific evaluation, restricted tool permissions, sandboxed execution, runtime monitoring. This is research, not observed attacks — report it as such. FCA AI-review analysis for payments firms (NEW, 13 Nov 07:59 SGT). Analysis of the FCA’s Wholesale Banks Supervision Insights 3 (2 Nov) and the joint Bank of England/FCA AI survey (196 firms; 86% of FS firms now use AI, 94% have a firm-wide AI policy, human review the most common control at 95%). It recaps FCA findings already reported: AI-generated code errors passing human review and causing a loss of service at one wholesale bank, staff use of unapproved AI tools leaking sensitive data, and untested kill switches. Recommendations: tested kill switches with named authorised operators, accurate AI inventories, disclosure amnesties for unapproved AI use, and treating unapproved AI as a standing board item. The FCA will not write new AI rules but expects senior-manager accountability and incident reporting to extend to “significant AI failures and near-misses”. The 12 Nov date is the analysis, not new regulator action. Standing: OCC Bulletin 2026-54 (released 4 Nov; analysis 11 Nov) creates no new rules and sets no supervisory expectations; Digidentity survey (11 Nov): 69% of UK banking customers doubt their bank can detect AI deepfake impersonation; 80% expect banks to use advanced deepfake detection; 53% say SMS one-time codes are no longer sufficient (survey data, not an incident). AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is now a standing fact on both sides: Fortinet’s two new FortiMail criticals were found with the Frontier AI model Claude Mythos; Microsoft’s FORGE Lab (11 Nov) reports AI-assisted scanning produced 33 kernel CVEs; Tenable notes the exploited Outlook CVE-2026-124194 was discovered by a researcher using Claude; Citrix’s 12 Nov NetScaler fixes followed a deeper codebase review using AI-assisted code analysis. Standing: NCSC UK chief executive’s 9 Nov warning of an AI-powered ransomware attack on UK critical national infrastructure being “highly likely” by 2028 is a forecast/assessment, not a new incident. Standing controls: CSA/GovTech/IMDA personal-AI-agent advisory (6 Nov) — do not give agents access to banking, government or healthcare accounts; Sophos malicious AI-agent “skills” research (6 Nov); Haiou “prompt adware” (Check Point, 4 Nov); Elastic’s finding that attackers execute commands through Claude Code and ChatGPT desktop in confirmed intrusions. Also standing: the npm REF8801 packages that rerouted AI-agent traffic through the operator’s API (@apexacc/cli replaced with a security placeholder 11 Nov; @apexfdn/copilot-mcp still published — treat as attacker infrastructure).
