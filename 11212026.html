Executive summary

1

CISA added four exploited flaws to its KEV catalogue on 20 November — and the first in-the-wild exploitation of the Cisco Expressway flaw was documented the same day. Apache Roller, VMware vCenter, SAP NetWeaver and Cisco Expressway all join the catalogue, each due for US federal remediation on Monday 23 November. Russian firm F6 investigated what it describes as the first exploitation of the Expressway flaw (CVE-2026-76466, 9.8), days after a public proof of concept appeared: two internet-facing boxes were breached and the attackers pivoted to internal cluster nodes. Separately, BleepingComputer reports the suspected shared vendor behind the n0n leaks of JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard data: the group claims it attacked Qubole on 11 November and used compromised Qubole credentials to reach customers’ AWS environments. Qubole has not responded and none of the three firms has named a vendor, so the link stays reported, not confirmed.

2

Plaid’s KARTE investigation is complete and the scale keeps growing — while Rakuten faces possible penalties and a class action passes 20,000 sign-ups. Plaid says the 13 November attack exploited a vulnerability behind a public data-retrieval route, then used stolen credentials to reach the KARTE admin console; data from about 2,200 companies leaked or may have leaked, including data of firms that had stopped using KARTE. ZOZO (about 29.1m records) and Marui Group (about 17.3m, including EPOS card holders) disclosed on 20 November, pushing published counts past 140 million records — a tally of company disclosures, not 140 million people. KARTE stays offline with a staged restart targeted for mid-January 2027, and Plaid now forecasts a ¥2bn loss. In Japan, Financial Services Minister Satsuki Katayama addressed the Rakuten Securities leak on 20 November and the FSA will consider a business improvement order; a lawyer-led class action has passed 20,000 registrants. In Korea, reporting on 20 November put updated totals on the September–October bank wave, and the FSS warned all 28 virtual-asset operators that they could be held responsible for unaddressed vulnerabilities.

3

AI agents crossed a line in a disclosed Amazon case — and vishing that defeats MFA is now documented in detail. Amazon Threat Intelligence reports that suspected China-nexus operators used the open-source agentic framework Cairn to autonomously find and exploit a previously unknown flaw in MONAI Label, a medical-imaging AI application, taking root on a university server in September; the disclosure is new, the activity is not. Unit 42’s “Dialing for Data”, published 20 November, details Com-affiliated vishing waves — including Bling Libra (ShinyHunters) and six activity clusters — targeting financial services, healthcare and legal firms, defeating MFA with adversary-in-the-middle proxies and enrolling unauthorised MFA devices within minutes. Patching priority: the four KEV items due 23 November, plus the PHP, Splunk and vCenter fixes from yesterday’s edition.

Com-affiliated actors — vishing waves targeting financial services, defeating MFA (Unit 42, 20 Nov)

Financial services, healthcare and legal firms, primarily in the US, UK and Australia

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published. Vishing posing as internal IT support directs employees to organisation-specific phishing pages; adversary-in-the-middle SSO proxies (Cloudflare-fronted, Evilginx2 / Doko’s Panel kits) capture credentials and MFA approvals in real time; unauthorised MFA devices enrolled within minutes (one device, “Pixel 10a”, reused across accounts); “keep me signed in” session tokens replayed to avoid re-prompting Duo MFA; scripted exfiltration from Microsoft 365, OneDrive and SharePoint (python-httpx, python-requests, Microsoft Graph SDK); extortion via Microsoft Teams messages and email directing victims to a Tor negotiation site; a malicious sideloaded browser extension masquerading as “Google Chrome”, downloaded from oskeysetup[.]com, that keylogs, steals cookies and session storage, scans QR codes for MFA tokens and streams webcams via the Chrome DevTools Protocol.
Impact
New in-window. Unit 42 published “Dialing for Data: Inside the Com’s High-Profile Heists” on 20 November, covering sustained vishing-led data theft and extortion since August 2026. Attribution covers Bling Libra (aka ShinyHunters) and clusters CL-CRI-1116 (Redact, formerly BlackFile), CL-CRI-1147 (Pink), CL-CRI-1182 (Falcon), CL-CRI-1192 (Helix), and newly uncovered CL-CRI-1264 (Vortex) and CL-CRI-1274 (Cinder), which overlap with activity Google tracks as UNC6671. Financial services were the most-targeted industry in August (about twice healthcare); healthcare overtook finance in September, with finance still joint-top in October. Attackers prioritised private investment portfolios, deal pipelines and large cloud data warehouses, occasionally exfiltrating tens of millions of CRM and recruiting records. Australian organisations rose from under 1% of targeted subdomains in August–September to about 13% in October. No new named victim organisations are identified in the report. Unit 42 notes recent arrests of suspected Bling Libra members, including the alleged operator of its extortion site. Unit 42 publishes IOCs (see Indicators).

Supply chain & third-party risk

Vendor, SaaS and service-provider exposure affecting financial organisations.

n0n — Visa, JPMorgan Chase and Mastercard: Qubole named as the suspected shared vendor (UPDATE)

BleepingComputer reports n0n claims it attacked Qubole, a cloud data-analytics firm owned by Idera, on 11 November, and used compromised Qubole credentials between 14 and 26 October to reach customers’ AWS environments through IAM roles configured for Qubole. The group claims 53.9 GB across 30,743 files taken directly from Qubole and nearly one petabyte from customer networks — both figures are the group’s claims, unverified. The lesson for banks is the one the Qubole campaign already taught: ask every marketing, benefits and analytics vendor what customer data it holds, which of your cloud roles it can assume, and whether those roles are still needed.

PLAID KARTE — investigation complete; ZOZO and Marui disclose; restart targeted mid-January 2027 (UPDATE)

Emerging fraud & attack vectors

“Party Panel” — rent-a-trojan Android banking malware targeting Indian bank customers (NEW, reported 20 Nov)

CloudSEK’s XVigil found a multi-tenant malware-as-a-service panel that generates fake Google Play pages and branded Android banking trojans for six Indian banks and card issuers — anonymised in the public report, so no bank is named — plus government-service themes (e-challan fines, farmer income support, pensioner life certificates). One unprotected server (103.151.199[.]22:8080) hosted the whole operation: 50 tenant accounts, 34 on paid 7–60-day licences approved 8–12 November, 19 live from 10 November. A fake credit-card “app” page harvests card number, expiry, CVV, date of birth and mother’s name, with keystrokes streamed even if the form is abandoned, then pushes a 4.4 MB sideloaded APK: the stage-1 dropper fakes a Play Store install to bypass Android 13+ Restricted Settings, disables Play Protect, and installs a hidden SMS stealer that forwards every bank OTP, uploads the SMS inbox and gallery photos, and sets call forwarding (*21*<number>#) so bank verification calls reach the fraudster. Exfiltration runs through a Firebase project and 13 Telegram bots, and the panel API answered without a login. CloudSEK assesses with medium confidence that the operator is India-based, and analysed samples statically — no victim records were accessed. CloudSEK published MITRE mobile techniques (T1406, T1582 SMS Control, T1616 Call Control, T1636.004, T1646). Banks should warn customers against sideloaded “bank apps” and consider moving from SMS OTP towards app-based or FIDO authentication.

Mail-bombing then Teams vishing for account takeover; AI-agent setup indicated, not confirmed (NEW, reported 20 Nov)

Fortra’s FIRE team tracked large-scale mail-bombing — inbox flooding via newsletter sign-ups, about 10,000 messages at peak per campaign and about 1,000 a day, delivered through abused Mailchimp / Mandrill, Amazon SES and SendGrid — followed by vishing over Microsoft Teams from attacker tenants impersonating internal IT, aiming for Quick Assist or remote access and account compromise, with phishing hidden inside the flood. FIRE said there are “some indications that these attacks are likely an AI Agent setup” — an agent instructed to crawl pages for newsletter subscription boxes. That is an indication, not confirmation. The playbook matches Microsoft’s Storm-1811 (the Black Basta access crew), but FIRE and Microsoft do not attribute these cases to a specific group. The campaign pattern was confirmed by Fortra; the FIRE update is dated 19 November and the reporting is 20 November. Banks should brief staff: a flood of subscription emails followed by an “IT helpdesk” Teams call is the attack, and no helpdesk should ask staff to grant remote access on the back of it.

Bazuka — Android malware framework with LLM-assisted victim selection (NEW, reported 20 Nov)

ThreatFabric disclosed Bazuka, a previously unknown Android malware framework (dropper, core and 23 modules) whose control panel integrates large language models to score and select high-value victims at scale. Modules cover overlays, keylogging, 2FA and SMS interception, remote control and crypto-address swapping; a novel fake-screen overlay screenshots the home and lock screen to hide background activity. Distribution runs through social-media ads, bogus sites and a dropper masquerading as a premium TikTok app; a Google Play route is suggested by panel code but not evidenced. Discovery came in late October 2026 during an NFC-relay banking-trojan investigation; the ThreatFabric technical report is dated 18–19 November and The Hacker News reported it on 20 November. No actor attribution. Sample indicator: C2 domain c2api[.]brokerok[.]org. Overlays target banking and payment apps and credential and OTP theft; LLM-assisted triage lowers the cost of running large fraud campaigns.

Com-affiliated vishing — finance is a primary target; MFA is defeated, not bypassed (NEW, Unit 42, 20 Nov)

AI in the current threat landscape

AI as a target, AI as a weapon or accelerator, and AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI — kept separate, with forecasts marked as forecasts.

AI as a target

Cairn agents autonomously found and exploited a zero-day (NEW, confirmed — Amazon, 20 Nov). Amazon Threat Intelligence reports that suspected China-nexus operators used the open-source agentic framework Cairn to discover a previously unknown path-traversal flaw in MONAI Label, an open-source medical-imaging AI application, and gain root on a university server. The campaign ran 8–10 September 2026 against East Asian educational institutions outside China — the disclosure is in-window, the activity is not. Operators gave a high-level objective (“obtain shell access”); the agents planned and adapted without step-by-step human direction. From API mapping to arbitrary file write took about 11 minutes; a later session planted a Python reverse shell in sitecustomize.py and triggered an API endpoint for root. Two failed efforts show the limits: a 16-hour, 148-action attempt on a Redis server failed, and more than 30,000 credential combinations over about six hours failed against a university GPU server. The agents also tried Log4Shell, SSRF against a Qdrant instance, and credential sprays on PostgreSQL and SSH. Amazon found the campaign because the attackers left their Cairn instance publicly accessible without authentication. Attribution is moderate confidence (Simplified Chinese prompts, China Standard Time script timestamps, target selection); Amazon assesses with high confidence that the MONAI Label flaw was a zero-day. Maintainers fixed it on 18 November (GitHub advisory, rated critical, versions through 0.8.5 affected; no CVE number yet). IOCs: 142.4.98.145, 142.4.107.34. No financial target was in this campaign, but it is the first documented case of AI agents finding — not just reusing — a zero-day and completing an intrusion autonomously, and it is directly relevant to banks running exposed AI applications and agent frameworks.

Exposed LiteLLM gateways scanned (NEW, reported 20 Nov). SANS Internet Storm Center honeypots observed scanning of LiteLLM’s MCP test endpoint, targeting CVE-2026-42271 chained with CVE-2026-48710 (see watchlist). Wiz found 294 of 3,074 internet-facing LiteLLM instances (9.6%) accepted a default master key or had no authentication. This is distinct from the KEV 18 November LiteLLM flaw CVE-2026-40217 (LLMjacking, due 2 December), whose attribution remains contested: GTIG attributes an LLMjacking intrusion to CVE-2026-40217; Corgea says the key-escalation technique is a separate bug fixed in 1.84.0 — do not present as settled. No new exposed-LLM-endpoint or LLM/API-key theft incident beyond the scanning was found in-window.

Korea’s FSS warned all 28 virtual-asset operators on 20 November that they could be held responsible for failing to address security vulnerabilities, as hackers increasingly exploit vulnerabilities using AI, and urged inspection of externally accessible systems, IT assets and APIs. The FSS warning is an enforcement signal, not a published finding.
