01 Executive summary

1 — Today is the Ivanti deadline. A patch alone does not close it.

The federal fix for CVE-2026-111357 is due today. CVE-2026-118527 is due 13 November. No new KEV entries were added on 31 October or 1 November. The catalogue is still version 2026.10.30 with 1,766 entries. Critique: that does not mean risk fell. The UTA0576 chain has been exploited since 3 October. It is still the main edge-VPN risk in this window. A patch stops future use of the flaw. It does not show whether the system was already breached.

2 — Binance is in recovery. Chainalysis was breached in a separate incident.

Binance resumed withdrawals in phases on 1 November, across 15 networks. It says it has replaced ~95% of the ~$6.3bn stolen, up from ~90%. A full proof-of-reserves report is expected on 3 November. Critique: the ~95% figure is Binance's own claim. It is not independently audited. A proof of reserves, when it comes, will show holdings on one date. It will not show all debts. Separately, Chainalysis said on 30 October, reported 1 November, that an attacker used “offensive agentic techniques” to reach customer names, emails, user IDs and role labels. That data could help target the investigators tracing the Binance funds. Chainalysis says investigation content, passwords, MFA secrets and session tokens were not accessed. It also says its incident is not related to Binance. Critique: those limits are also Chainalysis's own claims. They have not been independently checked in the sources read.

3 — New risks: a CVSS 10.0 cloud bypass, and an exploited payment flaw

Canonical LXD / MicroCloud CVE-2026-112816 (CVSS 10.0) was reported on 1 November. An attacker with network access to the HTTPS API can pose as a trusted client without its private key. Fixed snaps are out. Critique: a score of 10.0 shows how bad the flaw could be. It does not mean it is being used. No known exploitation was reported. That is different from Drupal Commerce CVE-2026-118539, which is confirmed as actively exploited and can affect vendors financially. No new FIN7, Scattered Spider, Carbanak, Akira, RansomHub or Cl0p attack on a BFSI victim was found in this window. No sale of access to a financial firm by an Initial Access Broker was found either. Critique: not finding a report is not proof that nothing happened.

02 Threat actor & campaign activity

Each entry gives the target, the method, and the impact. MITRE ATT&CK codes are shown only if a source published them. No source in this window did, so none are shown. Claims that are not proven are labelled as unverified.

TraderTraitor — North Korea, Lazarus cluster

Update · previously covered

Crypto exchange / virtual-asset finance · Global — Binance

TTPs / developments

No new MITRE list was published in the 1 November update. Withdrawals resumed in phases on 1 November, in the 06:00–09:00 UTC window, across 15 networks. Binance reports $4.649bn withdrawn and $1.01bn deposited in the first ~9.5 hours. That is a net outflow of ~$3.64bn, or ~2.1% of ~$171bn in assets under management. It says replacement is now ~95%. The last ~5% is in less-liquid tokens outside the top ten. The FBI list of 628 addresses to block, from 31 October, is unchanged. Critique: the flow and replacement figures come from Binance. They are not audited.

What to watch

Watch for phishing that copies Binance messages about withdrawals reopening. A full proof-of-reserves check is expected on 3 November. Critique: that check will not prove total debts. How the attacker first got in — said to be a DevOps laptop — is still not confirmed.

Impact: This is recovery, not a new theft. No new loss figure was reported. The main live risk is customers leaving. Critique: a high replacement share does not by itself prove the exchange is whole.

Unknown actor — Chainalysis breach

New · disclosed 30 Oct, reported 1 Nov

Blockchain intelligence / crypto-investigation services used by law enforcement, exchanges & financial institutions · Global

TTPs — as described, no MITRE mapping published

An outside actor used what Chainalysis calls “offensive agentic techniques”. The actor reached internal systems and tools, and accessed user IDs, full names, email addresses and role labels. Chainalysis says there is no evidence that investigation content, transaction or query results, clear-text passwords, MFA secrets or session tokens were accessed. Critique: “no evidence” is not the same as proof that nothing else was taken. The date it was found, the number of clients affected, the flaw used, and the tools reached are still not disclosed. The phrase “offensive agentic techniques” is also vague. It does not explain the method.

Why it matters

No funds were stolen here. Contact data was exposed. But names, roles and organisations are what an attacker needs for targeted phishing or voice fraud against investigators, compliance staff and law-enforcement contacts.

Impact: High risk of targeted phishing against people tracing the Binance funds. Chainalysis states clearly that this incident is not related to the Binance theft. Critique: that separation is Chainalysis's statement. It should be noted, but it is not independent proof.

Qilin — ransomware (RaaS)

Unverified claim

“Global Holding” — sector listed as “Other”; location and identity not established

TTPs / evidence cited

No MITRE list was in the reports. The listing cites HudsonRock-linked infostealer data: 1 employee credential and 1 third-party employee credential, 25 compromised passwords, 7 of them critical, and no cookies. The leak-site entry does not describe encryption, how much data was taken, a ransom demand, or screenshots. Critique: stolen passwords alone do not prove a ransomware breach.

Assessment

A leak-site listing dated 1 November is a claim. It is not proof of a breach, of encryption, or of data theft. Do not treat “Global Holding” as a confirmed victim in finance.

Impact: Not confirmed. The link to BFSI is indirect. The useful lesson is the path itself: stolen staff and third-party credentials can lead to ransomware. That path is worth defending against, even if this claim is false.

VYPR ransomware + help-desk vishing actors

Trend · Talos Q3, published 29 Oct

Finance tied as most-targeted sector — 18% of Talos IR engagements, tied with public administration · Global

TTPs — no MITRE list in the Talos summary read

Attackers posed as IT help-desk staff by phone, email and Teams. They used only names and email addresses. They talked victims into starting Microsoft Quick Assist or another remote tool. The tool then ran PowerShell to download and install an MSI file from a remote IP address. Endpoint protection was turned off with a vulnerable driver, a method called BYOVD. Data was taken over several days. VYPR is new. Talos describes it as not yet mature. It is built on leaked code linked to Conti, and it had no leak site in this case. Critique: this is one set of incident-response cases. It shows a method that works. It does not show how common the method is across all firms.

Q3 context

Ransomware and pre-ransomware cases rose to 27% of Talos Q3 work. The highest share in the prior four quarters was 20%. First access was by phishing in 37% of cases, and by flaws in public-facing apps in 33%. Critique: these are shares of Talos cases, not shares of all attacks worldwide.

Impact: No BFSI victim is named in this item. The value is in defence. Check help-desk identity well, and use phishing-resistant MFA — FIDO2 / WebAuthn or hardware keys — as Talos recommends. Treat any remote session that staff did not request as a high-priority alert.

Smishing / app-based fraud network — Delhi

Fraud · reported 1 Nov

Retail banking customers · India

Method

Fake SMS messages about traffic fines, deliveries and reward points were sent through a foreign-based app, “Wingo Cash”. The app was shared on WhatsApp. It sent 9–12 phishing SMS for each reward paid out. Money was moved through mule bank accounts.

Outcome

Delhi Police action was reported on 1 November: 5 arrests, ~3,300 SMS traced, and ~₹2 crore paid to over 41,000 app users in 3 days. Critique: police figures at arrest stage are allegations. They are not court findings.

Impact: 544 complaints, and over ₹10 crore said to have been defrauded. Critique: this is customer fraud. It is not a breach of a bank. The defence is to verify where SMS messages come from, and to warn customers about fine and reward lures.

Bank-card pickup scam actors — Guelph

Fraud · warning published 1 Nov

Banking customers, seniors · Guelph, Canada

Method

Callers pose as bank or credit-bureau staff. They claim there is suspicious activity. They ask for card details and PINs. They give a matching name and a code to sound real. They then send a person to the door to collect the cards, while the victim stays on the phone. The bulletin is dated 29 October. The warning was published in this window.

Police advice

Hang up. Call the bank on a number you trust. Banks do not ask customers to hand cards to a courier, or to share PINs.

Impact: Cards were then used for purchases worth thousands of dollars. Critique: a correct name and code do not prove the caller is from the bank. That is the core trick here.

Polymarket / DebugBear — supply-chain incident

Update · previously covered

Prediction-market / trading finance · International platform

Update

Polymarket's postmortem of 30 October, reported 1 November, puts total compensation at ~$3.9m. That is ~$2.7m to 726 drained wallets, plus ~$1.19m to ~5,500 wallets affected by the trading pause. By 30 October, 689 of 726 wallets had been fully refunded. The other 37 were to be paid as they were verified. Critique: these are the company's own figures. They are clearer than press estimates, but they are not an outside audit.

Root cause — preliminary

Second-hand reports link the script compromise to a DNS record change through DebugBear's registrar. They report no evidence of a breach inside DebugBear itself. Polymarket has not confirmed the vendor name. Critique: without that confirmation, the root cause should be treated as likely, not settled.

Impact: The loss figure now rests on the company's own numbers. The root cause does not. It is still early and second-hand. The lesson on third-party scripts, from the 1 November briefing, still stands.

04 Indicators & defensive actions

Signs to look for come first. Then steps for the SOC and for Vulnerability Management, in priority order. No hash or IP list is invented here. None was published in this window, except the FBI address list carried from 31 October.

Notable indicators & behavioural indicators
  • Ivanti chain (deadline today): Look for files on a Connect Secure appliance that should not be there, admin logins, and access to AD credentials. Use has been seen since 3 October, even on fully patched appliances (UTA0576). A passing Integrity Checker is not proof of safety.
  • LXD / MicroCloud: Look for an LXD HTTPS API that can be reached from the internet (core.https_address set), trusted-client sessions you did not expect, and VM or cluster actions from a client that showed only a public certificate. After the refresh, check snap list lxd microcloud against 6.10.1 / 5.21.9 / 5.0.11 / 4.0.15 and MicroCloud 3.4.1 / 2.1.5.
  • Drupal Commerce: Look for odd entries in cart, checkout and order logs, changed prices or payment records, and access without login to protected Commerce routes or fields. Check orders from before the patch.
  • Android “SMS of Death”: Look for repeated crashes of com.android.phone with no clear cause, loss of mobile service with no visible message, and repeated restarts after ~20 messages. This is loss of service, not theft of credentials.
  • Chainalysis exposure: Look for phishing, voice fraud and SMS fraud that copy Chainalysis. Signs include look-alike domains, copied branding, and password-reset prompts you did not expect. Messages may use real names, roles and organisations. Treat them as suspect even if the names are correct. A correct name is not proof.
  • Binance reopening: Look for phishing that copies notices about withdrawals reopening. Check the official withdrawal and network-status page for each asset and network pair before acting.
  • Help-desk vishing (Talos Q3 pattern): Look for help-desk calls, emails or Teams messages you did not request, requests to start Quick Assist or another remote tool, PowerShell downloads of MSI files from remote IPs right after a remote session, and BYOVD driver loads.
  • Fraud: Look for fake SMS about fines, deliveries or rewards (India pattern), and for bank or credit-bureau calls that ask for PINs and then send a person to collect cards at the door (Canada pattern).
  • Carried indicator set: The FBI list of 628 addresses to block, from 31 October (608 ETH, 19 BTC, 1 SOL), for Binance / TraderTraitor funds, is unchanged in this window.

SOC — prioritised

  1. Close Ivanti today: Hunt for use of AD credentials and for signs on the appliance from the October chain window. Rebuild suspect appliances. Only then close CVE-2026-111357. Plan CVE-2026-118527 for 13 Nov. Do not lower its priority because of its 4.3 score. In the chain, it matters more than the score suggests.
  2. Check that the 1 Nov deadlines are truly closed. That means Cisco CVE-2026-76418 and GitLab CVE-2026-97229, including the Ruby version. Also confirm Siemens V4.0.9.8 by tomorrow, 3 Nov.
  3. Limit and patch LXD / MicroCloud APIs now: Remove internet access where you can. Refresh to the fixed snaps. Check the version that is actually installed. Alert on new trusted-client sessions.
  4. Check Drupal Commerce orders and patch to 3.3.13 / 8.x-2.41. Reconcile price and payment-record changes from before the patch.
  5. Brief investigators, compliance staff and crypto desks on the Chainalysis contact-data exposure and on Binance reopening phishing. Enforce two rules: call back on a known number, and never share a one-time code or PIN.
  6. Protect the help desk from voice fraud: Require a ticket and identity check before any remote session or MFA reset. Use phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2 / WebAuthn / hardware keys). Alert on Quick Assist or other remote tools running on finance computers.

Vulnerability Management — prioritised

  1. Today, 2 Nov: Ivanti CVE-2026-111357 → 22.8R2.6 / 25.1.1.3. Hunt for compromise, and rebuild where signs are found.
  2. By 3 Nov: Siemens CVE-2026-110936 → V4.0.9.8. By 11 Nov: VMware ESX CVE-2026-41709. By 13 Nov: Ivanti CVE-2026-118527.
  3. Now: LXD / MicroCloud fixed snaps. Check the version installed, not just that an update ran. Drupal Commerce 3.3.13 / 8.x-2.41. Apache Fineract 1.16.1, where used. Check Android patch levels again once Google's November bulletin, due today, is out. Plan to replace or upgrade Android 12/13 devices that have no fix.
  4. Third parties and their suppliers: Ask Chainalysis for a written statement of scope for your organisation, once it issues its per-client breakdown. Ask the same of any vendor whose customer contact data was exposed. Add approval for third-party script changes — the Polymarket lesson — and controls on API credentials to vendor risk reviews.
  5. Do not close on version number alone if a Cisco, GitLab or Ivanti system was on the internet during a known exploitation window. Require a log review. For Ivanti and GitLab, also rotate credentials and tokens if any sign of use is found.
