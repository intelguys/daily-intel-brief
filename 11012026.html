01 — Executive Summary

Three things that matter most

A short summary only. The detail, and the limits of each claim, are in the sections below. Do not act on a headline figure without reading its limits.

1

State-sponsored crypto theft is the biggest event — act on the laundering trail now

On 31 Oct the FBI attributed the ~$6–6.4bn Binance theft of ~29 Oct to North Korea’s TraderTraitor (Lazarus cluster) and published 628 wallet addresses for blocking. The actors are converting proceeds mainly to ETH and moving them through DeFi and instant swaps. Binance said it had replenished ~90% of losses, frozen ~$3bn with partners, and planned to reopen withdrawals on 1 Nov.

Critique: the attribution is a formal law-enforcement claim, but the evidence behind it is not public in full, so it cannot be checked independently here. The loss total is not settled — sources give $6bn, $6.3bn and $6.4bn. The replenishment and freeze figures come from Binance and its partners, not from an audit. And the FBI release does not explain how the DevOps laptop was compromised, so the way in is still unknown.

2

The data incidents came through vendors, not through core banking systems

On 31 Oct, NTT Data suspended STdirectServices after unauthorised access to an unnamed subcontractor’s SaaS platform exposed Japanese bond buyers’ names and purchase amounts (Rakuten up to 362, SBI Securities 1,169, MUFG Morgan Stanley 95, Kintetsu total reported 2,678 — these counts overlap and must not be added together). Separately, AU Small Finance Bank said it was investigating a possible leak of limited vehicle-loan data on a Salesforce-hosted SaaS platform, after an unverified ScarlettGroup claim.

Critique: in both cases the firms say core systems were not accessed. That is their own statement. It has not been independently verified, and the subcontractor in the NTT case is not even named. The AU claim may not be a breach at all. What is clear is the fraud risk: phishing and vishing that quote real names, amounts and loan details. That is a risk of misuse of real data, not proof of account takeover.

3

The 1 November KEV deadline is the patching emergency — and the Ivanti chain is the trap

Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418 (8.6) and GitLab CVE-2026-97229 (10.0) both had a 1 Nov federal remediation date. Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-111357 is due 2 Nov and CVE-2026-118527 on 13 Nov. The Ivanti flaws chain with the 13 Oct CVE-2026-104935 to reach root, and the chain was used against a fully patched appliance.

Critique: a passed deadline does not mean every system is now safe. It only means the date has passed. A GitLab upgrade alone does not fix custom-Ruby builds, and a passing Ivanti Integrity Checker result does not prove an appliance was never touched. Patching without a compromise hunt is not enough.

Unverified claim

Banking, India — AU Small Finance Bank, vehicle-loan customers, via Salesforce-hosted SaaS.

No MITRE mapping published in sources.

Claimed access to KYC, PAN/Aadhaar, credit reports, bank statements, loan agreements, CRM, employee and audit-log data, with threat to leak.

Prediction-market / trading finance, international platform (US platform reportedly unaffected).

No MITRE list published in the Hudson Rock excerpt.

Malicious JavaScript served from a compromised third-party RUM script; hunted logged-in users’ trading tools / portfolio in the browser; sold positions and moved proceeds, or sent position tokens to an attacker-set address; reused already-authenticated session authority. Polymarket / DebugBear

Malicious third-party RUM JavaScript reading logged-in trading tools / portfolio, selling positions or sending tokens to a hardcoded fallback address (Arkham-linked wallet holding ~$2.65m). Watch for: any third-party script change on a trading/payment page, a production build note removing a vendor script (Polymarket build timestamped 17:59 UTC), and unexpected position sales with no credential-theft artefact.

Ivanti chain

Unexpected files on a Connect Secure appliance, admin logins and AD credential access, exploitation from 3 Oct against fully patched appliances (UTA0576). A passing Integrity Checker is NOT a clean bill of health.

GitLab

CI/CD config changes from newly created / untrusted accounts, worker crashes (Alpine: 8 crashes over 20–21 Oct), and downloads of the project’s container image by an unknown party (exploit tuning). Check both GitLab version AND bundled Ruby version.

Cisco ASA / FTD

Unexpected firewall reloads (show crashinfo) on SSL VPN devices with secondary authentication in any tunnel group, including DefaultWEBVPNGroup.

PAN-OS / PHP

Internet-reachable management interfaces, admin / config-log changes, PHP hosts with unpatched branches — treat any internet-exposed PAN-OS management interface as possibly compromised even after hotfixing.

Social indicators — NTT / AU / SBI exposures

No technical IOCs published. The indicator is social: phishing / vishing / smishing quoting a real bond name, purchase amount (e.g. ¥1m+ Kintetsu bond), loan amount, vehicle or EMI date, CIF ID or branch. Any message about “perk”, “compensation”, KYC update or “security check” referencing those details should be treated as suspect even if the caller knows correct personal data.

Critique: this warning rests on the firms’ statements about which data was exposed. If more data was taken than stated, the same messages become more convincing, not less. Correct personal details are not proof that a caller is genuine.

06 — Defensive Actions

Prioritised — in order

Two queues: SOC hunts and blocks today; Vulnerability Management closes each flaw and proves closure. “Patched” is a claim too — it should be checked, not just recorded.

🛡 SOC — do first

  1. Ingest and block the FBI TraderTraitor list today628 addresses + derived-transaction screening; alert on ETH-conversion / DeFi-swap patterns; brief crypto/fraud desks that exact-match lists will lag address rotation.
  2. Hunt, don’t just patch — Ivanti, GitLab, CiscoReview Ivanti admin logins/files and AD credential use; GitLab CI changes by new accounts, worker crashes and token use; Cisco unexpected reloads. Rotate runner/deploy tokens and privileged credentials where any sign of exploitation is found.
  3. Freeze third-party scripts on customer-facing finance pagesInventory all loaded vendor scripts (the DebugBear lesson), enforce subresource integrity / change approval, alert on script-content change or unexpected authenticated-session transactions with no login anomaly.
  4. Raise phishing / vishing defences for NTT / AU / SBI populationsWarn customers and front-line staff callers may quote real bonds, loans, CIF IDs and branches; enforce callback-to-known-number, never-ask-for-OTP/PIN/password rules; monitor lookalike domains and WhatsApp payment instructions.
  5. Treat exposed PAN-OS mgmt & PHP hosts as assumed-compromisedUntil log review is complete; restrict management to a jump host with permitted-IP allowlisting only.
  6. Lock down privileged endpoints that can reach signing / withdrawal systemsDevOps laptops, service containers and hot-wallet signing infrastructure need phishing-resistant MFA, EDR, and separation so one laptop cannot submit unauthorised withdrawals; rehearse withdrawal-freeze and stablecoin-freeze playbooks.

Fraud & attack-vector watch — honest scope

Fraud reporting in this window centred on vishing, payment diversion, and API-credential abuse. It did not include a newly reported mobile banking trojan, infostealer family, or DDoS-extortion campaign against a bank. Account-takeover risk here sits downstream of exposed KYC / bond / loan data (AU, NTT) and of authenticated-session abuse (Polymarket). It is not a confirmed credential-stuffing wave. Keep monitoring, but do not re-label these incidents as proof of account takeover.

Could not verify

  • ScarlettGroup / AU SFB: The breach itself is unconfirmed. Salesforce denies a platform vulnerability. The affected count, the data volume and the way in are all undisclosed.
