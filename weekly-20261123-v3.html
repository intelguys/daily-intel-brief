BFSI cyber · Asia/Singapore · Monday 16 November to Monday 23 November 2026.

Bottom line

Supplier failure defined the week: one compromise at a marketing platform, a middleware vendor, or a credit-union provider became dozens of firms' incidents. AI moved from research into confirmed operations: agents exploited a zero-day alone, cloned voices opened a ransomware gang's way in, and agentic tooling produced named victims. The most dangerous vulnerability news was old: a vCenter zero-day exploited since September, and 13 of the 20 tracked flaws were already exploited. Regulators turned incidents into duties, so for BFSI the perimeter is now the supplier's: rotate vendor keys, verify help-desk calls, and patch exploited flaws first.

  1. Close the four KEV items due today. Patch VMware vCenter, Apache Roller, SAP NetWeaver and Cisco Expressway. §1 Sector impact

Banks, insurers, payments and fintech — what the week means for the sector.

Third-party failure was the week's spine. One compromise became many firms' incidents: a marketing platform, a middleware vendor, a payments contractor and a credit-union provider each turned a single breach into sector-wide exposure. §1.1 — Plaid KARTE: one marketing platform, about 2,200 companies' data

Takeaway: A marketing-platform breach became a credential incident for 754 client companies, with a second disclosure wave due from 24 November.

Plaid's KARTE platform was attacked on 13 November. A vulnerability behind a public data-retrieval route was exploited. Stolen credentials then reached the KARTE admin console. Plaid completed its investigation on 19 November and published it on 20 November. No attribution has been published.

Data from about 2,200 companies leaked or may have leaked, in four overlapping groups. Integration credentials (secret keys and access tokens) were exposed for 754 client companies. Data customers had linked into KARTE was exposed for 570 companies. End-user behaviour data was exposed for 487 companies. Cached data was treated as potentially leaked for up to 409 companies. That last figure is an estimate: the cache is overwritten quickly, so Plaid cannot tell which items were actually read. No key misuse had been disclosed as of 20 November.

KARTE stays offline, with a staged restart targeted for mid-January 2027. Per-company handovers begin on 24 November. Plaid forecasts a ¥2 billion loss.

§1.2 — Rakuten Securities: up to about 15.72 million accounts

Takeaway: A test environment holding real customer data became a production-grade incident, with regulator and legal exposure arriving in the same week.

Rakuten Securities disclosed on 17 November that customer data in a test-environment system may have leaked. It confirmed the leak on 18 November. Possible unauthorised access was detected on 16 November. Test-environment data may have leaked on 11 November. The cause is under investigation.

Up to about 11.25 million accounts' login IDs were exposed. Up to about 4.47 million accounts had names, dates of birth, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, employers, withdrawal bank accounts, declared income and financial-asset data exposed. The two groups do not overlap. The maximum is therefore about 15.72 million accounts. No passwords or PINs are confirmed leaked. No unauthorised transactions attributable to the incident have been confirmed.

Japan's Financial Services Agency issued a reporting order on 19 November. Financial Services Minister Satsuki Katayama addressed the leak on 20 November. The FSA will consider a business improvement order if information-management flaws are found. Rakuten Bank shares fell about 15% on 18 November. A lawyer-led class-action recruitment site passed 20,000 registrants by 14:00 JST on 20 November. A fake "compensation policy" screenshot is circulating. Rakuten has announced no compensation, and contacts asking for lawsuit fees are fake.

Confidence: High — company disclosure, regulator action and market reaction are all confirmed. Priority: Monitor. So what for BFSI: Test environments holding real customer data need production-grade controls; the FSA's next step is the signal to watch. Sources: Reuters Japan — Rakuten Securities announcement (17 Nov, translated from Japanese) · NHK WORLD-JAPAN — Rakuten Securities possible leak (17 Nov) Still unknown: Whether the FSA issues a business improvement order.

§1.3 — Korea: the FSI audit becomes a sector-wide inspection

Takeaway: A breach at the sector's own assessment body became a 274-firm regulatory inspection, while the breach wave kept finding new victims through peripheral systems.

The Financial Security Institute (FSI) breach came before this window. A webshell attack at 13:54 on 9 November failed. Then an SMS-authentication bypass created a fake FSS-level account. The Financial Services Commission (FSC) gave that timeline to the National Assembly on 13 November. Its special audit of the FSI began on 16 November. The FSI evaluation system was still shut down at the end of the window, with no restoration date. Appeals from 2,912 firms in the assessment population are blocked. The wider Korean wave covers 37 firms and about 473,000 records in one month.

On 17 November the Financial Supervisory Service (FSS) called in CISOs and security officers from 227 electronic-finance and 47 online investment-linked firms. That is 274 firms in total. They face an intensive external-attack-surface inspection, with first-round self-assessments due on 30 November. The FSS warned of strict accountability if a breach followed a poorly done inspection. It also began surprise on-site inspections of high-risk branches at four banks, the first in about ten years. Intrusion attempts through external contact points continued, above all at small and mid-sized operators. The FSC will require large firms to run vulnerability assessments twice a year instead of once. They must also identify and classify all IT assets. A wider programme announced on 5 November covers full inspections of external access points at about 580 financial companies.

New incidents were disclosed the same day: Spoon (Giftiell, about 18,000 people's data) and Zoomfund (about KRW 300 million in customer deposits).

§1.10 — BankWeaver: bank middleware turned into a backdoor

Takeaway: The attacker needed no vulnerability: a misconfigured authentication service, open to the internet, handed over an administrator token.

Forescout Research (Vedere Labs) disclosed "BankWeaver" on 19 November. Attackers abused a Temenos Quantum Fabric authentication service configured for anonymous access and reachable from the internet. They exchanged an anonymous token for an administrator token without credentials. They then deployed two malicious Java components: FabricShell for command execution and database queries, and FabricShadow for host reconnaissance and data exfiltration. One unnamed financial services institution was confirmed compromised, in a breach running from early September 2026. Six further banks appear to share the same exposure, but compromise has not been confirmed for them. Temenos confirmed the cause is a misconfiguration. No MITRE IDs were published.

The actor is unnamed and no attribution has been published. Forescout identified over 30 IP addresses used during the campaign, rotated as source IPs. Most are compromised UK residential devices: network-attached storage and home routers with exposed CWMP. A handful are VPS nodes, from providers such as tzulo and Traefik/Docker. The actors mixed compromised devices and victim servers to route traffic. They used victim networks as staging points and stored reverse-shell scripts on free shared hosting services. One residential-proxy IP was seen downloading an ELF file designed to fingerprint the device, persist via BitTorrent DHT and co-opt the host into a botnet. Forescout says the botnet may not be directly related to the BankWeaver actor, since bots can be rented for a campaign, and the relationship is still under investigation.

FabricShell contained hardcoded PostgreSQL connection details and credentials for another victim. FabricShadow contained a hardcoded URL for the same victim's Red Hat Process Automation Manager, a webshell used as a staging server. That is evidence the actor reuses victim infrastructure. It is not evidence those victims were the same campaign's targets.

Confidence: High — vendor research and vendor confirmation of the misconfiguration; the actor is unnamed and the six further banks are exposure, not confirmed compromise. Priority: Act. So what for BFSI: Banks running Temenos Quantum Fabric should check whether their authentication service accepts anonymous access and is internet-reachable, and hunt for FabricShell and FabricShadow. Sources: Forescout Research — BankWeaver (19 Nov)

§1.11 — OPTIMJACK: a trusted script becomes a skimmer on 178 organisations' sites

Takeaway: No bank was breached, yet bank customers were skimmed on bank websites through a trusted script the banks themselves had chosen.

On 16 November, attackers used a vulnerability in Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform. The flaw was in the account signup flow. It allowed administrator impersonation and unauthorised changes to Project JavaScript. Researcher Scott Helme (Report URI) identified 188 infected snippets associated with 178 organisations. They include HSBC, Equifax, Ledger, Zoom, CNN, Salesforce, GitLab and IKEA. The count is a floor: Report URI could only check the snippets it found in time, and Optimizely has not published its own number. Optimizely's own website and login portal snippets carried the code too. 32 of the 178 organisations were in banking and finance.

The payload (track.js) replaced cryptocurrency wallet addresses in page text, form fields, links, images, the clipboard and outgoing requests. A card skimmer added at 13:43 UTC watched form submissions and POST requests for card number, expiry and CVV. It sent them to 1688360.cc/card.php. The payload also collected non-HttpOnly cookies and page screenshots. It attempted to deliver exploit kits to some iPhone and iPad users. It could not read card fields inside cross-origin hosted payment iframes. Optimizely fixed the vulnerability and removed the injected code the same day. The campaign was largely cleaned by 05:52 SGT on 17 November. Most sites were exposed for about 16 hours. No organisation's own servers were breached.

Crypto amounts taken are unreconciled ($7,040 vs $2,300 in different reports). Neither figure is used here as the total. The card-loss count is unknown. Exposure to the script does not by itself prove theft.

How the attacker got write access to so many unrelated customer projects is not established. An Optimizely spokesperson said the flaw was in the signup flow, but that does not explain the mechanism enabling cross-project modification. Reported (researcher analysis) technical links connect the incident to an actor that operated an injector domain in September 2026 against Turkish websites. §2.1 — United Kingdom: critical third parties designated, cyber-resilience consultation opened

Takeaway: Three UK regulators published one joint cyber-resilience consultation, while the Treasury widened critical-third-party oversight to nine firms.

HM Treasury made the second designation tranche under the Critical Third Parties regime on 16 November. The order is SI 2026/1203, in force on 23 November. Five firms were designated: BlackRock Financial Management, Bloomberg, Bottomline Technologies, Broadridge Financial Solutions and Form3 UK. They join the four cloud providers designated in July. The total is now nine, with joint Bank of England / PRA / FCA oversight of their systemic services.

On 19 November the Bank of England, the Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) and the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) jointly published a consultation. It covers cyber risk and resilience management (PRA CP17/26, FCA GC26/9). It sets out principles-based, outcome-focused expectations for how firms manage cyber risk and recover from incidents. It consolidates existing requirements and supervisory lessons rather than introducing new rules. It is accompanied by separate "effective practices" documents on AI and quantum risks. The consultation closes on Friday 26 February 2027, with final guidance expected in 2027. The draft guidance applies to banks, insurers, financial market infrastructures and FCA-regulated firms subject to existing operational-resilience requirements. It covers six areas: governance; identification and assessment; protection; detection; response and recovery; and assurance and continuous improvement. It is aligned with the NCSC's Cyber Assessment Framework.

The Bank's latest Systemic Risk Survey cited cyber as one of the risks that would have the greatest impact on the UK financial system. October 2026 saw the second-highest monthly number of cyber incidents reported to the FCA in five years. That was 52% above the previous 18-month peak in May 2025.

Confidence: High — Treasury designation and regulator consultation are primary sources. Priority: Context. So what for BFSI: UK firms in scope should respond by 26 February 2027 and treat the AI and quantum effective practices as the direction of travel for supervisory expectations. §2.2 — Singapore: MAS draws a line on AI agents; national CERT warns on Apache

Takeaway: Singapore regulated both ends of the AI question in one week: agents as financial actors that cannot yet prove their authority, and a widely used web server under active exploitation.

On 19 November, speaking at the Singapore FinTech Festival, Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) managing director Chia Der Jiun gave a warning. Consumers should not give personal AI agents the login credentials to their financial accounts. There is insufficient assurance that such access can be provided safely, he said. MAS says the practice is already happening. A bank receiving instructions from an agent may not know whether the agent has the customer's full authorisation. It may not know whether the agent is staying inside its mandate, or who is responsible if it transacts incorrectly or beyond its instructions.

MAS will facilitate a "know your agent" protocol for trusted information exchange between customer agents and financial institutions. It will cover the agent's identity, whom it represents, what authority it has, what mandate it operates under, and its origins and reliability. The protocol is the next phase of MAS's Safeguards for Agentic Finance at Runtime (SAFR) programme. MAS will also set up an Agentic Money Sandbox with the Future of Finance Institute, for corporate treasury management and payment services with tokenised deposits. No delivery date was given for the protocol, and no start date for the sandbox. Agents could move money and allocate assets more quickly, Chia warned, raising liquidity and financial-stability risks. Financial institutions may need to impose limits on agent-directed transactions.

The MAS speech text itself was not retrieved verbatim in this run. This account rests on the Straits Times report of the 19 November keynote, corroborated by Fintech News Singapore and TechNode. Context: the CSA / GovTech / IMDA joint advisory AD-2026-015 (6 November, pre-window standing guidance) already warns against giving personal AI agents one-time passwords. §4.5 — POS malware: over a million cards stolen from unpatched shop tills

Takeaway: researchers found an exposed attacker server holding a POS-malware campaign's full workings. Its carding panel held at least one million unique card records. The compromised terminals had not been updated for at least one year and nine months.

Cybernews reported on 18 November that researchers found an exposed attacker server. It held the complete workings of a campaign against point-of-sale terminals: malware source code, exploitation tools, and a carding panel named "VINY JUNIOR TRILHAS". The panel stored at least one million unique credit and debit card records: primary account numbers, validity periods, bank identification numbers, issuing countries and banks.

The server held records for 782 compromised systems across more than 20 countries, most of them in Brazil. The stolen cards skew toward Brazilian banks. The operator is assessed as Brazilian, based on the language in recovered documentation.

§4.6 — RIKEN Fugaku: a status dashboard became a foothold into Japan's flagship supercomputer

Takeaway: intruders entered through an internet-facing status dashboard. The haul that matters is not the user records but 430 credentials to other systems, already being tried elsewhere.

RIKEN shut down Fugaku, Japan's flagship supercomputer, after intruders got into its operations network. Between 10 and 13 November, the intruders operated several internal servers and planted malicious programs.

RIKEN's first notice, on 16 November, put up to 4,597 users' data at risk. That covered Fugaku and the Rikyu AI supercomputer. Its second report, on 20 November, says the intruders did access the user data. RIKEN now treats it as leaked.

For 4,222 Fugaku users, the leaked data covers names, email addresses and IDs. It also covers addresses, affiliations, phone numbers, and a record of the type of identity document used. The documents themselves were not taken. A further 375 Rikyu users bring the at-risk total to 4,597.

More seriously, the intruders collected and moved 430 credentials for outside systems. These were SSH private keys and access keys researchers had stored in home directories to connect to other supercomputers and services. 233 of those keys had no passphrase. RIKEN says the intruders have already tried to use some of them against other systems.

Fugaku was shut down within about 12 hours of the intrusion being confirmed. Confirmation came on 13 November at 16:33; shutdown followed on 14 November at 04:30. Every user's login key was invalidated. Keys taken from Fugaku still have to be revoked at each place they open, by the researchers who put them there.

RIKEN has not named the dashboard software, the vulnerability or the attackers. It has not given a restart date. Fugaku will restart only after its public services are separated from the management network. No evidence of external misuse has been confirmed beyond the credential-use attempts.

Confidence: High — RIKEN's 16 and 20 November notices, as reported by Japan Cyber Watch. Priority: Monitor. So what for BFSI: a low-value internet-facing page leading to a credential store that opens other systems is exactly how the week's bank-adjacent breaches worked. Treat a shared environment holding keys to other systems as a multi-system incident if compromised. §6.1 — Dragos canary research: prompt theft from inside AI inference providers

Takeaway: Attackers were already inside at least three AI inference providers, stealing prompts and credentials. A Zero Data Retention flag did not stop them.

Dragos published five months of canary-token research on 19 November. About two million prompts carried unique URLs and canary credentials to widely used AI services between July and November 2026. The credentials included AWS keys, DNS names, and git and SSH targets. Attackers were stealing data inside at least three AI inference providers. The earliest breach began in mid-July. None of the three knew until Dragos told them.

Marketplace resellers could view and retain customer prompts through standard logging. No exploit was needed. Providers appeared not to honour privacy settings, including Zero Data Retention. Dragos put it plainly: "A privacy flag doesn't help when the attacker is already inside."

In the largest breach, a couple of hundred planted AWS keys were stolen in short windows in late October. Every request in those windows was copied, including ones rejected before a model ran. The thief tested keys within hours. The testing drove tens of thousands of AWS API calls. It listed S3 buckets, searched Secrets Manager and Parameter Store, tried creating Bedrock API keys, and added IAM users.

On 1 November the same attacker used ARTEX, the LLM-driven penetration-testing framework. It was used "only to confirm that keys it already held were still live". Its user agents, artex-agent and artex-judge-r121, appear in CloudTrail. Dragos says they appear in no public indicator list.

A Chinese AI lab fetched secret URLs from more than half of the prompts sent to its models. That share rose to roughly two-thirds of requests marked no-collection or Zero Data Retention. The lab is unnamed.

§6.3 — Microsoft agent-framework PyPI hijack: a Cairn-labelled credential stealer

Takeaway: For about an hour, a routine pip install of Microsoft's official AI-agent SDK pulled a credential stealer published through Microsoft's own release pipeline.

A malicious version 1.26.1 of Microsoft's official agent-framework Python package was uploaded to PyPI at 20:35 UTC on 17 November. Version 1.26.2 followed at 21:08 UTC. Both were unavailable by about 21:30 UTC. The most recent safe version is 1.26.0.

Wiz assesses that a client certificate and private key for Microsoft's Engineering System Release Pipeline (ESRP) were compromised to publish them. The stealer drops .pth files into the site-packages root. The files are agent_framework_meta.pth and af_runtime_diag.pth. They run whenever Python starts in that environment, even if agent-framework is never imported.

The stealer harvested environment variables and credential files from AI-agent development environments. The harvest included LLM API keys and Hugging Face tokens. It also requested fresh identity tokens from GitHub Actions, Azure DevOps, and Azure.

The tooling identified itself as "Cairn", an open-source AI penetration-testing framework. It staged the package at /tmp/cairn_pkg and sent a CAIRN_ESRP_PROBE2 connectivity probe. That self-identification is the tooling's own label, not proof of the operator's identity.

Wiz could read only the 50 requests stored on the attacker's free-tier webhook. Four were from the attacker, 29 from sandboxes or scanners, and 17 from three distinct victims. Three victim exfiltrations were observed in that sample. That is not a verified victim total. Microsoft had published no security advisory by the end of the window.

Confidence: High — Wiz primary research with precise timestamps; the ESRP compromise is assessed, not confirmed.

Priority: Act.

So what for BFSI: Anyone who installed 1.26.1 or 1.26.2 should rebuild from 1.26.0 and rotate every secret the environment could reach.

Still unknown: How the ESRP certificate and key were compromised; the full victim count beyond the three observed.

§6.5 — Cairn / MONAI Label: autonomous AI agents find and exploit a zero-day

Takeaway: The first confirmed case of AI agents autonomously finding and exploiting a zero-day in the wild. Given one instruction, "obtain shell access", Cairn agents found a flaw in MONAI Label and reached root.

Amazon Threat Intelligence reported the case on 20 November, on the AWS Security Blog, published by Amazon CISO CJ Moses. The operator's own Cairn control panel was exposed on the internet without authentication. Amazon found it on 10 September. It revealed task settings, prompts, and run history.

The campaign ran about 48 hours from 8 September. It targeted university IP addresses in East Asia outside mainland China. The agents planned, executed, and adjusted their approach themselves. Against a MONAI Label server, they mapped the API and found an upload endpoint with a filename parameter. They went from mapping the API to arbitrary file write in about 11 minutes. In a later session, they planted a Python reverse shell in sitecustomize.py and triggered a training job to spawn a Python process as root.

The flaw is a path traversal in PUT /datastore/image and PUT /datastore/label. It affects MONAI Label 0.8.5 and earlier, all versions. Authentication is off by default. With authentication on, the ANNOTATOR role is enough. It is now CVE-2026-133968, rated 9.8. The source fix was merged on 19 October. Version 0.8.6 shipped on 22 November, the first packaged release containing the fix. It requires Python 3.11 or later and drops 3.9 and 3.10. Run it unprivileged. Any instance reachable from an untrusted network before 22 November should be treated as compromised until checked.

Amazon assesses with high confidence that it was a zero-day. It assesses with moderate confidence that the actor is China-nexus. The basis is Simplified Chinese prompts, China Standard Time script timestamps, and targeting of universities likely of strategic interest to China. No group is named. No patient scans or clinical systems were touched.

The "first confirmed case" reading is Amazon's own reconstruction from the exposed panel. It has not been independently confirmed. A second university network the actor tunnelled into is inferred, not confirmed, as compromised.

The agents also failed elsewhere in the same campaign. A 16-hour, 148-action attempt against an open Redis instance failed. A six-hour GPU-server credential spray of more than 30,000 combinations failed. Log4Shell injection and SSRF attempts failed. The agents persist without human direction, and against a soft target they find and exploit a novel flaw in minutes. Amazon's words: "An agent that discovers and exploits a zero-day in minutes completes its activity faster than many security operations centers can manually triage an alert."

Gambit Security documented a separate, financially motivated actor using Cairn against online retailers in September. That campaign took more than 600,000 cards. It shows Cairn in criminal as well as espionage use.

Technical entry (CVE, scores, fix): see §3.20.

Confidence: High — Amazon Threat Intelligence primary; the zero-day assessment is high-confidence. The China-nexus attribution is moderate-confidence, and the account is Amazon's own reconstruction.

Priority: Act.

So what for BFSI: No financial target was hit. But any agent or automation framework a bank or vendor runs must never be internet-reachable with authentication off.

Still unknown: Whether the second university network was compromised; whether other exposed MONAI Label instances were exploited.

§6.6 — ARTEX operators: several actors, one shared tool

Takeaway: ARTEX produced named victims this week. In every case the entry point was an ordinary credential failure: an unretired vendor key, or a dormant bot account with no second factor.

ARTEX is an open-source, LLM-driven autonomous penetration-testing framework. Its developer is Autumn-27. It was published in July 2026, and its repository was taken down and closed-sourced in October 2026. Forks and a maintained successor, BaaRai/ARTEX, circulate. Shared tooling does not mean a shared actor.

Earlier vendor work named three actors, all pre-window background. Storm-0940 is a PRC actor named by Microsoft for the ConnectWise campaign. VAPOR PANDA, named by CrowdStrike, hit more than 200 organisations in at least 14 countries, with 9 confirmed post-exploitation, and is explicitly separate from Storm-0940. Storm-3358 is Cambodia-based and targets Southeast Asia financials. The FBI's FLASH of 13 November warned ARTEX had been in malicious use since at least August 2026. Some intrusions reached privileged access in under three hours.

Barracuda — an unretired vendor key

Barracuda disclosed the intrusion on 11 November, from an advisory dated 10 November and updated 13 November. An attacker entered Barracuda's cloud environment with a cloud access key issued to an outside AI vendor for a finished project. Barracuda never retired the key. The vendor's development environment was compromised in October, and the key sat in a configuration file in the vendor's GitHub repository.

The key was first used at 04:33 UTC on 1 November. The last activity was at 23:31 UTC on 2 November. In about 44 hours the attacker moved through about ten cloud accounts, chaining one secret to the next. The chain ran through a data-analytics environment, a source-code access token, and Kubernetes clusters. The attacker left a backdoor in a Kubernetes cluster. An attempt to create a new cloud administrator identity was blocked. Barracuda discovered the activity on 4 November.

The attacker also searched for Ethereum keystore files, seed phrases, and private keys. Barracuda believes the attack was financially motivated. Barracuda matched the activity to ARTEX from probe names, the order of operations, and built-in credential-hunting routines. The match is a pattern match to the tool, not an attribution to an operator.

The attacker reached a system storing Barracuda XDR customer integration credentials. Barracuda found no evidence they were accessed or stolen. Potentially affected Managed XDR Cloud and Monitored Endpoint customers were asked to revoke and recreate their credentials.

Bitrise — a dormant bot account with no second factor

Bitrise published its incident report on 19 November. An operator pointed ARTEX at the mobile CI/CD platform between 25 October and 18 November. Its agents spent three days in October mapping the public surface, under user agents claiming an "authorised pentest". They returned on 12 November as an ordinary customer, running their own builds and testing cross-customer access.

On 13 November at 11:46:48 UTC, the attacker signed in to a dormant internal test bot account. It had been unused since 2022 and had no two-factor authentication. The sign-in used a valid username and password. Less than eleven minutes later, the agents were using cloud keys from that workspace's projects. Over the next ten hours, they chained one credential into the next: signing files, source code, infrastructure state, and build logs, across 28 private repositories.

Files in 15 customer workspaces were accessed without authorisation. They included one code-signing certificate file, one provisioning profile, and 13 diagnostic build logs from May 2026. One log held a credential, and another held an email address. The code-signing certificate's password was not reached. All 15 customers were contacted.

Bitrise detected the activity at 16:32 UTC and switched the whole platform off at 20:34 UTC. It revoked all 919 cloud service-account keys and every GitHub OAuth app token. It rotated every production secret it manages, and wiped and reinstalled thousands of build machines. The attacker returned on 18 November and replayed the attack. Every attempt was refused.

Bitrise identifies ARTEX by three fingerprints matching the framework's public source code: the norma/0.4 client string, task-numbered probe names, and "authorised pentest" user agents. The report publishes 76 indicators, 28 of them new. Bitrise could not establish which language model drove the agents, or who the operator is.

New Zealand — access gained, no victim named

On 21 November, Dr Emma Bickerstaffe told RNZ that attackers have used ARTEX against New Zealand organisations. She is the National Cyber Security Centre's cyber defence operations director. Some attempts failed. Others "successfully gained access to an organisation and viewed data". No victim is named, and no count or date is given. The activity is not attributed.

Forest & Bird and the New Zealand Defence Force both reported breaches on 20 November. RNZ states explicitly that the tools used in those two incidents are not yet known. They are not linked to ARTEX here.

Canada — ARTEX-server activity, ongoing for months

On 21 November, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security warned that cyber threat activity from servers running ARTEX has been ongoing for several months. It continues across multiple sectors. The Centre has not attributed the activity to a specific actor. Targeting is not an indication of compromise.

Dragos — ARTEX used to confirm keys already held

Dragos's 19 November canary study found that one attacker who had compromised two providers added ARTEX on 1 November. In this case ARTEX "did very little": a handful of calls confirming keys the attacker already held were still live. The providers are not named. None knew of its breach until Dragos told it.

Tool development — BaaRai/ARTEX v0.7.0 and a PHP2Shell exploit skill

The maintained ARTEX successor, BaaRai/ARTEX, shipped v0.7.0 on 21 November. It adds traffic capture, stealth pacing, and a per-host noise governor.

Netskope Threat Labs reported on 18 November that BaaRai/ARTEX ships a skill turning a PHP file-inclusion finding into verified remote code execution. The skill targets CVE-2026-109618 (9.8, PHP memory corruption leading to RCE) and CVE-2026-109619 (8.1, stale global state leading to RCE). Both have been in CISA KEV since 26 October. Its SKILL.md directs the agent to use it "when a task holds an LFI/file-inclusion finding on a PHP target". It states that it "turns a read-only LFI into verified RCE". The skill was committed on 21 October, the same day PHP released the fixes.

Netskope observed ARTEX downloads carrying the PHP2Shell skill alongside intense PHP2Shell exploitation. That indicates threat actors are also using the autonomous capabilities of ARTEX to exploit these vulnerabilities. The skill's safeguards require authorisation and mark two of its three exploit chains lab-only. They are written instructions to the model, and Netskope notes they are "not an effective guardrail".

Patch PHP to 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27, or 8.5.12. The 19 November releases (8.2.36, 8.3.37, 8.4.28, 8.5.13) also close CVE-2026-115560 and six further critical RCE flaws. PHP 8.1 and older get no fix.

European Commission campaign: see §2.3. The ARTEX link there is unconfirmed by the Commission.

Cloudforce One watch note: on 18 November, Cloudforce One reported on X the first instance of ARTEX and Java4Shell used from the same IP. A source IP scanned with a tool-revealing User-Agent, then about 17 hours later sent a Java4Shell exploit attempt claiming to be Chrome. No IP was named. The write-up itself says the observations do not establish three things. They do not show ARTEX sent the Chrome-disguised request, that either attempt succeeded, or that the two came from a common operator. A shared IP is not proof of a common operator. Status: reported, unconfirmed.

Hunt for the ARTEX user agents (artex-agent, artex-judge-r121, ARTEX-Nuclei-Fingerprint/1.0, ARTEX-CredHunter/2.0) and the X-ARTX-KEY request header.

Confidence: High — Barracuda and Bitrise rest on vendor primaries. The Barracuda ARTEX link is a pattern match, not an attribution. The New Zealand and Canada items are reported, not confirmed.

Priority: Act.

So what for BFSI: Retire vendor credentials when the work ends, and enforce second-factor authentication on every service, bot, and dormant account.

Still unknown: Which language model drove the agents; who the operators are; whether the New Zealand access led to data theft.

§6.7 — ruby2shell: an AI agent does lab-only exploit development

Takeaway: Within days of disclosure, an autonomous AI agent took a critical Ruby flaw from crash reproduction to working remote command execution. That was in the lab only, under conditions a stock application does not provide.

Ruby disclosed CVE-2026-132825 on 19 November. On 20 November, a repository (genaforvena) showed an autonomous AI agent taking the flaw from crash reproduction to tcache poisoning in a single session. The Veria Labs proof of concept is expected about 25 November, or later if major vendors have not shipped a fix. It is expected, not published.
