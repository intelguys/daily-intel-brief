Supply chain & third-party risk

Microsoft’s official Python agent-framework package hijacked on PyPI (NEW, reported)

Microsoft’s official agent-framework Python package (nearly 200,000 downloads a month, used to build and deploy AI agents) was hijacked: versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2 install info-stealing malware. The malicious package pulls in the genuine 1.26.0 to look legitimate while exfiltrating environment variables and credential files for Azure, Git, Docker, Hugging Face and other services to a hard-coded webhook.site address — and actively requests fresh identity tokens from GitHub Actions, Azure DevOps and Azure. It runs on install without being imported and is not persistent. The attacker uploaded 1.26.2 through Microsoft’s official PyPI publisher account after 1.26.1 was reverted, suggesting the credentials were not yet locked down. Microsoft told The Register it has “made some repositories private while we investigate malicious content” and is reviewing; the agent-framework GitHub repository was offline at the time of writing. Treat as reported (researcher and press reporting; Microsoft investigating, not a Microsoft Security blog post). Any firm that installed 1.26.1 or 1.26.2 should remove the package, rotate Azure, Git, Docker and Hugging Face credentials, and review GitHub Actions and Azure DevOps token issuance.

Wellington IT — credit-union supplier breach (UPDATE)

An authorisation flaw in Wellington IT’s CU Online platform was exploited by an automated script making about five million requests over 2–4 October 2026, using valid credentials, exposing saved payee records from about 40 Irish credit unions. AI-assisted hackers diverted approved loans at 12 Indian NBFCs; one lost ₹41.27 crore in 90 minutes (NEW, reported)

Hackers diverted approved-but-undisbursed loans at 12 non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) in Delhi-NCR over three months, taking more than ₹65 crore, as part of more than 150 FIRs filed by NBFCs across India. The method: find approved loans not yet disbursed and divert them to mule accounts. Delhi Police DCP Gaurav Tyagi said “We have detected traces of AI being used in these frauds,” attributing them largely to weaknesses in NBFCs’ public-facing systems. Separately, one South Delhi NBFC allegedly lost ₹41.27 crore in about 96 unauthorised transactions within 90 minutes on Monday 16 November, with IPs traced to Hong Kong. The Indian Express named Hero FinCorp (reportedly ₹12 crore, 17 August) and Unifinz (₹12.49 crore, 550 transactions, complaint 21 October). CERT-In and its financial arm CSIRT-Fin issued a “Critical” advisory to NBFCs, digital lenders, fintechs, payment providers and wallet operators on 9 October; the RBI issued confidential advisories to banks, including one on API hijacking on 16 November. The AI involvement is described by police as “traces of AI” and “possibly using AI”, not independently confirmed — label it reported. The operational point for lenders is the disbursement flow itself: an approved loan that has not yet been paid out is a standing instruction to move money, and an attacker who can rewrite its destination does not need to break authentication.

Operation Niagara — Canada–US operation against crypto “approval phishing” (NEW, confirmed)

The Ontario Securities Commission is coordinating Operation Niagara, a cross-border initiative with Canadian and US law enforcement, regulators and private firms (RCMP, OPP, US Secret Service, Toronto Police and others; Chainalysis, TRM Labs, Elliptic and others), launched on 16 November and reported on 18 November, to disrupt online crypto fraud focused on approval / permit phishing — where a victim signs a blockchain transaction that lets a fraudster drain their wallet without needing a password or seed phrase. Two hubs (Toronto, hosted by OSC / OPP; Washington DC, hosted by the US Secret Service) aim to identify victims in near real time before stolen access is used. No takedowns or figures have been reported yet. Approval phishing is a growing account-takeover vector for retail investors and a warning signal for banks’ crypto-adjacent customers.

Spanish family firm loses €600,000+ in bank-impersonation scam, 31 transfers (NEW, reported)

A Valencian events company lost more than €600,000 after criminals impersonated its bank, warning of a suspicious payment and steering the owners through a two-day scam ending in 31 transfers to about 20 mule accounts. The pattern to warn business customers about: a “bank” that asks you to approve a code to stop a payment is authorising the payment.

Scattered Spider member sentenced to six years for SMS-phishing / SIM-swap scheme, $11.6m crypto stolen (NEW, confirmed)
