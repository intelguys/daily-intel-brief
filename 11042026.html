Executive summary

1

The one new KEV entry is WordPress, due Friday 6 November — and the score itself is disputed. CISA added CVE-2026-119977 on 3 November, taking the catalog from 1,766 to 1,767 entries, with forensic triage requested. Wordfence rates it 8.1; the WordPress project rates the same issue Low. Exploitation attempts have been seen since 28 October, but no successful compromise via this race has been publicly confirmed in sources read. Patch to 7.1.4 or the branch backport, and check logs.

2

Two supply-chain and edge stories moved from warning to action on 3 November. OPSSHIELD disclosed that cPGuard versions 5.88.12–5.88.19 shipped a backdoor and harvested password hashes, SSH files and root database configuration after its update channel was compromised on 2 November; updating to 5.88.25 or later removes the backdoor but does not un-steal credentials. Separately, Barracuda disclosed two flaws (CVE-2026-116606, 9.3, and CVE-2026-111560, 8.6) that chain to root on its Email Security Gateway — with no exploitation reported yet.

3

The exchange recovery story split in two directions. Binance said on 3 November it had replaced 98% of affected assets, with a wider proof-of-reserves snapshot due 4 November — but that proof still covers only 10 assets at a 31 October snapshot, so it is evidence about balances at a cut-off, not an audit and not a post-mortem. Mercado Bitcoin revised its own figure twice in a day (about $38m, then about $36.3m affected with about $12.9m blocked) and still gives no reopening date; blocked funds are not recovered funds.

Supply chain & third-party risk

cPGuard / OPSSHIELD — NEW, 3 Nov

Stolen credentials let an attacker into cPGuard's update system on 2 Nov and push versions 5.88.12–5.88.19 carrying a backdoor that harvested /etc/shadow, /etc/passwd, root database configuration, shell history and SSH files (staged as loot.tgz in /var/tmp/.cpg91511). OPSSHIELD provisionally links confirmed retrieval to 32 servers; a secondary expanded figure (~2,950 servers, 7,900 billing accounts) could not be verified against the advisory and is not adopted here. Fix to 5.88.25+ and rotate all credentials that could have been on an affected server.

Google / Videostrong XCPE — NEW

A certified maker's firmware backdoor, not an update compromise. Banning the maker and seizing control domains removes the operator's channel but leaves backdoor code in boxes already sold — treat affected hardware as untrusted, not cleaned.

Abrigo / Sageworks — Update, unchanged

No new Abrigo statement in-window: Analyst and API are back, borrower-facing Online Portal Now remains unavailable, and the breach-or-not question is still unanswered. The outstanding ask for an attestation on scope and data types stands.

Barracuda as perimeter vendor — NEW

A security appliance that can be reconfigured or rooted from its admin interface is a trusted-component failure. Patch, then remove the admin interface from internet reach.

Emerging fraud & attack vectors

MoneySmart breach confirmed 3 Nov

MoneySmart (Singapore / Hong Kong) confirmed an unauthorised third party accessed some internal systems, discovered 29 Oct, and copied personal data of some customers. Expect follow-on targeted phishing and calls quoting real product details. MoneySmart says it never asks for passwords or OTPs by phone, email or text.

HKMA warning, 3 Nov

Fraudulent websites and internet-banking login screens reported for the Bank of East Asia, Shanghai Commercial Bank, Chong Hing Bank and HSBC, among others. Banks will not send hyperlinked SMS/emails to carry out transactions or ask for passwords/OTPs.

Belize bankers' warning, 3 Nov

Ongoing phone/messaging scams impersonating bank representatives to obtain credentials and password-reset codes. Legitimate institutions never solicit confidential information that way.

Exchange restoration phishing — ongoing

Binance and Mercado Bitcoin restoration stories are live phishing bait. Verify via official status pages only. Mercado Bitcoin repeats: it never asks for a password, code or recovery phrase, and offers no refunds via links, WhatsApp or phone calls.

AI in the current threat landscape

AI as a target, AI as a weapon or accelerator, and AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI.

AI as a target — LLMjacking, live today

Stolen service-account keys, GitHub tokens or LLM API keys, or exposed endpoints (including Ollama), are used to run inference at the victim's expense and resell it via "LLM mixer" services such as Jet Night. One actor made 500,000+ calls in under eight hours; charges above $100,000 in a day, up to about $1m before revocation. LiteLLM CVE-2026-40217 (8.8) turned ordinary virtual keys into PROXY_ADMIN credentials. No bank victim is named, and the figures are reported scales, not audited bank losses.

AI as a weapon — agentic intrusions, live today

Since late August 2026, actors have run ARTEX, PentAGI and CAIRN inside Mandiant engagements, turning high-level goals into commands via model APIs. GTIG places activity at stage 5 and predicts stage 6 within 1–3 months and stage 7 within 3–6 months. That forecast is an assessment, not an observation — no engagement counts or success rates are given.

AI as a risk and control problem for BFSI

Govern: (1) API and service-account key inventory and rotation; (2) endpoint exposure — Ollama / LiteLLM must not be internet-reachable without authentication; (3) spend and invocation monitoring, with revoke-and-rotate, not just spend caps; (4) agent-tool detection — rapid, tool-driven sequences from a single session; (5) open-source tooling — blocking one framework name does not block the technique.

No new AI-scaled phishing item in this window

No newly reported AI-generated phishing, vishing or deepfake campaign against a BFSI victim was found for 3–4 November. That is absence of reporting, not evidence it has stopped. The MoneySmart, HKMA and Belize warnings in this edition are conventional impersonation and are not re-labelled as AI campaigns without evidence.

  • WordPress: requests for staged installer paths under wp-content/upgrade/ around a core upgrade window.
  • Barracuda: unexpected ESG configuration imports; shell metacharacters in time-zone settings; any internet-reachable admin interface.
  • Linux TCP: IPv4 TCP SYN packets with Record Route / Timestamp IP options, especially against MPTCP listeners (including Go 1.24+ services).
  • LLMjacking: spend spikes above $100,000/day, 500,000+ calls in hours from one key, new-principal model invocations, LiteLLM PROXY_ADMIN keys via /key/regenerate.
  • US bank phishing: [keyword]-[bank].com domains (secure, disable, protect, cancel) behind Cloudflare; repeat-visit "dead" pages may be cloaking.
