Executive summary

1

Two confirmed Japanese financial breaches that the 14 November edition missed lead today. AXA Direct Japan says unauthorised access to its MyAXA customer portal may have affected about 597,000 accounts — names, addresses, dates of birth, emails and phone numbers — and its roadside-assistance provider was breached separately. Monex Securities says a third-party account-opening system may have exposed up to 1.13 million email addresses and 1,592 identity-verification document images, including driving licences, My Number card fronts and selfies. Both figures are “may have leaked” maxima, not confirmed theft counts — but both are prime phishing and identity-fraud material, and both companies say no account numbers, passwords or card data were in the affected systems.

2

Korea’s financial-sector incident wave keeps producing disclosures, and the counting is still contested. KOMSCO confirmed a breach of its chak local-currency app affecting 3,083 users, including account numbers and encrypted card numbers. KBIZ said its Yellow Umbrella mutual-aid site may have exposed member data, with the scale still unconfirmed — do not read the 1.92 million membership figure as a victim count. The FSI special audit starts Monday 16 November. A 14 November report of a “second emergency meeting” and a 16-firm count could not be reconciled with the regulator’s 14 October press release carrying the same figures, so it is not treated as a new development.

3

Binance’s recovery moved forward, while the patching backlog did not. The 198-token zero-knowledge proof is now live — verified by a live read at 00:08 SGT, with the Merkle root published and BTC at 100.05% — and withdrawals reopened on 87 networks, with full reopening aimed by about 22 November. No new CISA KEV additions were made on 14 November; a claim that two were added was refuted, since both were added on 24 September. N-able’s follow-up hotfix is still in validation with no fix as of 14 November, and a public proof of concept now exists for the Fortinet FortiManager 9.8 flaw, though exploitation is not confirmed.

Unknown actor — AXA Direct Japan

Insurer; MyAXA customer portal and outsourced roadside-assistance provider, Japan

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published. No intrusion detail published. Suspicious access to the MyAXA portal was detected at about 08:30 on 9 Nov and blocked at 10:53 the same day. The roadside-assistance provider Prestige Core Solution detected unauthorised access to its own systems on 29 Oct and told AXA on 2 Nov that some customer-related information had been taken outside its system.
Impact
Confirmed (company notices, 12 and 13 Nov; reported 14 Nov — catch-up, missed by the 14 November edition). About 597,000 MyAXA accounts may have been affected; the information that may have leaked is names, addresses, dates of birth, email addresses and telephone numbers. Credit card information was not included. Part of the leak has been confirmed; the cause and full scope remain under investigation. No misuse or secondary damage had been confirmed as of the 13 Nov notice, and affected customers are to be contacted individually. MyAXA was suspended and resumed on 12 Nov after security checks. The provider-side scope is still being examined and cannot simply be added to 597,000. Prestige International separately disclosed up to about 5.5 million records (duplicates included) across its own systems on 6 Nov. Critique: 597,000 is the number of accounts that may have been affected, not a confirmed count of exposed individuals — but the data types are exactly what impersonation fraud needs, and the two-incident structure (portal plus provider) shows the same third-party exposure pattern as Monex.

Unknown actor — KOMSCO “chak” local-currency app

State-run mint and security printer; mobile local-currency payment platform, South Korea

TTPs
No MITRE mapping published. Attacker and entry route not disclosed; investigation ongoing. Abnormal access was first detected at about 09:30 on 13 Nov.
Impact
Confirmed (company statement, 14 Nov; Korean press — translated). Unauthorised access to the chak platform exposed personal data of 3,083 users (as of 06:00 on 14 Nov): names, dates of birth, mobile phone numbers, account numbers, linkage information (CI), email addresses and encrypted card numbers. Arara — STATUS (Osaka City disclosure, 13 Nov, reported 14 Nov)

Osaka City confirmed 1,078,713 email addresses leaked from its online administrative system after attackers hit Arara, the email-delivery service used by its contractor TKC — email addresses only, no contents or My Number data. The platform’s 6 Nov third report put the total at 81,329,467 email addresses (deduplicated) across up to about 380 client companies, exfiltrated 28–31 Oct; another source cites about 77 million — the two totals conflict, do not merge them. The same platform was also abused 28–30 Oct to send up to about 7.13 million phishing emails impersonating a credit-card company. Osaka Prefecture separately disclosed ID-document images for 12 food-support applicants.

Critique: the count matters less than the channel — a ready-made phishing list of shareholder and citizen emails plus a proven bank-impersonation sending channel. Do not add the per-company figures together — they are subsets of the same incident.

Monex and AXA — the breach ran through a supplier (NEW)

Both of today’s Japanese breaches ran through a supplier. Monex’s breach was in a third-party account-opening system (the contractor is unnamed and the intrusion method undisclosed), and AXA’s roadside-assistance provider Prestige Core Solution was breached separately (detected 29 Oct; Prestige International separately disclosed up to about 5.5 million records, duplicates included, across its own systems on 6 Nov).

Critique: neither company has named its supplier’s failing, and in both cases the breached system sat outside the firm’s own perimeter — the pattern peers should be checking is which third-party systems hold customer identity data and whether those suppliers are in scope for the firm’s own incident response.

ScreenConnect and N-able — MSP supply chain (status)

Microsoft 365 outage credential phishing — NEW (confirmed campaign, alert 13 Nov, reported 14 Nov)

Attackers are impersonating Microsoft 365 service-health notifications to exploit the 12–13 Nov outage (Exchange Online incident EX1550543, a network-capacity failure Microsoft says shows no sign of malicious activity). Messages cite the real incident ID, with subject lines “Outage – [six-character code]” and sender display names of an organisation-specific prefix plus “Outage Notification” or “Service Health”. They falsely claim emails held during the outage will be permanently deleted within 24 hours unless the recipient clicks to restore them. Three sender domains are identified: moments-notice.com, planetmaison.com, newbooker.com. Microsoft characterises the campaign as “consistent with credential phishing” but has not confirmed credential theft or malware delivery; no actor attribution has been published. The UK’s NCSC warned on 13 Nov of scam calls and messages exploiting the outage.

Critique: the timing is the point — the campaign arrived while Defender XDR and Safe Links monitoring were themselves degraded by the outage, so the usual safety nets were thinner than usual. Verify via official status pages only.

YES Bank denial — NEW (confirmed denial, reported 14 Nov)

YES Bank has denied a report by The Banker, a Financial Times Group publication, alleging the bank suffered a cyber fraud loss of about $3.4 million (Rs 32 crore) involving government-linked accounts in an incident towards the end of October 2026. In a clarification issued on Friday 13 November, the bank called the claims factually incorrect: its banking systems had not been compromised, no government accounts had been affected, and it had suffered no monetary loss linked to the reported incident. It has asked the publication for a retraction. No independent forensic evidence of a breach has been published, and The Banker has not publicly responded to the retraction request in the reporting seen.

Critique: report this as a denial, not a breach — but note the separate, earlier disclosure: SG Finserve said on 3 Nov that it detected 15 unauthorised debits totalling ₹5.10 crore from two accounts at YES Bank’s South Extension II branch. That is a separate disclosure, not evidence for The Banker’s claim, and the two should not be merged.

Tamil Nadu government fund theft — NEW (arrests confirmed, reported 14 Nov)

Chennai cybercrime police arrested seven suspected money mules after cybercriminals used stolen credentials of authorised Block Development Officers to access the Tamil Nadu rural development department’s Single Nodal Account (HDFC Bank, CIT Nagar branch) between 12 and 30 October and move ₹22.3 crore in 46 transactions to 24–25 accounts. The money has not been recovered and the main culprits are still at large; the case was registered on 3 Nov.

Critique: the theft is an October event — the arrests are the in-window development. The method (stolen officer credentials used against a government nodal account) is a credential-theft and account-takeover pattern, not a bank-system breach, and the unrecovered funds show the laundering chain worked.
